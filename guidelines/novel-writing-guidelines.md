# Standing novel-writing guidelines

Adopted 19 October 2026 under the owner's **House Rivalry, Banter and Victory Traditions** instruction. Apply to future novel writing and authorized revisions. The accompanying editorial pass covers Chapters 02–08; the owner's subsequent instruction, **“Skip chapter 01”**, excludes Chapter 01 from that pass.

Read the relevant complete scenes, [character dossiers](../reference/INDEX.md), and [Winter Reserve lore](../reference/lore/winter-reserve-and-bottle-wagers.md) before writing. The novel establishes events; character sheets establish identities and relationships. Preserve ranks, command boundaries, character knowledge, equipment states, mission outcomes and major continuity. This guideline adds a standing characterization direction, not permission to revise unrelated canon or comic production.

## Competition as House culture

For the brothers, Dennis and veteran House commanders, a battle tests their craft as well as their courage. Let that attitude inform preparations, tactical choices within orders, private exchanges and later arguments. An engagement can express rivalry through a remembered maneuver, an unspoken challenge or a grudging compliment. It does not require a fresh wager in every scene.

Use concrete stakes and measures when a wager belongs: first authorized breach, enemy weapons or drives disabled, intact command capture, a clean extraction, sound engineering, or an effective maneuver. Rescue and prisoner recovery can be sources of pride, but people never belong to a competitor's category. Triage and operational need determine who receives help. No one delays assistance, withholds support, changes an assigned route or creates extra danger to improve a score.

Record enough of the terms for a later dispute to make sense: participants, the actual bottle or other stake, the measure, any witness and whether the result remains unresolved. Carry selected debts forward. A loss can be paid while the loser continues arguing about the interpretation. Avoid introducing so many named bottles and contests that the story becomes an account book.

The Charter and military orders govern every contest. A private wager confers no authority. Captains retain their ships; surrender, medical care, evidence protection and civilian safety remain obligations. A bold action needs an operational reason independent of its prize. Boasting stops for a contact report or order. Show the team covering, supporting and rescuing one another even when that means another competitor wins.

Keep tactical channels usable. Brothers may mock one another over a secure family or squad circuit when conditions permit; urgent traffic immediately takes priority. Under civilian cover, speech and communications must preserve the scene's existing knowledge and aliases. Do not add a public identity leak for a joke.

## Recognizable voices

| Character | Voice and competitive habits |
|---|---|
| Joel | Dry, precise, quietly confident. Chooses the terms carefully; an intelligent insult often exposes a flaw in someone else's claim. Enjoys winning and can acknowledge losing. His authority never excuses an unpaid debt. |
| Anton | Loud, provocative, delighted by a worthy opponent. Boasts, demands proper breach credit and contests interpretations long after the fighting. His desire to win does not weaken his discipline once an order is clear. |
| Christer | Deadpan and observant. Notices the overlooked condition, witnesses the wager, and places the perfectly timed remark. His restraint gives the joke force; he need not sound like a quieter Anton. |
| Daniel | Understated sarcasm and technical confidence. Treats intact systems, efficient work and correct measurements as achievements. Can win by understanding the machinery while everyone else admires the door. |
| Dennis Rask | Blunt hunter challenges and dark humor grounded in dangerous experience. His loyalty to Joel is that of a veteran friend and comrade, not a newly invented brother. Orders and custody protections constrain his destructive preferences. Do not give him the family's inherited alcohol tolerance. |
| Lars | Veteran authority, few words and devastating timing. Can humble his sons by choosing a better objective or reminding them what an experienced commander has already done. He honors his own wagers as firmly as theirs. |
| Senior admirals, Banner Captains, Grey Nomads and elders | Competitors with individual experience and histories. Some keep pools or inherited betting books; some remember a debt from decades ago; some beat the younger officers through preparation. Give each exchange a specific speaker and professional point of view. |

Banter should sound personal: remembered mistakes, familiar habits, a compliment disguised as an accusation. Vary its form and intensity. Allow a warm gesture or a shared silence to do work that another punchline would spoil. Junior personnel and allied commanders retain competence and dignity; they are not an audience assembled to admire the family.

## Bottles, hospitality and victory

Hjertstedt Winter Reserve is rare, extremely expensive and dangerously potent. A particular bottle can connect generations through its maker, batch, keeper or memory. The last of a family reserve cannot simply be replaced by buying something equally costly. Do not invent a new canonical recipe, strength or biological mechanism.

The agreed bottle changes hands even when the loser outranks the winner. Name the keeper when ownership matters. Distinguish a Bjorn-made bottle in Joel's cellar from Bjorn's personal stock; distinguish offering a prize from having authority to pledge someone else's reserve. Promises, transfers, receipt and opening are different events. Keep their sequence truthful.

The bloodline's unusual metabolism grants tolerance, not immunity. Injury, exhaustion and individual condition still matter. Ordinary House personnel, Hunters and guests do not inherit that trait through service or hospitality. Preserve the established small cups and one-cup limit: no second serving as a courage test, no pressure to accept the first, and a milder or nonalcoholic choice without insult. Honor medical restrictions and duty readiness. Drinking competitions do not decide combat fitness.

When recovery and relief allow it, let victories have a real table: plentiful food, music, laughter, extravagant stories, arguments over old performances and renewed challenges. Senior officers and ordinary comrades share the culture, with appropriate hospitality. Not every victory can support a feast, and nobody drinks to the neglect of a watch, the wounded or survivors needing food and shelter.

Remember the fallen as people. Leave room for names, empty places and stories that cannot be told by their owners. An unopened bottle may keep a place for the missing or wait for friends still in treatment. Do not force a joke into grief or imply that a favorable score compensates for a lost crew.

## Revision and review

Before changing an existing scene, identify its tactical purpose, emotional temperature, available time and character knowledge. Add or sharpen a beat where it reveals relationship, skill or history. Do not mechanically insert a challenge–bet–punchline sequence into every operation, interrupt critical action with extended chatter, or manufacture a celebration while rescue work is still urgent.

Read the revised scene with its neighbors. Check that the joke's speaker, channel and knowledge fit; the contest does not alter orders or outcomes; the bottle exists, belongs to its keeper and has not been pledged twice; the debt's later status is consistent; and casualties retain their weight. Preserve unresolved arguments rather than inventing a different canonical winner.

Update the combined novel, scene navigation, affected reference supplements and source hashes with each accepted revision. Keep the imported website edition identifiable, including its original source hashes. Follow [the standing GitHub-sync guideline](novel-github-sync-v01.md), commit and verify the authorized remote result. A repository revision does not deploy the website or revise approved comic pages.
