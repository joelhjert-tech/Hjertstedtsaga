# The Ships That Stayed

The old frigate had been scheduled to lose her engines on the same morning the Garooth came. Captain Ingrid Vale stood beneath the open machinery deck while a yard worker explained where the cutting teams would begin. He had drawn neat white lines across the hull, and he had called the ship a salvage lot three times. Ingrid had corrected him twice. On the third occasion she let it pass, because the frigate had not flown in six years and the young man was holding the yard's authorization. Then the warning sounded, and every light along the berth changed from white to red. For a moment nobody moved. A voice on the yard channel was calling for someone to open a pressure door, repeating the compartment number with increasing care, as though saying it correctly might make the lock respond. Another voice told him to wait. The first man began to scream before the channel went dead.

The first report placed six Garooth ships inside the system, with more contacts behind them. They were driving toward the mining world of Bracken and the repair yard above it. The nearest House patrol had acknowledged the distress call, but its commander could not promise to arrive before the raiders. Ingrid listened to the message on the yard's command deck while traffic controllers tried to clear civilian ships from the approaches. Bracken had one working spaceport on the near side of the planet. The other had been hit in the first attack. The port was still transmitting, but the signal carried only an open microphone and the sounds of people trying to get through a locked door. The traffic controller called back three times. At last a woman answered, too breathless to give her name, and said there was smoke on the stairs. Then something heavy struck close to her microphone and the transmission ended.

“How many people are still down there?” she asked.

“The port reports twelve thousand registered departures,” said Yardmaster Orin Pell. He looked at the numbers again. “And no reliable count of the people coming out of the mines. The shuttles can lift them, but they need somewhere to go.” A second channel broke through his report. Someone was calling from the ruined port, asking why the shuttles had gone without them. Pell reached for the transmitter, then stopped when the signal repeated word for word. It was a distress beacon replaying the last message it had captured. He turned the volume down, but he did not switch it off. The largest civilian transports were loading at the yard. If the Garooth reached the approaches first, those transports would be caught between the planet and the raiders. Pell touched the display and brought up a row of idle hulls. “Those are what I can give you.”

Ingrid saw two damaged escorts, a cargo tender, three tugs and her old frigate, the Northbound. One escort had no working main battery. The other had a hole through its forward compartments and no business being under power at all. The tender could carry people if the yard cleared its holds; the tugs could move the transports, though they could not protect them. Northbound had her guns and most of her crew aboard for the decommissioning, but the yard had already disconnected one engine. Ingrid asked for the chief engineer. Pell's expression told her what he thought of the plan before he made the call.

Far below, at the mouth of the eastern mine, Lysa Marr was trying to persuade a truck to start when the mountain shook. The driver, a gray-haired woman named Anik, kept one hand on the wheel while Lysa lay beneath the vehicle and tightened a fuel coupling. Dust sifted down into Lysa's collar. Somewhere beyond the ridge an alarm wailed, followed by the heavier sound of a blast. The mine radio, clipped to Lysa’s belt, crackled with overlapping voices. One caller was giving a location in a level that had been empty for months. Another kept asking whether anyone could hear her. Lysa answered, but the voices continued as if she had said nothing. “That your doing?” Anik called.

“If it was, I'd charge extra.” Lysa rolled clear and struck the underside of the truck with her palm. The engine coughed, complained and settled into a rough idle. Anik leaned out of the cab and shouted for the waiting miners to get aboard. There were twenty-six of them, including two with broken legs and a boy whose father was still underground. Lysa had promised to bring him to the port. His father had gone back with the last rescue team, and no one had heard from them since the first landings. The boy held his father’s spare headset against one ear. It was not switched on. When Lysa offered to check it, he gripped it so tightly that she let him keep it.

The road down ran through a narrow cut in the mountains before turning west toward the spaceport. Lysa knew every bend, every bad patch and every place where winter storms stripped the barriers away. She also knew the old service track that joined it halfway down. She told the House lieutenant at the convoy checkpoint about the track as soon as she found him. He was standing over a map with his helmet tucked under one arm, listening to three people speak at once.

“Can trucks use it?” he asked.

“Not safely. A crawler can, if its driver doesn't mind losing the paint.” She pointed to the steep line on his map. “And anything coming down that track can hit the road behind your first barricade.” The lieutenant introduced himself as Halen and sent two scouts to look. He did not have enough people to guard every approach. He had eighteen House soldiers, a larger number of local security officers and two mining crawlers with armored cabs. His orders were to keep the road open until the last convoy passed, then fall back to the port. The scouts returned with tracks in the shale and the sound of engines behind the ridge. One had also heard a man shouting from the abandoned loading shelf. He had called back, but the shouting stopped when the engines did. Halen sent the scouts to search from cover. They found an empty emergency shelter with its door torn open and a radio still transmitting inside. The man had recorded his location before the raiders arrived; the set was repeating him automatically. Halen took the radio with them in case anyone came looking for it.

At the yard, Northbound's chief engineer arrived with a burn on his cheek and an apologetic expression. “One engine will run,” he told Ingrid. “The other is on the floor. The port maneuvering thrusters are questionable, and if you ask for more than half power from the reactor, I'll become deeply unpleasant.” His name was Toman, and Ingrid had served with him long enough to know he was already being pleasant. He had found thirty-two yard workers willing to help crew the frigate. Most had never been in a battle, but they knew her systems better than anyone still wearing a uniform. One of them asked whether the frightened voices on the yard channel were an exercise. Toman told her they were not. He assigned her to the engineering station beside him and showed her where to find the emergency seals. Her hands shook so badly on the first switch that she had to use both of them.

The yardmaster gave Ingrid command of the ships that could move. The damaged escort with the dead main battery, called Lantern's Wake, could still use its smaller guns and its communications array. Its captain, Soren, proposed taking station between the planet and the yard, where the ship could relay traffic if the port's transmitters failed. Ingrid sent him there with one tug and the first empty transport. Northbound would cover the approach from the raiders. The second escort would stay at the yard as a fixed gun platform; its captain agreed only after Toman showed him the structural report. The cargo tender began taking aboard stretchers, water and every spare pressure suit the yard could find.

On the mountain road, the first Garooth appeared through a curtain of dust, riding a battered carrier down the service track. Their hull was patched with plates of different colors, and a gun mounted over the cab fired before the vehicle had cleared the ridge. The shot tore into the outer barricade. Halen's soldiers answered from the rocks above the road, forcing the carrier to stop short of the junction. Its rear ramp dropped, and enormous red-skinned figures spilled out in salvaged armor, carrying weapons built for hands much larger than Lysa's. They used the carrier for cover, firing uphill while others searched for a way around it.

Anik's truck was third in the convoy. The lead vehicle had stopped at the barricade, and the whole line was backing up around the bend. Lysa ran to the first cab and found a frightened driver trying to reverse a loaded hauler onto a narrow shoulder. She climbed onto his step, showed him where to place the wheels and stayed there until he had turned. Halen sent two soldiers to direct the remaining vehicles through the gap. Above them, one of the scouts stopped answering. His partner reached him and reported that he was alive but could not walk. The wounded man was screaming, not words at first, just a raw sound that carried over the open channel. His partner kept telling him where her hands were and what she was doing, even when he could no longer seem to hear her. Halen gave the covering team a target and sent another soldier up after them.

The Garooth had found a path above the service track. Lysa saw movement among the rocks and recognized the ground behind it: a steep slope of loose shale beneath an abandoned loading shelf. She called Halen and pointed it out. “There's a crawler up there,” she said. “We left it when the cable broke. If I can release the brake, it'll take the shelf with it.” Halen looked at the slope, then at the stalled convoy. He would not send her alone. A local security officer named Deren took the second crawler, and they climbed the maintenance path together while House soldiers covered the turn below.

Ingrid took Northbound away from the yard under one engine, moving slowly enough that Toman could watch the heat rise. Ahead of her, a Garooth pursuit corvette broke from the larger formation and accelerated toward the transports. Ingrid ordered a warning to the civilian captains, then brought her frigate across its path. The first exchange shook dust from Northbound's overhead panels. A voice came over the shared emergency frequency, asking a tug to retrieve an escape pod. The tug pilot answered that he could see it. Then he went silent, and someone else on the frequency began calling his name. Ingrid had no view of the tug from her station; the tactical display showed its signal disappear beside the approaching corvette. Her forward gun struck the corvette's flank, and the raider answered with missiles. Two were intercepted. A third hit the frigate's port side and tore open an empty storage compartment. The damage officer reported the breach and sealed the neighboring spaces before Ingrid could ask.

The corvette did not turn away. It rolled beneath Northbound's next shot and went for the transport behind her. Captain Soren saw the move from Lantern's Wake. His escort lacked the power to chase the raider, so he put his ship between it and the transport and opened fire with the smaller guns. The first burst missed. The second struck the corvette's engine housing, and its course shifted just enough for Northbound's gun crew to find it again. Their next shot disabled the raider's drive. The corvette drifted, still dangerous but no longer closing. Ingrid sent its position to the yard and ordered both ships back toward the evacuation lane. There were larger Garooth hulls still coming.

Lysa reached the abandoned crawler with Deren just behind her. Below, the road was alive with vehicles. The first had made it through the cut; Anik's truck was approaching the bend. Lysa climbed into the crawler and found the brake housing twisted under a fallen beam. Deren braced the beam with the other machine while she crawled beneath it with a wrench. A burst of fire struck the rocks nearby, scattering sharp fragments across the cab. Beneath the shooting Lysa heard someone call for help from farther up the track. The voice rose into a scream. She started toward it, but Deren caught her arm and pointed to the radio in Halen’s pack below: its transmit light was flashing with the same cry. The set was replaying another fragment from the shelter. That did not tell her whether the person who had recorded it was alive. Deren swore over the radio and shifted his crawler to shield her. “Take your time,” he said. “I'm enjoying myself.”

The brake released with a crack. Lysa pulled herself clear, but the crawler did not move. The broken cable had wrapped around its rear track. She seized a cutting torch from Deren's tool rack and burned through the strands while he kept the beam lifted. One strand snapped against her glove, hot enough to make her cry out. She cut the last one, and the crawler lurched forward. Deren swung his machine back as the abandoned vehicle rolled over the edge. It struck the loading shelf, vanished into dust and brought a wide section of shale down across the service track. The Garooth gun fell silent. For several seconds Lysa heard nothing but loose stones sliding down the slope. Then someone below the rockfall began pounding, slowly and irregularly, against metal. She could not see who was trapped or how to reach them. Halen ordered the last trucks through and brought his wounded scout down from the ridge. The pounding continued until the convoy rounded the bend.

The road did not stay safe. A second Garooth carrier reached the lower junction before the rear guard could withdraw. Deren turned his crawler across the bend and raised its armored blade, giving the soldiers room to bring the wounded onto Anik's truck. He refused Lysa's shouted order to follow until the last soldier had climbed aboard. The carrier's gun hit his cab. Lysa saw the crawler shudder, then roll sideways into the barrier. Halen pulled her down behind the truck as return fire struck the carrier. When they reached Deren, the cab was crushed. His radio was still open, carrying a woman’s voice from somewhere on the road. She was calling for a medic and giving directions that Lysa could not make sense of through the gunfire. Halen checked Deren for a pulse himself, then covered his face. He answered the woman, passed her location to the nearest security team and ordered the convoy to move. Lysa kept looking back until the crawler disappeared behind the bend.

At the port, the shuttles were lifting as fast as their crews could load them. The boy from the mine waited beside Anik's truck while Lysa searched the arrivals list for his father. His name was not there. She gave the boy to a medic who promised to stay with him, and gave the missing man's details to the port staff. There were still trucks on the road and less than an hour before the Garooth reached the airfield. Calls for help crowded the port frequency: a trapped driver, a missing child, a crew whose engine had stopped on a bridge. The dispatchers wrote down each location and sent help when they could. When they could not, they stayed on the channel and told the callers what they knew. Lysa heard one dispatcher ask a man to describe the room around him so she could keep him talking while a rescue team tried to reach his building. Halen placed his remaining people around the shuttle pads. He did not have the numbers to defend the entire port, so he concentrated on the eastern approaches and left the other buildings empty.

Above them, Lantern's Wake received a broken transmission from the port as its ground array failed. Soren's communications officer patched the signal through the escort and up to Northbound. Ingrid heard Halen report the last convoy's position, followed by the shuttle controller asking for a clear ascent. The empty transport was ready, but a Garooth boarding frigate had pushed past Northbound and was turning toward it. Soren opened fire to draw its attention. The raider answered with a heavy salvo, striking Lantern's Wake amidships. Her signal broke into static, returned for a moment and broke again. Beneath the bridge traffic came voices from an emergency channel that had not fully closed: someone shouting for a hatch to be cut open, another person trying to count the people still breathing. A final impact drowned them out. The channel stayed open, carrying only a thin, uneven hiss.

“Northbound, this is Wake,” Soren said at last. His voice was thin behind the interference. “We've lost maneuvering. We can still relay. Get the shuttles through.” Ingrid could see atmosphere venting from his ship and escape craft pulling away from its damaged side. The boarding frigate continued toward him, its assault clamps extended. Ingrid brought Northbound around, but the turn was slow on one engine. Toman reported that the reactor could give her more power for a short time. He also told her what it might cost. Ingrid accepted both parts of the report and gave the order.

Northbound surged forward. Her guns struck the Garooth frigate as it closed on Soren's ship, breaking one clamp and driving the raider off course. The enemy returned fire, and the old frigate's deck jumped beneath Ingrid's feet. A warning sounded from engineering. Toman held the power long enough for the gun crew to fire again, then brought it down before the reactor failed. The second shot disabled the raider's weapons on the side facing the shuttles. It was not destroyed. Its captain turned the surviving guns toward Northbound, but by then the last loaded shuttle had cleared the atmosphere and was running toward the transport under escort from the yard's remaining tug.

On the ground, Halen withdrew his people to the final shuttle while Lysa helped the medic lift a wounded miner aboard. Garooth fire struck the far side of the pad. The pilot waited until Halen counted his soldiers, then lifted as the last two pulled the ramp shut. Lysa looked back through a small window. The port was burning in three places, and the road up the mountain had disappeared into smoke. She could not see Deren's crawler. The boy sat strapped beside her with Anik's arm around his shoulders. He asked if his father was on another shuttle. Lysa told him the truth: she did not know, but his name was on the search list.

Lantern's Wake kept the relay open until the last shuttle reached the transport. Then her signal failed. Ingrid sent a tug toward her, but Soren reported that fires had reached the remaining magazines and ordered the tug back. He gave his position, the number of escape craft launched and the names of the compartments still unaccounted for. The tug waited outside the danger area and recovered survivors until the escort broke apart. Its pilot could hear a signal from inside the wreck, a woman asking whether anyone was coming. He marked its source and asked Soren for permission to approach. Soren said the fires were too close to the magazines. The pilot argued until the voice stopped. He kept the frequency open while he searched the debris for escape craft. It found Soren's first officer among them. It did not find Soren. Northbound covered the retreat to the yard, where the surviving ships joined the transports and moved away from Bracken before the main Garooth force arrived.

Two days later, after the convoy reached safety, Lysa found Ingrid in the cargo tender's makeshift records room. The captain was helping a clerk compare names from the port, the shuttles and the recovered escape craft. A list of the dead lay on one table; a longer list of the missing lay beside it. Lysa gave the clerk Deren's full name and the name of the scout whose life he had helped save. She asked about the boy's father. No one had found him yet. The boy had been given a berth with Anik, but he slept with his father’s silent headset against his ear. Lysa had stopped herself twice from asking him to take it off. The clerk added the location of his last reported descent to the search record and promised to pass it to the crews preparing to return.

That evening the survivors gathered in Northbound's mess. There was food from the tender's stores, tea for anyone who wanted it, and scarcely enough chairs. Toman read the names of the frigate's dead. Soren's first officer read those of Lantern's Wake. A miner spoke about Deren, who had once driven a crawler through a loading-bay wall and spent a month insisting the wall had been in the wrong place. People laughed, including Lysa, though she had to wipe her eyes afterward. When the names were finished, Ingrid left the list open on the table. There were still people to find, ships to repair and a world to return to. When someone dropped a tray in the galley, several people flinched before anyone could laugh. Lysa heard a faint burst of static and reached for her radio; the set was switched off. Across the room, the tug pilot stood near the door, watching the corridor as though he expected someone to come running through it. Ingrid asked if he wanted to sit with them. He hesitated, then brought his cup to the table. For one evening, the crews had a place to sit together and remember why they had stayed.
