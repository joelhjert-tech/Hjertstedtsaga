# THE KHARAD TRIAL

## The Things Beneath the Ash

A Hjertstedt Saga Side Story

Talyrn Continuance — Vaerith, the Odrava

### I. The Scoreboard

The first man to die was already dead when he called for help.

Nobody knew that yet.

Nara Veyl stood on a ridge overlooking the abandoned industrial district, watching black smoke rise from the atmospheric-processing towers.

The smoke moved against the wind.

That bothered her.

"Thirty-seven points," Jarek announced.

Nara turned.

"What?"

"Thirty-seven points for finding the water reservoir yesterday. Minus five for Bren eating that fungus."

"It wasn't fungus," Bren protested.

"You were sick for six hours."

"Could have been something else."

"You were eating it when you collapsed."

"Coincidence."

Lysa smiled despite herself.

Taren, walking behind them, said nothing.

The five candidates had been inside the Kharad Southern Training Exclusion Zone for three days.

They had four days remaining.

The exercise was supposed to measure survival, cooperation, navigation, and emergency decision-making under pressure.

The instructors had removed their weapons, restricted their supplies, and given them a destination almost two hundred kilometers away.

Nobody had explained why that destination mattered.

That was normal for the Kharad.

The institution did not train ordinary soldiers.

Its graduates became Thyrak.

Intervention specialists.

Men and women expected to enter places where conventional forces had already failed.

Collapsed stations.

Burning warships.

Dead habitats.

Reactor chambers.

Battlefields where rescue operations had become more dangerous than the fighting itself.

Their instructors had repeated one lesson until every candidate knew it.

Strength creates obligation.

Bren had developed his own interpretation.

"If we're supposed to suffer together, we might as well keep score."

The game had started on the first day.

Points for discovering water.

Points for repairing equipment.

Points for helping a teammate.

Penalties for poor decisions.

The scoreboard was scratched into a piece of industrial slate that Jarek carried in his pack.

Nobody took it seriously.

Not officially.

But when Taren had started shaking from exhaustion the previous evening, Nara had ordered a rest.

Bren had protested.

Jarek had awarded Nara ten points.

Bren stopped arguing.

Now, on the third morning, they were healthier than most candidate groups.

Their respirators were maintained.

Their water was clean.

Their shelters had survived the freezing nights.

And they were still laughing.

The laughter stopped when the ground shook.

A low vibration rolled beneath their boots.

Far across the valley, something exploded inside Processing Complex Nine.

A white flash.

Then a black column of debris rose into the atmosphere.

Several seconds later, the sound reached them.

It was not the sharp crack of a conventional explosion.

It sounded like something enormous breaking beneath the earth.

Nara activated her wrist display.

ENVIRONMENTAL ALERT

INDUSTRIAL CONTAINMENT FAILURE

EXERCISE SUSPENDED

PROCEED TO SHELTER

A second warning appeared.

ATMOSPHERIC CONTAMINATION — SEVERE

"Well," Bren said.

Nobody answered.

The horizon was changing.

A wall of darkness was spreading outward from the distant industrial towers.

It swallowed the lower structures first.

Then the hills.

The cloud carried a faint orange glow within it.

Nara checked the projected storm path.

It was moving directly toward them.

"Royal Hotel," Taren said.

Everyone looked at him.

"The maintenance chamber we found yesterday. Three-point-eight kilometers west."

Jarek nodded.

"Pressure door. Concrete roof. Old ventilation system."

"Does it work?" Lysa asked.

"One way to find out."

Nara looked back toward the storm.

The wind had begun carrying small black particles across the ground.

"Move."

They ran.

Aboard a command aircraft holding forty kilometers above Vaerith, Commandant Reth Varun watched the first environmental sensors disappear.

One after another.

Twenty-seven monitoring stations went dark.

Then forty-three.

Then the entire eastern section of the training zone vanished from the orbital display.

"Containment failure at Complex Nine," Instructor Maelis reported.

"Cause?"

"Unknown."

"Storm composition?"

"Industrial ash. Corrosive compounds. Biological contamination indicators."

Reth turned.

"Biological?"

Maelis checked the analysis again.

"Probably damaged waste-processing cultures."

"Probably?"

"Identification is incomplete."

A warning appeared across the main tactical display.

GROUND NETWORK COMPROMISED

CANDIDATE TRACKING DEGRADED

Reth leaned forward.

"Abort the exercise. Launch every available recovery craft."

"Already underway."

"How many groups remain in the affected zone?"

"Eight."

"Personnel?"

"Forty."

Reth watched the storm move across the map.

It was growing faster than the atmospheric models predicted.

A new warning appeared.

UNAUTHORIZED SIGNAL DETECTED

The signal originated from beneath Complex Nine.

It carried a Talyrn emergency authentication code.

An obsolete one.

Maelis frowned.

"That's impossible."

"Explain."

"That code was retired ninety-three years ago."

The transmission opened.

A man's voice filled the compartment.

"Control, this is Recovery Team Twelve. We have personnel trapped below the processing complex."

Reth froze.

"Team Twelve?"

Maelis was already searching.

"There is no Recovery Team Twelve assigned to the district."

The voice continued.

"Requesting extraction. Four survivors. Atmosphere failing."

Static crackled across the speakers.

Then the same voice spoke again.

Exactly the same words.

Same breathing.

Same pauses.

Same background noise.

"Control, this is Recovery Team Twelve. We have personnel trapped below the processing complex."

Maelis looked at Reth.

The commandant felt a cold pressure forming beneath his ribs.

"Find the source recording."

"Commandant—"

"Now."

The computer located it.

An archived emergency transmission.

Recorded ninety-three years earlier.

During the final evacuation of Processing Complex Nine.

The speaker was identified as Lieutenant Arel Dov.

Official cause of death: industrial containment collapse.

Recovery status: unrecovered.

Reth stared at the screen.

"Shut down all automated signal relays."

Maelis hesitated.

"Sir?"

"Something down there is transmitting with a dead man's voice."

### II. The Royal Hotel

By the time Nara reached the maintenance chamber, the ash was thick enough to hide the sun.

Taren found the entrance first.

A rusted service door stood half-buried in the side of the ridge.

Jarek forced the manual release.

The door moved a few centimeters.

Stopped.

Then moved again with a shriek of corroded metal.

A narrow corridor waited beyond.

Dark.

Cold.

Dry.

Nara swept her helmet lamp across the walls.

Old industrial markings.

Emergency shelter instructions.

A faded evacuation diagram.

And a warning painted beneath several layers of dust.

DO NOT OPEN INNER ACCESS

Someone had scratched another message below it.

The letters were crooked.

Deep enough to cut through the paint.

IT LEARNS YOUR VOICE

Bren leaned forward.

"That's encouraging."

Lysa touched the writing.

"Someone carved this by hand."

"How long ago?" Taren asked.

"Long enough."

Jarek inspected the pressure door at the far end.

Its seal indicators were dark.

"Backup power's dead. Mechanical lock still works."

"Can we shelter here?" Nara asked.

"Yes. If the hinges hold."

They entered.

Bren and Jarek dragged an equipment cabinet across the outer entrance.

Taren unfolded the portable atmospheric monitor.

Lysa checked everyone's respirator filters.

Nara set their locator beacon on a raised platform and activated it. The local lamp pulsed, but no receipt came back from Control.

She left it transmitting.

Outside, the storm struck.

The first impact sounded like heavy rain.

Then gravel.

Then thousands of small objects scraping across the door.

The noise grew until conversation became difficult.

A minute later, their external sensors registered almost complete darkness.

The storm had arrived.

They were alive.

For the moment.

Jarek sat on the floor and removed the slate from his pack.

"Five points each for reaching the hotel."

Bren looked at him.

"Only five?"

"Complimentary accommodation."

"Terrible establishment."

"No refunds."

Nara smiled.

Then a voice spoke over their emergency channel.

"Please."

The smile vanished.

A second transmission followed.

"Help us."

Taren checked the signal.

"Group Twelve. Northeast. One-point-six kilometers."

Four life signs appeared.

One amber.

Three green.

Lysa leaned toward the display.

"How old are those readings?"

Taren checked. The relay was refreshing the display, but the suit packets beneath it carried no readable time.

"I can't tell."

Nara studied the map. Group Twelve's last verified route crossed the eastern industrial trenches. A staff-refuge shaft stood near the reported position, linked to their shelter by a maintenance conduit.

Bren rose.

"We can reach them underground."

"If they're there," Lysa said.

Nara activated her microphone.

"Group Twelve, this is Candidate Group Seventeen. Identify yourselves."

Silence.

Then:

"Nara?"

The voice belonged to her younger sister.

Her sister was on Vaerith's northern continent, hundreds of kilometers away.

"Nara, please. I'm scared."

Jarek slowly lowered the slate.

Lysa looked directly at Nara.

"Do not answer."

The voice changed.

"Control, this is Recovery Team Twelve. Four survivors."

Then Bren heard his own voice coming from the radio.

"Double points."

Nara switched off the receiver.

"Nobody follows a voice. We hold here."

The atmospheric monitor sounded a different alarm.

Taren checked the sampling tube, wiped it clean and tested again. The reading continued to rise. Ash was forcing its way through the old ventilation intake.

Jarek put his hand against the housing. Air hissed around a seam behind the manual damper.

"The handle shuts this grille. It doesn't close the feed below it."

"How long on filters?" Nara asked.

Lysa compared the contamination reading with their remaining cartridges.

"Forty minutes at this rate. Less if the intake fails completely."

Their beacon still showed no acknowledgement. They had no extraction time.

The map placed a mechanical isolation valve at the conduit junction. Beyond it, the refuge shaft offered another way to the surface.

Nara laid a finger on the junction.

"We shut the feed and inspect the refuge access. We stay together. No sealed door gets opened."

Lysa watched her.

"And the four people on that display?"

"We look for physical evidence. Suit tags, a person we can see. We don't trust the relay to tell us who is alive."

The plan had a practical purpose. It also took them closer to the signal. Nara knew how much she wanted that to be a coincidence.

She transmitted their route and intended return time on the beacon's narrow emergency channel. Again, no receipt.

Jarek marked their way back on the slate. Bren checked the inner access wheel, leaving the cabinet braced against the outer entrance.

At the door, Lysa caught Nara's sleeve.

"If the valve works, we come back."

Nara nodded.

"If we find evidence of survivors, we assess it here. Together."

They checked each other's seals before stepping through.

### III. The Conduit

The inner pressure door opened into a maintenance tunnel.

Nara stepped through first.

Her helmet lamp illuminated a narrow passage stretching into darkness.

Metal pipes ran along the ceiling.

Condensation dripped from broken insulation.

The air pressure was low but stable.

They kept their respirators sealed.

Jarek checked the structural map.

"Conduit runs east for two kilometers. Several access shafts. Some collapsed sections."

"Any power?"

"Intermittent."

Bren raised his lamp.

"Why?"

"Because something is drawing current."

"From where?"

"Good question."

They moved in single file.

Nara first.

Taren behind her.

Lysa in the middle.

Bren next.

Jarek last, carrying the repair kit.

The tunnel was filled with old machinery.

Pumps.

Valves.

Inspection cabinets.

Heavy emergency doors.

Many had been forced open decades earlier.

Others remained sealed.

Every few hundred meters, they found evacuation signs.

Most pointed west.

Away from Complex Nine.

After twenty minutes, they reached a junction.

A large circular door blocked the eastern passage.

Its surface carried deep scratches.

The isolation valve stood beside the door. Jarek turned it until the flow indicator dropped. Taren watched the shelter monitor on his wrist; the contamination reading stopped climbing.

They could go back.

A second route marker pointed through the circular door toward the staff refuge. Four suit signals still showed beyond it.

Nara kept her hand off the wheel.

"Read the access status. We are not opening it."

Jarek connected his diagnostic unit to what the old diagram marked as a maintenance socket. He had not checked whether its data connection was isolated from the lock.

The display illuminated.

A status message appeared.

INNER ACCESS RESTRICTED

BIOLOGICAL CONTAINMENT ACTIVE

Jarek stopped.

"Nara."

She came closer.

"What?"

"This isn't an ordinary maintenance door."

Another message appeared.

CONTAINMENT FAILURE — NINETY-THREE YEARS ELAPSED

AUTOMATED ISOLATION MAINTAINED

"Nearly a century," Taren said.

Jarek looked from the warning to the route marker on his map.

The map did not show a quarantine.

Nara checked the map.

Group Twelve's emergency beacon was somewhere beyond it.

"Can we contact Control?"

Taren tried.

Nothing.

Only static.

Then something knocked on the other side of the door.

Three times.

Slowly.

Everyone froze.

A voice came through the metal.

"Please."

A pause.

"We're trapped."

Nara approached cautiously.

"Identify yourself."

"My name is Eren."

"Which candidate group?"

"Twelve."

"How many of you?"

"Four."

Lysa leaned close to Nara.

"Ask something the radio hasn't already told us."

Nara nodded.

"Eren, what color are your assessment markers?"

Silence.

Then:

"Please."

The voice sounded closer.

"Open the door."

Jarek's diagnostic unit changed.

AUTHORIZATION ACCEPTED

The door lock released.

Nobody had touched the control.

Nara stepped backward.

"Jarek."

"It accepted the diagnostic handshake. I didn't send an opening command."

The locking bolts retracted.

One.

Two.

Three.

A thin black line appeared between the door and its frame.

Something pushed from the other side.

The door opened a few centimeters.

Bren grabbed the emergency wheel and forced it shut.

A violent impact struck from the other side.

He shouted.

Jarek grabbed the wheel with him.

Another impact.

The entire frame shuddered.

A hand appeared through the gap.

It had too many joints.

Its fingers bent around the edge of the door.

The skin was gray.

Wet.

Covered in tiny dark nodules.

Lysa stumbled backward.

The fingers tightened.

Metal creaked.

Nara seized the emergency cutting tool from Jarek's kit.

She drove its heated blade into the exposed fingers.

A shriek erupted from beyond the door.

Not one voice.

Dozens.

Men.

Women.

Children.

All screaming at once.

The hand withdrew.

Bren and Jarek slammed the door shut.

Nara hit the mechanical locking lever.

The bolts engaged.

Something struck the other side again.

Then again.

Then silence.

Bren backed away.

His glove was split across the palm. When the wheel had kicked backward, it had trapped his last two fingers against the frame.

Lysa sealed the glove before she opened the medical pouch. Bren watched her hands and said nothing.

"What," he whispered, "was that?"

Nobody answered.

Their radio came alive.

Nara heard herself speak.

Perfectly.

"Jarek. What's our score?"

The five candidates stood motionless.

The voice continued.

"One hundred points for every person we bring back alive."

Jarek stared at the speaker.

Then he took the slate from his pack.

His hands were shaking.

He scratched a single line through the scoreboard.

"New rule," he said.

"No more answering voices."

### IV. The Dead Team

Commandant Reth Varun watched the recording three times.

The emergency signal had not originated from Group Twelve.

It came from beneath the abandoned complex.

The life signs had been fed through an old industrial relay. Valid archive credentials had let the relay present them as current emergency traffic.

The voices were worse. One came from a dead recovery officer. Others had been spoken by living candidates only minutes earlier.

Reth ordered the ordinary recovery craft to remain outside the contaminated region and called for containment specialists.

A senior medical officer opened the quarantine record.

Ninety-three years earlier, a reclamation organism had spread from its processing vats into the lower conduits. The record described engineered waste cultures that incorporated material they should have broken down.

One image showed a worker caught against a control cabinet. Dark tissue joined him to the wall. His mouth was open; so were the mouths forming beside it.

Reth closed the image.

"Survivors?"

"The last recovery team never returned. Thermal clearance failed below the plant. They sealed the remaining access points."

"Under whose authority?"

"An earlier Continuance emergency administration. The regional transfer file marked the surface reclaimed. The quarantine annex was never attached."

Reth looked at the training zone map.

The exercise had been conducted directly above it.

For decades.

"Preserve every site-transfer record," he said. "Independent review. Our training authority is included."

The medical officer nodded, then stopped him before he closed the file.

"Commandant. Some of the recovered tissue retained neural structure. We cannot tell you whether anything inside it remained aware."

Reth looked once more at the closed image.

"Neither can we tell the candidates that a human voice means a human survivor."

Reth looked at the five candidate signals moving west through the old service tunnel.

Then he gave an order he had never expected to issue.

"Prepare an armed containment insertion."

### V. Everybody Goes Home

The candidates had retreated nearly a kilometer when the first door opened behind them.

It did not explode.

It did not make a sound.

The lock simply disengaged.

Nara heard a faint metallic click.

She turned.

Far behind them, a rectangular patch of darkness interrupted the tunnel lights.

Something stood within it.

Human-shaped.

Still.

Its head was tilted at an unnatural angle.

"Bren?"

Bren turned his lamp.

The figure stepped into the light.

It wore a Talyrn industrial uniform.

Old.

Torn.

Covered with black growth.

Its face had been destroyed below the eyes.

Something moved beneath the skin.

Then it spoke.

"Control, this is Recovery Team Twelve."

Nara's stomach tightened.

"Run."

They ran.

The thing followed.

At first it walked.

Then its body changed.

Its legs folded beneath it.

Its arms extended.

The spine bent backward.

It began moving on four limbs.

Fast.

Its hands struck the concrete with wet impacts.

Taren screamed.

Bren caught him by the pack and pulled him forward.

Jarek threw a maintenance lamp behind them.

The figure passed through the light.

For an instant Nara saw its back.

More faces were embedded beneath the skin.

Small ones.

Old ones.

Some with their eyes open.

One mouth moved.

"Help us."

The tunnel ahead narrowed.

A structural support had collapsed, leaving a gap barely wide enough for one person at a time.

Nara pushed Taren through first.

Then Lysa.

Then Jarek.

Bren turned toward the approaching creature.

"Go!"

Nara grabbed his arm.

"Don't be stupid."

"Somebody has to slow it!"

"Not alone."

Jarek was waiting beside the gap with his equipment pack open. Nara pulled out an industrial sealant canister.

The material was designed to expand rapidly inside damaged pressure joints.

Jarek understood immediately.

He connected the canister to the emergency power cell.

"Thirty seconds."

"Can you make it faster?"

"Twenty."

The creature entered the narrowing tunnel.

Bren threw a piece of broken pipe.

It struck the thing's shoulder.

The creature barely reacted.

Nara backed through the gap.

Bren followed. A torn support caught Nara above the elbow and opened her sleeve. Lysa pressed a seal patch over the cut while Jarek activated the canister.

White industrial foam erupted across the narrow opening.

It expanded violently.

The creature struck it.

A black arm punched through.

Jarek increased the electrical charge. The damaged connector flashed beneath his gloves. He held it in place until the foam hardened into a dense plug.

When he let go, the glove liners came away from his palms. He closed his hands before Bren could see.

The arm remained trapped.

For several seconds it continued moving.

Then the fingers began to separate into thin strands.

The strands searched the air.

Lysa watched in horror.

"It's changing."

Nara seized the power cell.

"Then we don't stay."

They ran.

Behind them, the plug began cracking.

The shelter door was still intact when they reached it.

Nara forced it open.

The five candidates stumbled into the maintenance chamber.

Bren collapsed against the wall.

Lysa checked his hand.

Taren sank to the floor.

Jarek sealed the inner door and immediately began welding its locking mechanism shut.

Nara keyed the beacon’s emergency transmitter.

"Kharad Control, Group Seventeen. Emergency. Biological containment breach. Hostile organism inside the service tunnels."

Static.

She repeated the message.

This time a voice answered.

"Group Seventeen, this is Commandant Varun."

Nara reached toward the receiver, then stopped.

"Recovery check. Beacon challenge. No spoken response accepted."

She pressed the sealed challenge switch. The beacon generated a new number and sent it on its narrow recovery link.

Taren watched the independent verifier. An answer arrived signed against the current recovery key held in their offline exercise kit. The display showed the challenge number and their beacon serial together.

He checked both twice.

"Current key. Our challenge. Our unit."

Only then did Nara answer.

"Commandant, we need extraction."

"Understood. Hold your current position. Do not open the outer entrance."

"Sir, there are other candidates—"

"Group Twelve is accounted for."

Nara stopped.

"What?"

"All members of Group Twelve were extracted before the storm reached them."

The room went silent.

"Then the suit signals were false too?"

Reth did not answer immediately.

When he spoke again, his voice was hard.

"The old relay supplied them. Do not reconnect equipment to the local network. Your recovery verifier stays isolated."

Taren held the small device clear of the wall sockets.

A metallic impact struck the inner bulkhead.

Everyone turned.

Another impact.

Then scratching.

The welds glowed faintly.

Jarek backed away.

"It's here."

Nara checked the outer entrance.

The storm was still raging.

Filter projections indicated lethal exposure after prolonged contact.

Beside her, their emergency beacon was transmitting.

And somewhere overhead, a rescue craft was trying to reach them.

The inner bulkhead shuddered.

A seam split.

Black fluid seeped through.

Bren stood.

His injured hand trembled.

"How long?"

Jarek checked the weld.

"Maybe two minutes."

Nara looked at the room.

Five candidates.

Bren could barely close his damaged hand. Jarek held his burnt palms clear of his clothes. Taren was counting his breaths against the noise. Lysa checked the seal over Nara’s arm, then looked back toward the failing door.

Then Taren pointed toward the floor.

"There's another route."

A small maintenance hatch lay beneath the equipment platform.

Jarek dragged the platform aside.

A ladder descended into darkness.

"Where does it go?" Nara asked.

Taren checked the old diagram.

"Storm-drain discharge. Western slope."

Nara sent the route through the verified link. A recovery marker appeared four hundred meters beyond the drain outlet.

"Separate landing beacon," Varun told her. "Your shelter beacon stays where it is. Follow the new marker only while your verifier confirms it."

"Outside?"

"Underneath the ridge first. Then surface access."

Another impact struck the door.

Metal tore.

Nara looked at the four candidates.

"Down."

Nobody argued.

They descended.

Jarek tried to tighten the hatch with his burnt hands. Nara put her good hand over his and helped him turn the last fraction. The lid seated behind them.

As the lid settled, Nara heard a voice from the chamber above.

Her voice.

Perfectly copied.

"Everybody goes home."

### VI. The Sixth Voice

The storm drain was barely high enough to stand in.

Water flowed around their boots.

The tunnel smelled of metal and old chemicals.

They moved west.

Taren led.

Nara followed.

Bren stumbled behind her.

Lysa supported him.

Jarek brought up the rear.

After ten minutes, the sound of the storm began leaking through the passage ahead.

They had reached the outer slope.

A heavy drainage gate blocked the exit.

Jarek inspected it.

"Manual release."

He set his burnt hands against the wheel and could not hold it. Nara reached past him. Together they forced the gate open.

Beyond lay darkness.

Ash swept across the ground in thick sheets.

Nara checked their respirators.

They could survive a short crossing.

The extraction crew's landing marker still answered the verifier. Four hundred meters from the outlet. Their own locator remained in the shelter behind them.

"Single line," she ordered.

They connected their safety cable.

Taren first.

Nara second.

Bren third.

Lysa fourth.

Jarek last.

Then they stepped into the storm.

The wind nearly knocked Nara off her feet.

Visibility dropped to less than five meters.

They followed the verified landing bearing on Taren’s locator.

Ten meters.

Twenty.

Fifty.

Their filters began clogging.

Bren stumbled.

Lysa caught him. His shoulder struck her respirator coupling. The seal lifted for a moment before she forced it home.

She coughed once, hard enough to stop her. Then she tightened Bren's tether and kept moving.

Nara checked the beacon distance.

Three hundred and fifty meters.

Then three hundred.

A sound came through the squad channel.

Breathing.

Not unusual.

Five people fighting for air in contaminated weather.

Then she counted the breathing patterns.

One.

Two.

Three.

Four.

Five.

Six.

Nara stopped.

"Radio check. Names."

"Taren."

"Bren."

"Lysa."

"Jarek."

"Nara," she answered.

A sixth transmission followed.

"Nara."

Her own voice again.

"Ignore the voice," she said. "Check the line."

Something pulled it hard.

Jarek shouted. Nara felt the load catch at her harness and reached for the wall that was no longer there.

She turned her helmet lamp toward the rear.

Bren, Lysa and Jarek were still on the cable behind her. Beyond Jarek stood another figure, holding the trailing safety line in one dark hand.

Its head hung forward.

Then it raised its face.

Nara saw her own features staring back.

Not a perfect copy.

Something assembled from memory.

Eyes set slightly too far apart.

A mouth that opened too widely.

Skin shifting over structures that did not belong beneath it.

The thing smiled.

"One hundred points," it said.

Bren screamed and lunged forward.

Nara drew the emergency cutter.

The creature pulled the cable again.

Jarek lost his footing.

Lysa fell.

Taren was dragged backward.

The fall bunched them together. Jarek struck Nara’s boot as the line hauled him through the ash.

Nara had one second to decide.

If she cut the cable, they could escape.

If she did not, the creature might pull them all into the darkness.

Her instructors' words returned.

Strength creates obligation.

Then another lesson followed.

Do not waste lives for pride.

She reached past Jarek's rear harness ring and cut the trailing cable between him and the creature.

All five candidates remained connected on her side. The sudden release threw them forward.

The creature recoiled with the severed line.

For an instant, Nara saw black tendrils wrapped around it.

The organism had not merely followed the cable.

It had been growing along it.

Toward their suits.

Toward their seals.

"Run!"

They ran.

The creature came after them.

Nara heard it moving through the storm.

Not footsteps.

A wet, scraping rush.

The extraction crew’s landing beacon appeared ahead.

A red light pulsing through the ash.

Beyond it, something enormous descended from the clouds.

A Kharad extraction craft.

Its landing thrusters blasted the ground clear.

A side hatch opened. The recovery light repeated the short flash sequence on Taren's isolated verifier.

He pointed at it.

"That craft."

Armored containment troops emerged.

"DOWN!" one shouted.

Nara threw herself forward.

The troops opened fire.

Heavy pulses struck the darkness behind them.

Ash exploded into black clouds.

A scream rose above the gunfire.

Not one scream.

Hundreds.

A soldier dragged Taren toward the craft.

Another lifted Bren.

Lysa followed.

Jarek stumbled through the hatch.

Nara was last.

She turned.

Through the storm, the creature stood motionless.

It had stopped pursuing them.

Its borrowed face was already collapsing.

The eyes sank inward.

The mouth widened.

Then it spoke.

Not through a radio.

Not through a speaker.

From somewhere deep inside its shifting body.

"Don't leave us."

The voice was small.

Human.

Terrified.

For one terrible moment, Nara believed there was someone alive inside it.

Someone waiting to be rescued.

The containment officer grabbed her shoulder.

"Candidate! Move!"

She hesitated.

The thing raised one arm.

A human hand pushed through the black surface.

Five fingers.

Bleeding.

Reaching.

Then the officer dragged her inside.

The hatch slammed shut.

The extraction craft climbed.

Below them, the ash storm swallowed the creature.

Nara watched until she could no longer see it.

### VII. The Final Assessment

Seventeen days later, Nara stood before the Kharad review board.

Her left arm remained bandaged.

Bren had lost two fingers.

Taren was receiving treatment for acute stress injuries.

Lysa had suffered chemical damage to her lungs.

Jarek had severe burns across both hands.

All five had survived.

But none had returned unchanged.

The biological incident had killed eleven people.

Most were industrial recovery personnel working near the old complex when containment failed.

Two were members of the first response team.

The Kharad had sealed the entire district.

Orbital observation platforms now watched the region continuously.

No further transmissions had been detected.

That did not mean the organism was dead.

The review chamber was silent.

Commandant Reth Varun stood before them.

"You disobeyed your shelter instructions," he said.

Nara remained at attention.

"Yes, Commandant."

"You entered a restricted maintenance system."

"Yes."

"You attempted to investigate an unverified distress signal during an active industrial emergency."

"Yes."

"You exposed four other candidates to a threat you did not understand."

Nara swallowed.

"Yes."

Reth studied her.

"Do you believe you made the correct decision?"

For several seconds, she could not answer.

She had asked herself the same question every night.

She remembered the hand reaching from the darkness.

The voices.

The people who might have been trapped inside the organism.

She remembered cutting the rope.

Leaving that thing behind.

"Not entirely, Commandant."

"Explain."

"The air valve needed closing. We verified that with our own instruments. The refuge access was different. I let old route markers and suit signals turn it into the same decision. I should have brought us back after the valve was shut."

"You transmitted your route."

"But we had no acknowledgement. That was not coordination."

"And the decision to help?"

Nara looked at the floor.

"I still think we had to consider it."

"Even knowing what you know now?"

"Yes."

Reth watched her carefully.

"Why?"

"Because next time the signal might be real."

The commandant said nothing.

Nara continued.

"But considering a rescue isn't the same as rushing toward it. We nearly died because I confused wanting to help with knowing how."

A long silence followed.

Then Reth nodded.

"That," he said, "is an answer worth remembering."

He turned toward the other candidates.

"Jarek Solven. You maintained the group's equipment under extreme conditions and identified several viable escape routes."

Jarek straightened.

"You also connected an unfamiliar power system to a containment lock without verifying its purpose."

"Yes, Commandant."

"Do not do that again."

"No, Commandant."

"Bren Taval. Your proposal to hold that passage alone with a length of industrial pipe was exceptionally poor."

Bren stared ahead.

"Yes, Commandant."

"Your willingness to protect the others was not."

Bren looked surprised.

"Thank you, Commandant."

"Do not mistake that for praise of your method."

"No, Commandant."

Reth continued.

"Lysa Orven. You repeatedly identified risks your group initially dismissed. You maintained medical support while injured yourself."

Lysa stood silently.

"Taren Vos. Your route planning saved the group. You also identified the storm drain when no other candidate could find an exit."

Taren lowered his eyes.

"Thank you."

Finally, Reth returned to Nara.

"You kept your people together. You changed your plan when it failed. You brought them back. You will study why you had to."

He set a document beside the assessment file.

The training district remained suspended. Its inherited safety certification had been withdrawn. An independent panel would examine the buried quarantine record, the continuing use of the site and the delays in recovering the candidates.

"Your mistakes are in this report," he said. "So are ours."

Then he placed Jarek's battered slate on the table.

The scratched marks remained beneath ash and blood.

"One hundred points for a person brought home?"

"Yes, Commandant," Nara said.

"Keep the slate. Add a deduction."

"For what?"

"Every life placed at unnecessary risk."

She looked at the five names across its top.

"How many points?"

"Enough that you never stop counting."

### VIII. The Voices That Remained

Three months later, the western training grounds reopened under a new safety certification. The industrial district remained closed, its service connections severed and its barriers under continuous observation.

Investigators recovered enough of the old communication system to explain the archived calls. The organism had been using the hardware left below the plant.

They could not establish how it had reproduced Nara's sister. No matching recording had been found. Access to old personal traffic remained possible. So did errors in Nara's memory of the voice.

Neither explanation made her sleep better.

The unresolved question went into the report. It did not become permission to call every desperate voice false.

Years later, a young candidate stopped beneath the slate on an instructor's wall.

Five names remained legible across the top. Under them, in a later hand, someone had scratched:

FIVE RETURNED.

DEDUCTIONS STILL BEING COUNTED.

"Did they pass?" the candidate asked.

Nara looked up from the route plan on her desk.

"We continued training."

"All five?"

"All five."

The candidate studied the scratched figures.

"What happened to the people calling from below?"

Nara set down her pen.

"We never established how many were still people."

"Would you go back for them?"

Outside, a recovery craft lifted from the field. She waited until its noise had passed.

"I would start by finding out what we could reach, what we could verify and who I would be sending. Then I would have to answer that question."

The candidate looked disappointed. Nara remembered wanting a simpler answer herself.

Far below the abandoned district, a monitoring device recorded three faint impacts against a buried inspection casing.

A pause.

Three more.

Its vibration classifier assigned them to structural settling. The sequence entered the routine archive without opening an alert.

Then a quiet female voice reached the pickup.

"Scoreboard."

The damaged speech channel passed its data into the same archive.

"Who's winning?"

No biological alarm sounded. No operator was asked to listen.

Below the concrete, something scratched another mark.
