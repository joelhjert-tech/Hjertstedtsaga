# THE SILENCE BENEATH SHARDVEIL

### A Hjertstedt Saga Side Story

The Six Who Entered the Dark

The Known Reach — Shardveil Nebula

## I. The Acceptable Loss

Commander Vexthor had watched three fleets fail to clear the Shardveil Nebula.

The first had lost two patrol frigates, and the second had withdrawn after its supply vessels were ambushed. The third had never entered: its commander had examined the defensive estimates, calculated the probable casualties, and declared the operation economically unjustifiable.

That had been eight months ago.

Since then, another nineteen civilian ships had disappeared along the trade corridor.

Their crews had not been recovered.

Their passengers had not been found.

And the organization responsible continued operating behind the nebula's radioactive dust and shattered mining installations.

The Zathine Corsair Syndicate.

Forty-seven identified defensive positions.

Twelve fixed gun platforms.

Seventeen missile emplacements.

Eighteen sensor and communications stations.

Nine armed vessels.

More than three hundred fighters.

And an unknown number of captives.

Vexthor stood aboard the Caldrathi observation ship Delta Nine, watching the latest tactical assessment rotate above the command table.

His four eyes studied the overlapping firing arcs.

"An assault would be expensive," he said.

His tactical officer, Shiral, nodded.

"Blue Republic analysts estimate substantial losses if a naval force enters the central defensive volume."

"And the prisoners?"

"Unknown status."

Vexthor turned toward the dark nebula beyond the observation window.

The pirates had fortified an abandoned industrial complex within a dense field of wreckage. Radiation and metallic dust degraded long-range sensors, while old mining structures concealed missile platforms. The approach routes were narrow; an attacking formation would struggle to bring its full firepower to bear.

The Zathine had not built an invincible fortress.

They had built one that would cost more to capture than most governments were willing to pay.

Then Shiral spoke.

"Commander. A new ship has arrived."

Vexthor turned.

"Identification?"

"Independent registry. House von Hjertstedt authentication attached."

The display showed a narrow, dark insertion vessel.

Small.

Almost insignificant beside the nebula.

"Escort?"

"None nearby."

"Warship classification?"

"Covert insertion craft."

Vexthor studied the hull.

Its armor appeared light.

Its weapons were concealed.

Its engines were designed for short, controlled burns rather than prolonged combat.

He looked toward Shiral.

"Is that all?"

"One vessel, Commander."

"Troop complement?"

"Six."

Vexthor stared at him.

"Six?"

"Confirmed."

He looked back toward the pirate fortress.

Forty-seven defensive positions.

Hundreds of fighters.

Nine armed ships.

And House von Hjertstedt had apparently sent six people.

"Open communications."

The reply arrived as an audio transmission.

A woman's voice.

Calm.

Professional.

"This is Captain Nira Khan. House special-operations detachment."

Vexthor leaned toward the speaker.

"Captain, you are approaching the Shardveil exclusion zone."

"Correct."

"Do you understand the enemy's defensive strength?"

"Yes."

"Then I must advise you that a direct assault would be suicidal."

A pause.

"We're not conducting a direct assault."

Vexthor examined the small insertion craft.

"What is your objective?"

"Recover prisoners. Disable the fortress's coordinated defenses. Identify the individuals responsible for the disappearances."

"With six people?"

"With six people inside."

The distinction bothered him.

"Explain."

Khan answered:

"No, Commander."

Before he could respond, the little ship accelerated toward the nebula.

Its engines burned for seventeen seconds.

Then shut down.

Its running lights disappeared.

The vessel entered the drifting cloud of irradiated debris.

And vanished.

Vexthor stared at the empty tactical display.

Shiral spoke quietly.

"Commander, that's suicide."

Vexthor watched the last sensor trace fade.

"Perhaps."

Then he noticed something.

The House ship had not approached directly toward the fortress.

It had entered along the remains of an old industrial service route.

A route absent from the Republic's standard military charts.

Vexthor leaned forward.

"Magnify their last recorded course."

Shiral complied.

The insertion craft had disappeared behind a derelict ore-handling platform.

From there, its projected drift carried it beneath the fortress's main sensor arrays.

Vexthor studied the geometry.

Then said:

"They knew where the blind approach was."

## II. Six Against the Dead

Inside the insertion vessel Black Needle, Captain Nira Khan watched the last external navigation light disappear behind the debris.

"Passive approach confirmed," Eda Moren reported from the pilot controls. The systems engineer had brought Black Needle through the debris herself.

Khan checked the mission clock.

Forty-three minutes until the next pirate sensor sweep crossed their projected position.

The ship's interior was cramped.

Four massive Proctor-class armored figures occupied reinforced deployment cradles.

Their armor was dark bronze-black, scarred from previous campaigns.

Heavy shields and specialized weapons hung beside them.

Two lighter-equipped specialists sat in the rear compartment.

Six personnel.

Khan commanded the team.

Garrik Dane carried the heavy warded shield and close-assault weapon.

Yara Venn specialized in host identification and containment.

Orren Pike carried the breaching and suppression equipment.

Eda Moren was their systems engineer.

Lio Fen was their medical and prisoner-recovery specialist.

The four heavily armored operators were a forward detachment from a larger Hunter Lance; the remaining Hunters were with the supporting containment force.

Eda and Lio completed the six-person forward cell.

Eda set the insertion vessel to maintain its clamps and accept her encrypted recall signal. No seventh crew member remained aboard.

They were not ordinary marines.

They were Seraph Hunters and the specialists required to keep prisoners alive during their work.

Their primary enemy was not necessarily the pirate crew.

It was whatever had taken control of that crew.

Khan opened the restricted mission file.

ZATHINE STRONGHOLD — SHARDVEIL

PIRATE COMMAND AUTHORITY: COMPROMISED

POSSIBLE SERAPH HOST-PROCESSING FACILITY

CIVILIAN CAPTIVES: CONFIRMED

PRIORITY: RECOVER LIVING HOSTS AND PRISONERS

SECONDARY: ISOLATE CONVERSION INFRASTRUCTURE

The records were incomplete.

House intelligence had recovered fragments of prisoner-transfer manifests.

Human captives were being selected from pirate raids and moved toward an isolated medical section.

Some later appeared in communications wearing the same faces but using different authentication patterns.

A captured technician had described surgical machinery, sealed neural vessels, and guards who spoke using the voices of dead people.

Those details had changed the mission.

A conventional assault could kill the pirates.

It might also kill every captive and destroy the evidence of a Seraph operation.

Orren checked the seals on Eda's equipment case. One latch would not close.

"You packed the diagnostic cables again," he said.

"Because you keep using the last set to tie doors open."

"And the doors stayed open."

Eda freed the cable from the latch and snapped it shut. "Try asking before you improve my tools this time."

"I'll ask the door first."

Lio passed Orren a fresh dressing for the worn seal at his wrist. "And you'll tell me when that rubs through."

He tucked it into a pouch. "There. Everybody has an assignment."

Khan checked the target layout.

Three principal command relays linked the fortress's defenses.

Destroying them would not disable every weapon.

But it could prevent the Zathine from coordinating the forty-seven defensive positions as a single system.

The House fleet could then engage vessels attempting to escape the nebula.

That was the plan.

Not six people against an armada.

Six people opening a narrow path for a much larger operation.

A secure voice entered Khan's helmet.

"Dusk command to Black Needle."

Khan straightened.

"Go ahead."

Commander Dennis Rask appeared on her internal display.

His broad, battle-worn armor was recognizable even through the narrow communications feed.

He commanded the anti-Seraph containment element supporting the operation.

"Your latest assessment?" he asked.

"Approach remains uncompromised. Three relay targets. Prisoner compartment located provisionally. Possible host-processing site beneath the main command deck."

"Any confirmed Husk Guard?"

"None yet."

Dennis looked toward the tactical diagram.

"Then assume they have something protecting the lower levels."

"Understood."

"Fleet support is positioned beyond the primary defense field. They won't come in until you've opened a safe firing corridor."

Khan nodded.

"And the Justicar's instructions?"

Dennis's expression tightened.

"Unchanged. Living hosts are to be assessed before termination. Prisoners are not military objectives."

Another voice joined the channel.

Annette von Hjertstedt.

Head Justicar.

Her image appeared beside the mission record, the small pink rose of her judicial authority visible above her temple.

"Captain Khan."

"Head Justicar."

"Preserve the conversion records if you can do so without jeopardizing recovery. We need evidence of who ordered the transfers."

"Yes, Justicar."

"And remember that an infected person may still be recoverable."

Dennis muttered something unintelligible.

Annette looked toward his image.

"I heard that."

"I didn't say anything actionable."

"Good. Keep it that way."

Khan allowed herself one brief smile.

Then Annette's expression hardened.

"If you encounter a Seraph command entity, do not allow it access to your authenticated communications or external authority systems. Its control of a host does not give it control of every network. Keep those boundaries intact."

"Understood."

Dennis leaned toward the display.

"Bring our people home, Khan."

The channel closed.

The insertion craft reached the underside of an abandoned ore-processing structure.

Its maneuvering jets fired in short pulses.

Magnetic clamps attached to the old hull. The ore structure screened Black Needle from the predicted sensor sweep; Eda checked its concealment before leaving the controls.

The crew tested the pressure boundary.

Confirmed the approach was clear.

Then opened the equipment hatch.

The six operators entered Shardveil.

The first corridor was dark.

Old industrial dust covered the floor.

A maintenance lamp flickered beside a broken ventilation grille.

Something scratched against a metal wall.

Khan raised her weapon.

The sound stopped.

Yara scanned the passage.

"One biological trace. Human."

"Armed?"

"Unknown."

Khan advanced.

Her helmet light swept across a figure sitting against the wall.

A man in a pirate engineering uniform.

His face was pale.

His hands were covered in dried blood.

He looked up.

"Please."

Khan stopped.

"Identify yourself."

"Technician Halren Moss."

She checked the recovered crew list.

The name matched.

Moss began crying.

"They took the others."

"Who?"

He looked toward the dark corridor behind him.

"The doctors."

Khan lowered herself slightly.

"Where?"

"Below."

"How many?"

"I don't know."

He grabbed her armored wrist.

"Please don't let them put me back."

Lio approached with a medical scanner.

The readings were strange.

Elevated neural activity.

Unusual tissue responses.

Possible contamination.

Yara studied the result.

"Khan."

The captain turned.

"Possible compromised host. That is a finding, not a verdict," Yara said.

Moss began shaking his head.

"No. No, I'm still me."

Khan looked directly at him.

"Then help us keep you that way."

She ordered a secure medical restraint.

Moss did not resist.

Then the lights in the corridor went out.

A voice emerged from a wall-mounted speaker.

It was the same voice as Moss's.

Calm.

Perfectly clear.

"Technician Halren Moss reported dead forty-one hours ago."

The man on the floor began screaming.

A second message followed.

"Captain Khan, your presence is acknowledged."

Khan slowly raised her rifle.

The speaker continued.

"We have been expecting Hunters."

Beyond the corridor, something heavy struck a sealed door.

Then again.

And again.

Garrik raised his shield.

Orren activated his weapon.

Khan checked the mission clock.

They had been inside for eleven minutes.

Their enemy already knew they were there.

"All personnel," she ordered, "containment formation."

Another impact shook the door.

The damaged frame began bending inward.

Khan looked toward Yara.

"Host classification?"

"Unconfirmed. We need further examination."

"Then keep him alive."

Garrik stepped forward.

His massive shield filled the corridor.

The first locking bolt tore free.

Then the second.

The door began opening.

Behind it stood a figure in pale, damaged armor.

Human in proportion.

Wrong in movement.

Its head tilted toward the Hunters.

A ruined face stared through a broken visor.

Then it spoke with the voice of a child.

"Please don't hurt me."

Khan recognized the armor.

Husk Guard.

"Confirmed hostile," she said.

The creature lunged.

And the first battle began.

## III. The Face Behind the Door

The Husk Guard struck Garrik's shield with enough force to drive the armored Hunter backward.

Metal screamed.

The creature's first blow damaged the shield's outer plating.

Its second caught Garrik across the shoulder.

A warning flashed inside his helmet.

LEFT SHOULDER ACTUATOR DAMAGED

"Garrik, down!" Khan ordered.

He dropped to one knee.

Orren fired.

A concentrated burst struck the creature's chest, tearing away a section of pale armor.

It continued advancing.

Yara activated her null projector.

The specialized field disrupted the creature's immediate control response, slowing its movements for a moment.

Not long.

But enough.

Khan stepped past Garrik's shield and drove her weapon into the exposed gap beneath its damaged neck armor.

The creature convulsed.

Its ruined body struck the wall.

Then stopped moving.

For several seconds, no one spoke.

Lio checked the captive technician.

Moss was shaking uncontrollably. Lio checked his restraint and kept the medical scanner connected.

"Is it dead?" he whispered.

Khan examined the creature.

"Yes."

Moss stared at the corpse.

"That used to be our captain."

Yara turned.

"Your captain?"

Moss nodded.

"Captain Mara Seln."

He began crying again.

"She brought us here. She was going to sell the prisoners. Then the doctors arrived, and she changed."

Khan looked at the dead Husk Guard.

The armor had concealed most of its features.

But the surviving facial structure matched the pirate captain's registry.

The woman who had commanded the fortress was now a ruined biological weapon.

"Record identification," Khan ordered.

Yara authenticated the image.

"Recorded."

Khan looked toward the others.

"Move."

They found the first command relay twenty minutes later. Moss came with them under restraint, monitored by Lio; Khan would not leave a possibly compromised person alone in the passage.

It occupied a protected room beneath the old ore-processing machinery.

Three technicians were working inside.

Two surrendered immediately.

The third opened fire.

Khan returned a short burst, striking his weapon and shoulder.

He fell.

Lio began treating him while Eda examined the relay. The two who had surrendered were secured together, away from the wounded technician. Khan sent their location to the approaching security teams.

"Military command interface. Pirate modifications over older civilian hardware."

"Can you disable it?"

"Yes. But the defense network will detect the loss."

"Then be ready to move."

Eda disconnected the relay's targeting links and physically severed its outgoing command conduits.

A warning appeared.

OUTER DEFENSE GRID — CONTROL NODE ONE LOST

Across the nebula, seventeen missile and sensor positions stopped receiving coordinated updates.

Their local weapons remained active.

Their crews remained dangerous.

But the fortress could no longer use those positions as one integrated firing system.

Aboard Delta Nine, Vexthor watched the change.

"Commander!" Shiral exclaimed.

"What?"

"Seventeen pirate defensive positions have lost command synchronization."

"Destroyed?"

"No. Their transmitters remain active, but their targeting network has fragmented."

Vexthor leaned toward the display.

"How many House vessels are inside?"

"Still one."

"Then how are they doing this?"

Shiral hesitated.

"Sabotage?"

Vexthor stared at the nebula.

He had spent years studying naval tactics.

He understood missile ranges.

Formation discipline.

Supply lines.

Defensive screens.

But the little House vessel had entered beneath the fortress's sensor network and delivered personnel directly into its command infrastructure.

It was not fighting forty-seven defensive positions.

It was preventing forty-seven positions from fighting together.

That distinction changed everything.

Inside the station, Khan's team advanced toward the lower detention complex.

The passage narrowed.

Old machinery lined the walls.

Several doors had been welded shut.

Then Lio's medical scanner detected biological activity.

"Captain. Multiple human signatures."

"How many?"

"At least sixty. Maybe more below."

They entered a former cargo-processing hall.

Rows of medical restraints filled the chamber.

Some were occupied.

Others were empty.

The air smelled of antiseptic, burned circuitry, and something sweet that had begun rotting.

A man lay strapped to a treatment table.

His chest moved shallowly.

Beside him stood an empty neural vessel.

The machinery around his head carried Seraph biological residue.

Lio approached.

"He's alive."

Yara scanned the patient.

"No confirmed controlling organism. Severe neurological trauma."

Khan examined the next table.

A woman lay beneath a medical cover.

Her eyes were open.

She watched Khan approach.

"Are you real?" the woman whispered.

Khan lowered her weapon.

"Yes."

"That's what the others said."

Lio moved closer.

"What's your name?"

"Elena Farr."

"Elena, I'm a House medic. I'm going to examine you."

The woman began trembling.

"Don't put me in the machine."

"We won't."

"You promise?"

Lio looked toward the rows of restraints.

Then back at her.

"I promise I won't put you in that machine."

The woman closed her eyes.

For the first time in days, she slept.

Eda located the detention archive.

It recorded 143 people taken from missing civilian vessels.

Thirty-one had already died.

Sixteen had been transferred elsewhere.

Ninety-six remained alive in the complex.

Some were ordinary prisoners.

Some had undergone experimental procedures.

A smaller number carried unresolved indications of host contamination.

Lio brought Moss into the medical hall and handed his monitoring to Yara. His status remained uncertain; he would travel under medical restraint and receive an individual examination, not be counted among the ninety-six civilians in the archive.

Khan studied the records.

"Can we get them all out?"

Lio looked toward the occupied medical tables.

"Not with six people."

The captain opened the secure mission channel.

"Containment command, this is Khan. Prisoner complex located. Ninety-six living captives. We require medical extraction and security teams."

Dennis answered.

"Received. We're moving."

"How long?"

"At least twenty minutes before the first rescue craft can approach, if you can open the inner corridor. Your second relay is still controlling it. We will need a fresh assessment once it is down."

Khan looked toward Eda.

The engineer understood.

"I can take it down."

"Then do it."

A new voice entered the pirate station's internal communications.

Calm.

Human.

Almost pleasant.

"Captain Khan."

She froze.

The voice continued.

"You have entered a medical facility without authorization."

Khan looked toward the nearest security camera.

"Identify yourself."

The voice answered.

"Doctor Sareth Vale. Director of the Zathine Recovery Institute."

Yara checked the medical archive.

The name belonged to a human specialist recruited to the fortress eleven months earlier.

Then she examined his neurological records.

Her expression changed.

"Captain. Probable Seraph command host."

Khan raised her rifle toward the camera.

"You are holding prisoners."

"They are undergoing restoration."

"Against their will."

"Individual consent is an inefficient foundation for species improvement."

Khan felt a familiar coldness settle behind her ribs.

She had heard Seraph commanders speak that way before.

People became materials.

Bodies became vessels.

Identity became something to extract, store, and replace.

The voice continued.

"Your House understands the value of preserving knowledge, Captain. We merely preserve it more completely."

Khan answered:

"Release the prisoners."

A pause.

"No."

Then every door in the detention complex began closing.

And the lights went out.

## IV. The Forty-Seventh Battery

The emergency lighting returned in red.

Lio looked at the medical displays.

"Patient support systems are losing power."

Eda checked the control network.

"Somebody's shutting down the local environmental supply."

"Can you stop it?"

"Not from here. They've isolated the detention power bus."

Khan examined the floor plan.

The second relay lay above the medical complex.

The third controlled the fortress's central command and weapons distribution.

If both remained active, the Seraph commander could keep the House fleet outside while trapping the prisoners below.

Khan opened the team channel.

"Two objectives. Garrik and Yara secure the prisoners with Lio. Orren, Eda, with me."

Garrik looked toward his damaged shoulder.

"Captain, my left actuator—"

"Can you still hold a shield?"

"Yes."

"Then that's your job."

He nodded.

The teams separated.

The second relay was protected by six armed pirates and two Husk Guard.

Khan observed them through a service camera.

The pirates were ordinary personnel.

Frightened.

Exhausted.

They carried rifles and wore patched boarding armor.

The two Husk Guard stood motionless beside the command interface.

Their pale armor was heavier.

Their bodies had been extensively rebuilt.

One still wore part of a civilian identification bracelet around its wrist.

Eda examined the relay.

"I can reach it through the cooling conduit."

"How long?"

"Five minutes if the service access isn't blocked."

Orren checked his weapon.

"We'll keep them occupied."

Khan looked toward the heavy security door.

"Do not fire into the relay itself. We need to disconnect its command lines without overloading the detention power network."

Orren grunted.

"Complicated."

"Yes."

"Would be easier to blow it apart."

"Then be glad Eda came."

Khan opened the service door.

The first pirate fired immediately.

Rounds struck her Proctor armor.

She returned fire, dropping the man behind a control cabinet.

Orren advanced with his heavy weapon.

A second pirate fell.

Another surrendered, throwing his rifle to the floor.

Khan signaled for him to remain down.

Then the Husk Guard moved.

The first struck Orren across his armored chest.

He staggered backward.

The second came through the smoke toward Khan.

She raised her shield.

The impact drove her against the wall.

Her helmet display flashed warnings.

RIGHT KNEE ACTUATOR — DAMAGE DETECTED

ARMOR INTEGRITY REDUCED

She fired at close range.

The creature's shoulder armor shattered.

It continued.

Orren activated his null projector.

The field disrupted the Husk Guard's response for a moment.

Khan seized the opening.

She forced the creature against the control housing and fired into its exposed neck assembly.

It collapsed.

Orren was fighting the second.

Its cutting weapon had damaged his arm.

He used the wall to keep it from reaching the exposed engineer.

"Eda!" he shouted.

The engineer crawled beneath the relay platform.

"Working!"

The Husk Guard struck again.

Orren caught the blow on his armored forearm.

A warning sounded.

ARMOR PENETRATION — LEFT ARM

He gritted his teeth.

Then drove his weapon into the creature's chest.

The heavy discharge threw it backward.

It struck the floor.

Stopped moving.

For a moment, the chamber fell silent.

Then Eda's voice came from beneath the console.

"Relay disconnected."

The command display went dark.

CONTROL NODE TWO LOST

Khan checked the tactical feed.

Most of the inner defense grid had lost centralized targeting.

The remaining batteries could still fire independently.

But the pirate command network was collapsing.

She opened the House channel.

"Containment command. Second relay neutralized. Extraction corridor becoming available."

Dennis answered.

"Confirmed. Fleet is moving."

Then the third relay activated its emergency reserve network.

The station's main command system attempted to restore the lost connections through a separate communications path.

Eda stared at the display.

"That's not good."

Khan looked toward her.

"What?"

"They're routing the entire defense network through the central weapons platform."

"Which one?"

Eda enlarged the map.

The station's largest installation appeared.

DEFENSE POSITION FORTY-SEVEN.

A fortified ammunition and command platform connected directly to the primary detention facility.

Its weapons overlooked the only safe approach available to House rescue craft.

Khan examined the coordinates.

"Can we disable it remotely?"

"No. Its local command authority is isolated."

"Then we go there."

Orren looked at the damage on his left arm.

"How far?"

"Six hundred meters through the central maintenance spine."

He checked his remaining ammunition.

Then grinned beneath his helmet.

"Short walk."

Khan did not smile.

Because the station had begun broadcasting another message.

This one was a live video feed.

Doctor Sareth Vale stood inside the final command chamber.

His face was human.

His expression was almost gentle.

Behind him, sixteen captives were secured inside medical restraints. Their medical tags matched sixteen of the ninety-six people recorded alive in the facility; they were not the sixteen transferred away earlier.

The command chamber adjoined the detention wing, but a locked internal passage separated Vale and his hostages from the larger medical hall. A maintenance junction below the chamber connected both sides. That junction was the route Khan's team had to hold.

He looked directly into the camera.

"Captain Khan. I have considered your request."

Khan waited.

"I will release the prisoners."

A pause.

"On one condition."

"What condition?"

"Your team must surrender its armor and weapons."

Khan looked toward the captive faces.

Several were conscious.

One was crying.

The doctor continued.

"You have demonstrated an impressive ability to destroy machinery. I would prefer to preserve the minds controlling it."

Khan opened the private team channel.

"He's not negotiating."

Orren answered.

"Agreed."

Eda looked toward the third relay.

"He's using the captives to keep us from destroying the command platform."

Khan nodded.

"And the House fleet won't fire on that compartment."

She studied the platform's maintenance routes.

Then a narrow smile appeared beneath her helmet.

"Then we won't destroy it from outside."

## V. The Last Door

The final six hundred meters took nineteen minutes.

Khan's team moved through a narrow industrial passage beneath Defense Position Forty-Seven.

The walls vibrated with the firing of its heavy missile batteries.

Every discharge shook dust from the ceiling.

Outside, House ships were closing on the nebula.

Inside, the Seraph commander was preparing to kill his captives.

Eda checked the station diagram.

"The command chamber is directly above us."

"Access?"

"Maintenance lift. One pressure door. Probably guarded."

Orren looked at his damaged left arm.

"Then we'll use the door."

Khan examined the final approach.

"Not until we confirm what's behind it."

They reached the lower access.

A faint voice came through the station speaker.

Doctor Sareth Vale.

"You've come a long way, Captain."

Khan looked toward the camera.

"We're coming for the prisoners."

"Of course you are."

The doctor smiled.

"That is why your House is predictable."

The maintenance lift opened.

Three Husk Guard stood inside.

Khan shouted:

"CONTACT!"

The first creature charged.

Orren caught it against his heavy armor.

His damaged arm actuator failed.

The impact drove him backward.

Khan fired into the second creature's exposed knee joint.

It collapsed.

Eda activated the lift's emergency isolation system.

The doors began closing.

The third Husk Guard forced its arm between them.

Metal bent.

Orren stepped forward.

He drove his heavy weapon into the creature's chest and fired.

The shot destroyed part of the lift machinery.

The armored figure fell backward.

The doors closed.

For a moment, the corridor was silent.

Then a deep metallic impact struck the other side.

Another.

Then another.

Orren checked his ammunition.

"Captain, that door won't last."

Khan looked toward Eda.

"Can you reach the command controls?"

"Through the maintenance shaft. But somebody has to hold this junction."

Orren raised his weapon.

"I'll do it."

Khan examined his armor.

The left arm was almost useless.

His ammunition was low.

His cooling system was damaged.

"Orren—"

"I can hold them longer than either of you."

She hesitated.

He looked directly at her.

"Go."

Khan nodded.

"Two minutes. Then withdraw."

Orren gave a tired laugh.

"Always optimistic, Captain."

Khan and Eda entered the maintenance shaft.

The final relay lay directly above them.

Its control lines connected the entire pirate defensive network to Position Forty-Seven.

Eda opened the panel.

"Found it."

"How long?"

"Three minutes."

From below came a burst of gunfire.

Then Orren's voice.

"One down."

Another explosion.

"Two."

Khan gripped her rifle.

"Eda."

"Working!"

The relay's locking systems resisted.

Eda bypassed the damaged local control panel and physically disconnected the command trunk.

The display turned red.

CENTRAL DEFENSE AUTHORITY LOST

BATTERY FORTY-SEVEN — LOCAL CONTROL ONLY

Khan opened the House channel.

"Containment command. Final relay disabled."

Dennis answered immediately.

"Confirmed. Fleet beginning suppression."

Then Orren's voice returned.

"Khan. More contacts."

"Withdraw."

A pause.

"Can't."

She looked toward the maintenance camera.

Orren was leaning against the damaged pressure door.

Three Husk Guard bodies lay around him.

Beyond the door, more armored figures were approaching.

The door's emergency locking system had failed.

Orren was holding the manual actuator in place with the remaining strength of his powered armor.

If he moved, the barrier would open.

Behind it lay the route toward the captives.

Khan looked toward Eda.

"Can we restore the lock?"

Eda examined the schematic.

"No. The lower control mechanism is destroyed. That door blocks the lift from the command chamber into the maintenance junction. If it opens, the Guard can reach the passage behind us and the detention hall."

The sixteen hostages remained in the chamber above, on the far side of the failing door. The separation charges would bury the junction, not the chamber or the medical hall; a second secured access would still allow a recovery team to reach the hostages once the guns were suppressed.

Orren answered through the channel.

"Captain. I've got one option."

Khan understood before he explained.

The maintenance shaft's emergency separation charges could collapse the narrow approach between the command chamber and the detention facility.

They were designed to isolate industrial fires and pressure failures.

But Orren was standing on the wrong side of the separation line.

"Negative," Khan ordered.

"Then they reach the prisoners."

"We'll find another way."

"Not in time."

A heavy impact sounded.

Orren grunted.

His helmet camera shook.

Then he spoke again.

"Captain."

Khan closed her eyes.

"Go ahead."

"Get them out."

She hesitated.

Then authenticated the emergency isolation command.

Orren activated the local separation charges.

The final image from his camera showed the damaged pressure door opening.

Several Husk Guard moving through it.

And Orren standing between them and the inhabited sections.

Then the charges detonated.

The corridor collapsed.

The blast threw Khan and Eda against the shaft wall. A fragment cut through Eda's sleeve, and she cried out as her arm struck a pipe. Khan pulled her clear of the falling debris.

Orren's signal disappeared.

Khan remained motionless for two seconds.

Three.

Then Eda spoke.

"Captain."

Khan looked toward her.

"The prisoners."

She nodded.

"Move."

There would be time to count the dead.

Not yet.

## VI. The Nebula Burns

Commander Vexthor watched the first major explosion illuminate the Shardveil Nebula.

It erupted from the central defensive platform.

Then another detonation followed.

Then another.

Shiral stared at the tactical display.

"Commander, the pirate defense network is collapsing!"

"All forty-seven positions?"

"Negative. Three principal command relays are offline. The remaining batteries are firing independently."

Vexthor leaned forward.

"Then why are they exploding?"

"Several platforms are engaging false targets. Others are attempting to withdraw ammunition and craft under fire. We're detecting internal damage and uncontrolled launches."

Vexthor studied the pattern.

The pirates were losing coordination.

Their weapons remained dangerous.

But their defenses could no longer act as one formation.

Shiral caught a weak drive trace slipping between two abandoned ore platforms.

"A ship is using the old service route, Commander. I cannot keep a lock through the dust."

Vexthor compared the trace with the last known positions of the civilian traffic. The route led toward the trade corridor.

"Do not follow it into the field," he said. "Mark the last reliable bearing and transmit it to the House intercept group. Tell them our track is intermittent; they must confirm the target before firing."

Shiral sent the bearing. A House frigate acknowledged and altered course to watch the exit. Vexthor kept Delta Nine outside the firing lanes, where its sensors could continue to support the rescue.

Then the first House warship emerged from beyond the nebula.

An Oath cruiser.

HVS Flint Oath.

Two Vigil frigates followed.

A Sanctuary tender remained behind their formation.

The House had not sent six people to defeat an armada.

It had sent six people to break the defensive network so its ships could reach the raiders.

Aboard Flint Oath, Captain Elin Skaar watched the pirate contacts separate.

"Nine armed hulls identified. Three attempting to break out of the nebula."

"Remaining ships?"

"Inside the defensive field. Tracking degraded."

Skaar examined the approach geometry.

"Vigils, intercept the breakout group. Do not pursue into the blind sectors. We have civilian prisoners aboard that installation."

The frigates acknowledged.

The first pirate vessel accelerated toward the outer debris field, close to the bearing Vexthor had passed on. Long Vigil identified it independently before engaging.

Its engines burned brightly.

It launched four missiles toward HVS Long Vigil.

The House frigate turned beneath the attack.

Defensive guns intercepted two.

A third lost guidance.

The fourth struck its outer armor.

The frigate shuddered.

"Impact! Deck Five!"

"Forward defensive mount damaged!"

"Two casualties!"

The frigate's captain returned fire.

A disabling salvo struck the pirate vessel's propulsion section.

Its engines failed.

The ship began tumbling.

A second pirate vessel attempted to escape behind it.

Flint Oath opened its forward batteries.

The first shot destroyed the pirate's main gun.

The second penetrated its damaged central hull.

A secondary explosion tore through the vessel.

The pirate ship broke apart.

The third attempted to withdraw.

A Vigil frigate caught it beyond the debris field and disabled its maneuvering systems.

Across the nebula, the remaining Zathine vessels began scattering.

Without the central defense network, they could not coordinate a concentrated attack.

Several withdrew through old industrial passages.

Others were trapped behind damaged docks.

Their crews began abandoning the fortress.

The House did not destroy every pirate ship.

It did not need to.

It had broken the syndicate's ability to control the corridor.

Now the priority was recovering the prisoners before the Seraph destroyed the evidence.

Dennis Rask studied the tactical display aboard Flint Oath.

"Prisoner recovery?"

"Extraction teams approaching the detention complex."

"Remaining hostile contacts?"

"Multiple Husk Guard signatures inside the station."

He looked toward the medical recovery channel.

"Get the Sanctuary specialists ready. Full contamination precautions."

Then Annette's voice entered the secure link.

"Dennis."

"Head Justicar."

"Captain Khan has reported one operator missing."

"I know."

"Do not let the recovery teams abandon the search without confirming the compartment is inaccessible."

Dennis examined the collapsed maintenance shaft.

The internal cameras showed a wall of twisted metal.

The structure beyond was open to a damaged industrial void.

"No survivable compartment identified," he answered.

A pause.

"Then preserve everything you can," Annette said.

Dennis nodded.

He had sent Hunters into worse places.

He had brought some of them home.

Not all.

"Understood."

Inside the detention complex, Garrik and Yara were holding the main medical entrance.

Lio had begun transferring the captives into emergency transport pods.

Ninety-six living prisoners had been located.

Some could walk.

Others required stretchers.

Several were unconscious.

The medical team had no way of knowing which captives were entirely free of Seraph contamination.

Every evacuee needed examination.

But they could not leave them inside the fortress.

Garrik stood beside the damaged entrance.

His shield was cracked.

His left shoulder actuator had failed.

Yara monitored the biological readings.

"More contacts."

"How many?"

"Four."

Garrik checked his weapon.

"Of course."

The first Husk Guard appeared through the smoke.

Its armor was scorched.

Its movements were uneven.

But it was still dangerous.

Garrik fired.

The creature stumbled.

Then advanced.

Yara activated her null projector.

The specialized field briefly disrupted the creature's control.

Garrik stepped forward.

His damaged shield struck the creature's chest.

He drove it backward into the corridor.

Then fired into the exposed armor beneath its neck.

The Husk Guard fell.

The remaining contacts withdrew.

Garrik looked toward the evacuation pods.

"How many left?"

Lio answered.

"Twenty-three."

"Then hurry."

The final evacuation began twelve minutes later.

A House recovery craft attached to the damaged docking collar.

Medical teams entered under armed protection. A second team used the secured access to reach Vale's chamber and move the sixteen hostages to the medical hall. Moss and the surrendered technicians were transferred separately under guard for examination and questioning.

The prisoners were moved in groups.

The first craft departed.

Then the second.

Then the third.

Khan arrived with Eda as the last group was being loaded.

Lio looked toward them.

"Where's Orren?"

Khan said nothing.

Lio understood.

She lowered her eyes.

Then returned to her patients.

During the final fighting, a purge command cut life support to a locked room in the detention wing. Lio reached the emergency controls with a recovery medic, but two patients died before the room could be opened. Three other prisoners died of their injuries during the fighting and evacuation.

Five prisoners had died in all, including the two killed by the purge. Lio recorded their names before returning to the living.

Ninety-one were recovered alive.

Thirty-one had been confirmed dead before the mission.

Sixteen had been transferred to an unknown destination.

Khan looked at the final manifest.

The numbers were an accounting, and it was incomplete. Lio checked the list a second time, making sure the five who had died were named alongside the ninety-one who had survived.

Doctor Sareth Vale was found in the central command chamber.

He was still alive when the House containment team reached him.

His human body had been severely injured during the fighting.

The controlling Seraph organism remained active.

He attempted to negotiate.

He offered information about other host-processing facilities.

Transit routes.

Captured identities.

Military infiltration networks.

In exchange, he demanded release from House custody.

Khan listened.

Then answered:

"You can give your testimony to the Justicars."

Vale smiled.

"You think your courts can judge something older than your civilization?"

"No."

She raised her weapon.

"But they can judge what you've done to the people here."

He attempted to trigger a concealed security mechanism.

Khan fired.

The shot destroyed the control in his hand and disabled the host body's remaining ability to resist.

The House containment team secured him.

Medical specialists stabilized the host as far as possible.

The Seraph entity was placed under restricted custody.

The recovered evidence was transferred into an isolated archive.

The command center was secured.

The mission was over.

## VII. The Cost of Six

While recovery teams secured the station, Eda sent an authenticated recall to Black Needle. The craft released its clamps and followed its programmed route toward the House docking collar. A surviving local battery caught it on the approach: a near miss damaged one engine, and debris tore away the forward docking assembly. The craft reached the collar on its remaining maneuvering jets and attached using its intact aft docking port.

Five hours after entering, the little insertion vessel emerged from Shardveil with the five surviving members of the cell aboard. A few patients were with Lio and Yara; the other survivors had been transferred on the larger recovery craft.

Its armor was scorched.

One engine was damaged.

Its forward docking assembly had been destroyed.

The mission markings remained intact.

Vexthor watched it appear on the observation display.

"House vessel detected," Shiral reported.

"Contact confirmed."

"Personnel?"

Shiral checked the available biometric records.

Five living operators aboard.

One dead.

Two seriously wounded.

Vexthor stared at the figures.

Then the visual transmission arrived.

Captain Nira Khan appeared.

Her face was pale.

Blood marked the side of her forehead.

Her armor had been damaged across the chest and right leg.

Behind her, Garrik sat inside a medical restraint.

Eda was receiving treatment for the fracture and fragment wounds she had suffered in the collapsing shaft. Her equipment case sat beside her; one latch hung open.

Khan picked it up and closed it. Eda watched her hand for a moment.

"He always checked it," she said.

Khan set the case within her reach. Neither of them tried to fill the silence.

Yara and Lio were helping stabilize the recovered prisoners.

One deployment cradle remained empty.

"Commander Vexthor," Khan said.

He straightened.

"Captain."

"The Zathine command network has been neutralized. House naval forces have secured the outer approach. Prisoner recovery is complete for this facility."

Vexthor looked toward the still-burning nebula.

"You entered with six people."

"Yes."

"And destroyed the fortress?"

Khan shook her head.

"No."

He frowned.

"The defensive stations are collapsing."

"We disabled their command network. Their own emergency procedures and damage contributed to the destruction. Our fleet engaged the armed ships."

"Then what did your team accomplish?"

Khan looked toward the medical compartment.

"We found the prisoners."

Vexthor remained silent.

She continued.

"We stopped the conversion operation. We recovered evidence. We opened a safe approach for the fleet."

"And the casualties?"

Her expression hardened.

"One of ours didn't make it."

Vexthor looked toward the empty deployment cradle.

"What was his name?"

"Orren Pike."

"Rank?"

"Specialist. Seraph Hunter."

"How did he die?"

Khan hesitated.

Then answered:

"Holding a corridor so the prisoners could get out."

The observer's four eyes lowered.

For the first time since the operation began, he understood what the sensor display had concealed.

He had watched explosions.

He had watched defensive positions disappear.

He had watched pirate ships retreat.

At first he had imagined the six attacking the fortress as a single overwhelming force. Even after the fleet arrived, his display had shown him only the explosions, not what had happened inside.

Instead, those six had crawled through maintenance shafts.

Fought in narrow corridors.

Carried wounded prisoners.

Lost one of their own.

And risked their lives to prevent a much larger force from having to bombard a station full of captives.

The achievement was not simply their ability to kill.

It was their ability to recognize what needed to survive.

Vexthor requested a private briefing with the House commander after the operation.

Dennis Rask appeared on the secure display.

His broad armor carried the scars of campaigns Vexthor had never studied.

"Commander."

Vexthor straightened.

"I have reviewed the mission."

Dennis waited.

"The Republic believed that the Shardveil fortress could only be neutralized through a major naval assault."

"That would have been expensive."

"And would probably have killed the prisoners."

"Yes."

"Your six operators changed the battle."

Dennis nodded.

"That was their job."

Vexthor looked toward the casualty record.

"One died."

"Yes."

"Two wounded."

"Yes."

"And you consider the mission successful?"

Dennis studied him.

For several seconds, he said nothing.

Then:

"We recovered the prisoners we could reach. We stopped this facility from processing more hosts. We captured a Seraph command entity and preserved evidence of the operation."

A pause.

"We also left people behind who are still missing."

Vexthor lowered his head.

Dennis continued.

"Success means the mission achieved its objectives. It does not mean the dead are acceptable."

The transmission ended.

## VIII. The Threat Assessment

Three weeks later, Commander Vexthor submitted his final report to Caldrathi High Command.

It was not the report his superiors expected.

He did not recommend creating a new threat classification simply because six humans had accomplished something astonishing.

He described exactly what he had witnessed.

A small insertion vessel exploiting a neglected approach.

Specialized armored personnel trained to fight an enemy ordinary infantry could not safely handle.

An engineer capable of isolating military command systems.

A medic trained to recover captives from biological contamination.

A naval force positioned to exploit the moment the defensive network failed.

And a legal command structure that had required the soldiers to preserve prisoners and evidence despite the danger.

Then he examined the casualties.

One House operator dead.

Two wounded.

A damaged Vigil frigate with dead and injured crew.

Ninety-one captives extracted alive, several in critical condition.

Thirty-one prisoners confirmed dead before rescue.

Five more lost during the operation.

Sixteen still missing.

The Zathine Corsair Syndicate's central fortress had fallen.

But the wider Seraph threat remained.

Vexthor looked toward Shiral.

"What should we call this capability?"

The younger officer hesitated.

"Special operations?"

"That's their classification."

"Then perhaps an apex threat designation?"

Vexthor studied the casualty report.

He thought of Captain Khan.

The wounded.

The empty deployment cradle.

And Orren Pike, who had died holding a broken door.

"No."

Shiral looked surprised.

"No, Commander?"

"Apex suggests invulnerability. They are not invulnerable."

"Then what do we record?"

Vexthor considered the question.

"That House von Hjertstedt can use a small specialist team to make a much larger military operation possible."

He looked toward the wreckage of Shardveil.

"And that their enemies may not understand which part of the operation is most dangerous until it is already complete."

The final discovery arrived after the main inquiry had begun.

Annette von Hjertstedt was examining the recovered prisoner-transfer archive when Kira identified a discrepancy.

Sixteen captives had been shipped out of Shardveil before the assault.

Their departure had been recorded.

Their destination had not.

But the navigation file contained one surviving authorization signature.

It belonged to an old transit corridor.

A route absent from current civilian maps.

The address was incomplete.

The receiving authority was unknown.

And the transfer had been authenticated by someone using a legitimate commercial identity that should not have possessed access to the Seraph network.

Annette studied the record.

"Lucy."

"Yes?"

"Can you verify the original authorization?"

"Partially. The signature is genuine. The issuing authority remains unidentified."

Annette looked toward the casualty file.

Sixteen people.

Still missing.

She opened a secure communication channel.

"Dennis."

His reply arrived seconds later.

"Go ahead."

"We've found where the next investigation begins."

He studied the data.

Then looked toward the unknown transit route.

"Another nest?"

"We don't know."

"Another pirate fortress?"

"We don't know that either."

Dennis was silent.

Then:

"How many people?"

Annette examined the manifest.

"Sixteen confirmed transfers."

He nodded.

"Then we start with sixteen."

Three weeks earlier, after the evacuation and the first recovery work, the surviving House vessels had begun departing Shardveil.

The pirate stronghold remained dark.

Its defensive command network was destroyed.

Its remaining installations were being secured by recovery teams.

The prisoners who had survived were receiving treatment.

And aboard the Sanctuary tender, one deployment berth remained empty.

Captain Nira Khan stood beside it.

A small identification plate had been attached to the frame.

ORREN PIKE

SERAPH HUNTER

She rested one armored hand against the metal.

There would be an inquiry.

A casualty hearing.

Letters to his family.

A record of his final actions.

She would have to explain why he had remained behind.

Why she had authorized the emergency separation charge.

Why the mission had continued after his death.

She would answer every question.

That was part of command.

Then the shipwide medical channel sounded.

Another patient required assistance.

Khan looked toward the empty berth one final time.

Then turned and went back to work.

Because the prisoners were alive.

Because sixteen others remained missing.

And because somewhere in the Known Reach, another sealed door was waiting to be opened.

Six had entered Shardveil.

Five had returned.

And ninety-one people had been given the chance to leave the darkness behind.

The End.
