# THE LAST PASSENGER OF VEYR'S CROSSING

A Tale from the Known Reach

The first thing Commander Elias Vorn noticed was that the ship had no business being there. The second was that someone aboard it was still paying for electricity. He stood behind the tactical station on the bridge of the House von Hjertstedt reconnaissance frigate HVS Skymning, watching the sensor returns resolve into an old passenger transport. Beyond the forward screens, the Veyr's Crossing relay hung against a field of distant stars, its ancient Varanthi structure broken in three places and surrounded by the debris of centuries. Its authentication systems were unreliable, its lanes absent from modern navigation libraries, and the nearest inhabited settlement lay more than six days away through poorly maintained approaches. Elias had spent nineteen years in House service learning what could hide in places the maps had forgotten.

"Contact bearing zero-eight-four, elevation minus twelve," Lieutenant Sera Kald reported. "Range eighty-three thousand kilometers. Passive thermal return confirmed. I'm getting reactor emissions, Commander."

"From the relay?" Elias asked.

"Separate contact. Civilian hull, approximately nine hundred meters. Minimal maneuvering emissions. No active communications." Sera adjusted her filters, bringing a long passenger spine and four engine nacelles onto the display. Several windows showed faint internal illumination. "She's old. I'm not finding a current registry match."

Captain Mara Teld rose from her chair and joined them. Compact and close-cropped, with a mechanical left hand that clicked softly when she flexed it, she studied the image without touching the display. She had refused every offer to replace the hand after the Pale Corridor boarding action; she liked machinery that made noise when it worked. "Run the historical registries," she said. "Civilian passenger traffic, old imperial routes, pre-collapse salvage records. Everything we have."

The Skymning had come to Veyr's Crossing after House intelligence detected unauthorized authentication attempts around several neglected relay approaches. Three merchant ships had disappeared during the previous month, although none had been confirmed destroyed. Raiders using obsolete navigation keys were one possibility, and Mara's orders were to document the relay and return without provoking unnecessary trouble. The frigate carried eighty-six personnel, a small boarding detachment, a Justicar and enough specialists to examine the relay, but Elias had no intention of bringing her alongside an unidentified ship until he understood what was aboard. He was preparing a passive survey when Sera's search returned a match.

"Passenger transport Mourning Star," she said. "Registered to the Veyr Transit Cooperative. Last authenticated departure seventy-three years ago." She checked the record again before continuing. "Twelve thousand, four hundred and eighteen registered passengers. Two hundred and ninety-three crew. She left Tarsen Hollow for the old Veyr's Crossing transfer station and never arrived. The investigation closed eleven years later without confirmed wreckage."

Elias looked at the transport's lights. The Skymning could not evacuate a fraction of that many people, even if any had survived. "Hold at Whisper Range while we build a sensor picture," he recommended. "If somebody's using her as a concealed base, I don't want to find out alongside."

Mara agreed and ordered witness drones deployed for passive observation. She asked Chief Engineer Tovan Rell to assess the transport's reactor profile, then called Justicar Haldane to the bridge. The drones took time to close, their feeds building a picture of mismatched hull plates and docking collars that appeared to have been sealed from the outside. The name MOURNING STAR showed beneath old scarring, along with a silver bird crossing a field of stars. One maintenance drone came out of a hatch carrying a panel, fitted it over a section that appeared undamaged, and returned inside. Another spent four minutes examining a weld without making a visible repair.

"Scheduled replacement, perhaps," Rell said over the engineering channel when Elias pointed it out. "But after seventy-three years I'd expect the schedule to have collapsed. And she shouldn't have enough fuel to keep those reactors running. Someone could have refueled her, or the registry could be wrong. I can't tell you which from here."

Sera brought a drone closer to the forward observation gallery. Behind the broad windows, a dozen figures sat around tables under weak lights. One appeared to hold a glass. Another leaned toward a companion, its face hidden in shadow. None reacted to the drone. Elias could not tell whether they were people, mannequins or something else, and the window armor prevented a reliable reading of individual body heat. Haldane arrived as Mara ordered a boarding assessment: a small team, independent communications, environmental protection and a way to withdraw without trusting the transport's systems. No one would enter until they had a secure approach.

A civilian announcement interrupted them. It welcomed passengers aboard the Mourning Star, confirmed that boarding was complete and asked parents to keep their children in designated areas during departure. The voice was calm and practiced, though interference roughened the recording. When it ended, Sera checked the signal. "It wasn't broadcasting when we arrived," she said. "It started after the drone approached the passenger spine. I can't tell whether it responded to us or whether that's coincidence."

The bridge returned to its work. Elias watched the gallery while Rell compared the reactor output with the transport's old specifications. The figures remained in their chairs. A second signal came from the forward passenger section, narrower and directed toward the Skymning. Sera isolated it, and a woman's voice emerged through a soft crackle. "Unidentified vessel, this is passenger compartment seventeen aboard the Mourning Star. We can see your lights outside. Please respond."

Mara signaled Elias to answer. "This is Commander Elias Vorn of the House von Hjertstedt reconnaissance frigate Skymning. We've identified your vessel and are assessing its condition. Are you in immediate danger?"

"I don't know." A door sounded somewhere behind her. "My name is Lyra. My brother went to find a crew member. He hasn't come back, and the doors to the corridor won't open for me."

"Lyra, are you alone?" Elias asked.

She hesitated. "Yes. I think so. I heard him outside a little while ago, but he didn't answer when I asked him our mother's name." Her breathing sharpened over the channel. "He kept telling me to open the door. He sounded as though he was standing right against it."

Mara moved closer to the communications station. "This is Captain Mara Teld. Stay inside for now. Can you tell us what happened aboard your ship?"

"Captain, I don't understand. We only departed yesterday." The words came slowly, as though Lyra was listening for something beyond the door. "My brother went out after breakfast. Everyone in the corridor was standing still. I thought they were waiting for an announcement, but one of them was holding a cup and the tea had gone black."

Elias met Mara's eyes. She did not ask Lyra to account for the missing years. Instead, she asked for her brother's name and a description of the compartment. Lyra said he was called Aren, that he wore a brown coat with a torn cuff, and that she had wedged a chair under the door handle. There was a window on the outer hull, an emergency cabinet beside it and a small service hatch in the ceiling. Her water tap still worked. The air tasted metallic, but she was breathing comfortably. While she spoke, Sera found a Lyra Venn and an Aren Venn on the passenger manifest. The matching names were evidence, Haldane cautioned, not proof of the caller's identity or of anyone else's condition.

The Justicar laid a hand on the back of Mara's chair. "We have a person asking for help and a ship full of people we haven't examined. Record the exchange and the manifests separately. Don't let the age of the records decide who gets a chance." Mara nodded. She ordered a distress report sent by a disposable courier buoy toward the nearest House station, including the relay coordinates, the missing merchants and the transport's registry. The buoy would have to traverse the same uncertain approaches as any other vessel; help was days away at best. She would not wait that long to attempt a rescue, but neither would she put a boarding party through a sealed collar without knowing where it led.

Sera mapped the passenger compartment from the transport's surviving plans and the drone footage. An external maintenance hatch lay two decks below Lyra's window, connected to a service trunk that led upward beside compartment seventeen. Rell studied the route with her. "The trunk has its own pressure boundary," he said. "If the plan is right, a cutter can seal to the hatch without opening the passenger deck to space. If the plan is wrong, my team backs out. We don't cut into a live compartment blind."

"And the ship's systems?" Mara asked.

"We don't connect to them. Manual tools, independent air and lights. I'll put a pressure sensor on the hatch before anyone opens it." Rell paused. "If Lyra can reach that emergency cabinet, there may be a maintenance diagram on the inside of the door. Ask her to describe it. Old ships get modified."

Lyra found the diagram, yellowed beneath a clear cover. With Sera's patient questions, she traced the lines from her compartment to the service trunk. One branch had been crossed out by hand, and a new route marked around it. Sera compared the description with a repair seam visible outside. "The archive plan is out of date," she said. "The trunk bends aft before it reaches her deck. We can still get there, but the team needs a longer line and a second air bottle." Elias felt the shape of the task settle into something he could act on. It did not make the passengers in the gallery any less disturbing, but it gave them a route.

Mara sent Elias with two boarders and a medic in the cutter. Rell remained aboard the Skymning to monitor the hatch readings and guide them through the altered trunk. Haldane assigned one of the boarders an independent recorder and instructed her to preserve whatever they found without disturbing the passengers unnecessarily. "You're there to bring people out," the Justicar told Elias. "Not to produce an answer for my report."

The cutter crossed beneath the transport's passenger spine, and the Mourning Star grew until its hull filled the forward view. At close range the repairs were not merely mismatched; some panels had been cut to fit by hand, their edges thick with layered welds. A pale strip of metal ran between two seams, stamped with lettering too worn to read. The cutter pilot held against the hull while the boarding team attached a seal around the maintenance hatch. Rell watched their pressure readings from the frigate. When the seal held, Elias turned the manual release, and the hatch opened inward on a dark shaft smelling faintly of warm dust. Their suit lights found a ladder and bundles of cable disappearing above and below them.

The medic, Lieutenant Nerin, checked the portable air monitors before they climbed. "Nothing useful about the passenger deck from here," she said. "We keep helmets on until I say otherwise."

They passed the alteration Lyra had described: an old trunk sealed by a heavy plate, with a narrower passage built around it. The newer walls carried the same silver paint as the rest of the ship, but someone had brushed it over scratches and old handprints. Elias stopped at a dark mark near the bend. It was a child's handprint, or a small adult's, and there were others above it, overlapping until the marks became a smear. He photographed them and moved on. Their line dragged softly behind them as they climbed toward the access panel outside compartment seventeen.

Halfway up, Lyra spoke over Elias's suit channel. Sera had kept the call open. "He's outside again." Her whisper was so quiet that Elias nearly missed it. "He says you can come through the passenger corridor. He says the service hatch is dangerous."

"Don't open the door," Elias said. "What can you hear?"

"Him breathing. And the announcement." She was silent for a moment. "No. The announcement is coming from him."

Nerin glanced at Elias through her visor. Above them, something tapped against the trunk wall: three light contacts, a pause, then three more. It could have been machinery. Elias listened for movement along the shaft but heard only his own air supply and the scrape of a boarder's sleeve against the ladder. "Rell, are you seeing any pressure change?" he asked.

"None at your position. The passenger deck beyond that panel is pressurized, but I can't tell you what's making the noise." The engineer's voice stayed level. "The manual release is on your left when you reach the landing. Don't force it until Sera confirms the compartment number."

At the landing, the panel was marked SERVICE ACCESS 17 in faded white paint. Its release wheel resisted Elias's first pull. Sera asked Lyra to knock on the inner hatch, and three uneven blows came through the metal directly ahead. When Elias knocked back, Lyra made a small sound of relief. The boarders secured themselves on either side while he eased the wheel around. A narrow door opened into the rear of the passenger compartment, and a woman stood there in a travel coat, gripping a metal water flask. She was pale and trembling, but she kept the flask raised until Elias lifted both empty hands.

"Lyra? I'm Elias. Nerin is a medic. We're going to check you and take you to the cutter." Nerin approached slowly, explaining each movement before touching her. Lyra had a shallow cut on one hand and showed signs of dehydration, but she could walk. Her eyes kept returning to the main door, where a chair leaned beneath the handle. Someone had scratched the paint around the lock from the corridor side. Elias listened, but the voice was gone. He asked if she had seen Aren after he left, and she shook her head. "I only heard him. He said our mother had sent him, but she died before we bought our tickets."

The main door handle moved down. The chair shifted a fraction and held. A calm voice outside said, "Please ensure that all personal belongings are secured." Lyra gripped Nerin's arm so hard that the medic's sleeve creased. Elias motioned the others toward the service hatch, then saw a photograph on the table: Lyra and a man in a brown coat, standing beside an older woman beneath the silver bird emblem. Lyra snatched it up when he pointed, folded it against her chest and climbed through the hatch behind Nerin. The handle moved again as Elias closed the access door. This time there was no announcement, only a gentle, repeated pressure against the chair.

They were descending when the service trunk lights came on. Elias had not touched a switch. Ahead of them the wall lamps burned a weak amber, illuminating the ladder down to the bend. The tapping began again, now below them, and one of the boarders reported movement through a grille in the bulkhead. Elias angled his light toward it. A man in a brown coat stood on the other side, his torn cuff caught against the mesh. His face was partly hidden, but he was upright and his chest seemed to move. "Aren?" Elias called, and the man raised his head. Behind him, further along the narrow passage, other figures stood shoulder to shoulder in the dim light.

Lyra tried to turn on the ladder. Nerin held her steady before she could lose her footing. "That's him," Lyra said. "Please, that's him."

Elias looked for a way through. The grille was bolted from the other side, and the altered trunk separated it from the ladder with a pressure bulkhead. A door was marked on Sera's diagram beyond the bend, but no connecting route was shown. The tapping stopped. The man lifted one hand and laid it against the mesh. Elias asked him to move toward the door, and for a moment he did not respond. Then he said, in a voice Elias recognized from the recording, "Our estimated arrival at Veyr's Crossing remains unchanged."

"That doesn't mean he isn't alive," Lyra said. "Please don't leave him there."

Elias could not tell whether Aren was injured, drugged or repeating words he had heard. He asked Sera to find another entrance while the boarders checked the seam around the grille. Rell interrupted with a pressure warning: the outer hatch they had entered was still sealed to the cutter, but the pressure in the short chamber beyond it was beginning to fall. "Something's opened on the ship side of that boundary," he said. "I can't isolate it from here. You have maybe four minutes before I lose confidence in the cutter seal."

Mara heard the report. "Elias, can you reach the person behind the grille?"

"Not yet. We're looking for the door."

Sera brought up the external footage. A maintenance drone was working beside the cutter's seal, its tool pressed against an access plate. She could not see whether it had caused the pressure loss. "There's a second hatch thirty meters aft," she said. "The old diagram puts it on the other side of that bulkhead. I don't know if the passage still connects."

"Pilot, can you reach it?" Mara asked.

"Not while we're attached here, Captain. I can move after the team is aboard."

Lyra was crying now, trying to see past Nerin. Elias put his palm against the grille opposite the man's hand. "Aren, we're coming back around," he said. "If you understand me, move away from the hatch at the far end. We're going to try to open it." The man did not move. Behind him, a figure turned its head toward Elias, and another turned after it. Nerin guided Lyra down the ladder while the boarders followed. Elias remained long enough to mark the grille and photograph the passage, then withdrew as Rell counted down the pressure margin. By the time they reached the cutter, the chamber beyond the seal was hissing. The pilot disengaged and backed clear without damaging the hatch.

Mara could have ordered the Skymning away then. She had a survivor, a distress report in transit and a ship whose systems were behaving in ways nobody understood. Instead, she ordered the pilot to the second hatch and kept the witness drones on both sides of the transport. Rell talked the boarders through another seal while Nerin treated Lyra in the cutter's small cabin. Elias remained in his suit beside the outer door. The second hatch opened on a storage passage, not the space behind the grille. A welded bulkhead blocked the route shown on the old plan, its paint the same silver as the altered trunk. The team searched for another entrance while Sera mapped every visible hatch from the drone feeds.

They found three. One led to an empty equipment locker. Another would not hold a pressure seal. Behind the third was a narrow corridor full of passenger luggage, stacked from deck to ceiling. Elias found a gap between cases and followed it for several meters until a fallen rack barred the way. Beyond it, through a slit between two suitcases, a light burned over a closed door. He called Aren's name. Something answered with the departure chime, so close that he felt its vibration through the deck. The rack was heavy, and shifting it would take tools from the cutter. He marked the position and withdrew to collect them.

"Commander," Sera said as he reached the hatch, "the transport is changing course."

The Skymning's sensors had detected a slow thrust from one of the Mourning Star's stern nacelles. Rell could not determine whether it was deliberate navigation or an automated correction. The vector carried the transport toward the debris surrounding the broken relay. Mara ordered the cutter clear while there was still room to maneuver, then brought the frigate across the predicted path. She tried hailing the transport on every civilian frequency Sera could identify. Lyra called her brother by name. No answer came except the departure announcement, repeating until Sera lowered the volume.

A drone watched the observation gallery as the Mourning Star passed behind a broad fragment of the relay. The figures were still seated. One of the tables had been overturned, though nobody had seen it fall. Mara requested a tractor solution; Rell warned that the old passenger spine might not withstand the strain, and the debris left too little room to test it safely. She ordered the pilot to shadow the transport at a distance while Sera looked for a path back to the marked hatch. Lyra sat beside Nerin with her photograph in her lap, listening to the bridge traffic without asking anyone to promise what they could not do.

The transport disappeared behind the relay fragment for less than a minute. When the Skymning cleared the obstruction, Sera's display showed only debris and the frigate's own returning drones. She widened the search, then checked the raw passive feeds one by one. One drone had lost contact while following the transport; another showed the Mourning Star's stern sliding out of view, but the image ended before the ship emerged on the other side. No one had seen a drive flash or a transit. Mara ordered an expanding search and kept the cutter ready. They found no wreckage, no reactor emissions and no usable signal from the missing drone.

They stayed through two full search cycles. Sera placed independent beacons around the relay fragment and programmed them to record rather than answer any transmission. Rell marked every unsafe approach he could identify, and the pilot checked the second hatch's projected course against the debris field twice. Haldane took Lyra's account only after Nerin had treated her and she was ready to speak, recording where her memory ended as carefully as what she remembered. The Justicar sealed the manifest, the boarding footage and the external sensor logs as separate evidence. None established what had happened to the other passengers or to the three merchant ships.

Before the Skymning left, Mara sent a second courier with the search record, the beacon positions and a request for a properly equipped recovery force. She made no claim that the transport was destroyed or that its passengers were dead. "We'll return if the beacons report her," she told Lyra. "And if another ship finds her first, they'll know where we saw your brother."

Lyra held the folded photograph in both hands. "Will they believe me?"

Haldane answered before the captain could. "They'll have your account, our recordings and my name on the report. If anyone says that means there is nothing to investigate, they can answer to me." Lyra looked down at the photograph, smoothing the crease across Aren's coat. She did not thank them. Elias was glad she did not feel obliged to.

The frigate turned toward the nearest inhabited approach, carrying one passenger whose place in the present had yet to be determined. Nerin arranged a quiet cabin and left the door under Lyra's control. Sera gave her a copy of the photograph after Rell's equipment had made a clean scan, and Haldane began the work of tracing any surviving family without treating the old compensation records as proof that none remained. On the bridge, Mara kept the relay in the rear display until the distance reduced it to a faint mark among the stars. Elias stood beside her, listening to the soft click of her mechanical hand against the arm of her chair.

Several hours later, Sera called him to the sensor station. One of the beacons had captured a brief signal before distance carried the Skymning beyond its range. There was no image and no reliable fix, only a few seconds of sound. Elias listened to the familiar departure chime, followed by the passenger-service voice beginning its welcome. Beneath it was another sound: three light knocks, a pause, and three more. Sera had already attached the recording to the beacon's location and sent it after the courier. Elias listened once more, then asked her to save the unfiltered copy as well. He could not tell whether the knocks were a message, a fault in the recording or machinery moving against an old bulkhead. Someone returning to Veyr's Crossing would need the chance to find out.
