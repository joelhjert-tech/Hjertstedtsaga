# THE NAMES BENEATH THE IRON

A Hjertstedt Saga Side Story — The Thirty-First Crossing

The laughter began before Warrant Sergeant Brenwick reached the center of the chamber.

It came from the upper diplomatic galleries, where representatives of the Known Reach sat beneath the immense observation windows of the Crown of Sol.

Brenwick kept walking.

His left leg hissed with every step.

The old House armor was gray beneath centuries of accumulated repairs and fifteen years of deliberate neglect. Its chest plate was cracked. One shoulder was missing. A long fracture crossed the visor.

The suit's internal mechanisms supported a body that had never entirely recovered from Haldane.

"Is that what House von Hjertstedt sends to represent its military?" someone whispered.

"Perhaps their shipyards have finally run out of replacement parts."

Several delegates laughed.

Brenwick stopped beneath the chamber's central lights.

Around him sat representatives of the Terran Federal Protectorate, Throne Imperium, Solar Dominion, Zenith Imperium, Hiigaran Restoration, Talyrn Continuance, and the independent human governments.

Alien delegations occupied the surrounding galleries.

The Blue Republic.

The Verdant Iron Compact.

The Caldrathi.

The Kesh.

The Orani.

Other civilizations that had accepted Terra's invitation, some attending remotely because their governments remained hostile to one another.

For six days, they had debated whether independent military forces should be permitted to enter restricted territory during civilian emergencies.

The House opposed a blanket prohibition.

Several empires demanded one.

Now the delegates wanted evidence.

Lord Dravik of the Kesh leaned forward.

"Sergeant Brenwick. We requested testimony concerning House rescue operations. Why have you arrived wearing equipment unfit for active service?"

Brenwick looked up.

"Because you asked what the House has done for the people of the Reach."

"And your armor is supposed to answer that?"

"Part of it."

An Imperial delegate frowned.

"Surely your government possesses newer equipment."

"Yes."

"Then why wear this?"

Brenwick rested his damaged gauntlet against the old breastplate.

"Because the newer suits weren't there."

The laughter faded.

Across the chamber, Joel von Hjertstedt sat with the House delegation. Beside him, Annette wore the black and crimson of the Head Justicar, a small pink rose above her temple.

Neither interrupted.

Dravik gestured toward the presentation floor.

"Then explain."

Brenwick looked toward Earth, turning slowly beyond the windows.

"Haldane Research Ring. Fifteen years ago."

He paused.

"We made thirty-one crossings before the station broke apart."

His mechanical leg hissed.

"Three hundred and twelve people came home."

He looked toward the delegates.

"Some of the people who brought them home didn't."

Brenwick remembered Haldane as fire.

An enormous research ring falling toward its own star.

A failed drive-core experiment had torn away the main stabilization assembly and pushed the station into a decaying orbit. Its thermal shields were overloaded. Its remaining engines could no longer correct the trajectory.

The station was dying.

On the bridge of HVS Last Lantern, Captain Yara Vell studied the distress reports.

"Population?"

"More than a thousand still unaccounted for," her navigator answered.

"Escape craft?"

"Most destroyed or trapped behind damaged docking structures."

"Other rescue vessels?"

"Withdrawing."

"Reason?"

"Regional command has declared the station unrecoverable. Rescue operations are prohibited inside the exclusion volume without special authorization."

Yara looked toward the main display.

A thousand people were calling from the wreck.

Some had minutes of air remaining.

Others had reached shelters with enough supplies to survive for hours.

But the station's orbit was collapsing.

Soon there would be nowhere left to go.

Brenwick approached the command table.

"Captain. The old ore-transfer gantry is still attached."

"Not for long."

"We can use the rescue sled."

"One sled won't hold a thousand people."

"No. But the abandoned ore barge beside the station has sealed cargo compartments. If we stabilize its atmosphere, we can use it as a temporary evacuation shelter and tow it clear."

Yara studied the projected route.

Two hundred and seventy meters of exposed truss.

A damaged docking collar.

No atmosphere.

Increasing radiation.

The old cargo sled could carry ten to twelve passengers each crossing.

They would need repeated trips while the crew maintained the fragile connection.

Yara turned toward her eight shipmates.

"Anyone unwilling to enter that station can say so now."

Nobody spoke.

Medic Leva Orn checked her oxygen supplies.

Engineer Joren Pell opened the docking schematic.

Rigging specialist Mara Kel examined the truss.

The remaining crew prepared navigation, medical reception, the tow barge, and damage control.

Yara nodded.

"Then we go."

The first crossing brought out eleven people.

The second, ten.

By the fifth, Brenwick's armor was already scorched.

He carried an injured research worker to the sled while a civilian dockmaster named Varek Oss helped load the passengers.

Varek was Tolu, with six thin fingers and a damaged respirator strapped across his face.

He had remained at Haldane's cargo terminal after most of the surviving station crew withdrew.

"Why are you still here?" Brenwick asked.

Varek pointed toward the line of civilians gathering behind the emergency door.

"Because someone has to count them."

He had organized the survivors by medical need.

He had marked safe passages through the damaged decks.

He had found emergency air cylinders and dragged them into the terminal.

And now he was helping the House crew load people who could barely stand.

Brenwick offered him a place on the next sled.

Varek shook his head.

"After the others."

Brenwick recognized the answer.

It was the kind people gave when they had no intention of leaving until their work was finished.

The sixth crossing almost killed them both.

A structural beam fell across the loading corridor.

The rescue sled struck the debris.

Its outer frame bent.

A little girl screamed as the compartment tilted toward the exposed truss.

Varek grabbed the manual stabilizer.

The mechanism was jammed.

He forced it downward.

The sled leveled.

Brenwick pulled the passengers across.

When he returned, Varek was still beside the controls.

His respirator had begun leaking.

"Come with me," Brenwick ordered.

Varek pointed toward the emergency board.

"Another group is coming from Medical Twelve."

"Then someone else can take over."

Varek looked toward the dark corridor.

"Who?"

Brenwick had no answer.

He went back for the next group.

On the ninth crossing, he found a Kesh boy beneath a collapsed sleeping compartment.

The child's left arm was broken.

His face was covered in dust.

When Brenwick lifted the beam, the boy recoiled from the enormous armored figure.

"Don't hurt me."

Brenwick lowered himself onto one knee.

"I'm here to take you out."

The translator struggled.

The boy looked at the fractured helmet.

"You're broken."

"Only the outside."

Brenwick secured the child's breathing mask.

"What's your name?"

"Tavo."

"All right, Tavo. Hold on."

The boy wrapped his good arm around Brenwick's neck.

They crossed the damaged truss together.

Back in the Crown of Sol, Dravik's fingers tightened around his ceremonial staff.

"Tavo?"

Brenwick looked toward him.

"Yes, Delegate."

"Tavo of the Kesh?"

"Broken left arm. Didn't cry once after we got him moving."

Dravik swallowed.

"He is my sister's son."

Brenwick nodded.

"Then I'm glad he grew up."

"He is a pilot now."

"Good."

Dravik lowered his eyes.

"The official record said a patrol vessel rescued him."

"It did."

"Nobody told us who carried him."

Brenwick looked around the chamber.

"That happened quite often after Haldane."

He paused.

"But Tavo wasn't the only child."

The twelfth crossing began with a schoolteacher named Lessa Vey.

She was Orani, with a blue-green crest and burns along both arms.

She had gathered eleven children inside a sealed classroom after the research deck caught fire.

The compartment's emergency shutters had jammed.

Hot gas was entering through the upper ventilation system.

Brenwick reached the school with a portable rescue shelter.

Lessa helped him secure the children.

Then the fire door behind them began opening.

Its actuator had failed.

Flames spread into the corridor.

Brenwick looked toward the manual control.

It stood on the far side of the door.

Beyond the safe compartment.

Lessa saw it too.

She handed him the last child's breathing mask.

"Take them."

"We can find another way."

"No."

She looked at the children.

Several were crying.

One little boy held her burned hand.

"Listen to Sergeant Brenwick," she told them. "He's taking you somewhere safe."

Then she stepped into the corridor.

Brenwick shouted after her.

"Lessa!"

She reached the manual release.

Pulled.

The fire door began closing.

Heat poured through the narrowing gap.

Brenwick pushed the children toward the rescue sled.

Lessa remained at the control.

The mechanism moved slowly.

Too slowly.

The flames reached her suit.

She screamed.

Still, she held the lever.

The door closed.

Brenwick heard the locking bolts engage.

Then Lessa's emergency channel went silent.

Eleven children survived.

Their teacher did not.

Brenwick carried them to the barge.

He remembered one asking when Lessa would join them.

He had said he didn't know.

It was a lie.

He had known.

He simply could not bear saying it.

By the fifteenth crossing, the remaining school shelters were losing atmosphere.

Leva Orn accompanied Brenwick to recover seven trapped children from a lower sleeping compartment.

They found the children beneath a table.

One was unconscious.

Another could barely breathe.

A little Orani girl with a blue-green crest watched Brenwick through his cracked visor.

She began screaming.

He knelt beside her.

"Easy."

She recoiled.

"Easy," he repeated, quieter.

Leva connected the portable shelter.

The oxygen indicator turned red.

Its reserve system had failed.

The children would suffocate before reaching the rescue sled.

Leva checked her own air supply.

Then looked at Brenwick.

"Take them."

"What?"

"I'm connecting my reserve."

"You won't have enough to get back."

"Then you'd better move quickly."

"Leva—"

"Seven children. One working tank. Make the decision."

She connected her suit's remaining oxygen supply to the shelter.

The pressure stabilized.

Brenwick began pulling it through the damaged corridor.

Leva followed.

Twenty meters from the sled, she collapsed.

Brenwick left the shelter and went back.

Her suit alarm was sounding continuously.

He tried to lift her.

She pushed weakly against his breastplate.

"Children."

"They're safe."

"Go."

He dragged her another few meters.

Then her hand fell away.

The medical monitor went flat.

He tried to resuscitate her.

Once.

Twice.

The station shook.

The rescue sled began sliding toward the damaged truss.

Captain Yara's voice burst through his helmet.

"Brenwick! The collar's moving! Get those children across!"

He looked at Leva.

Then at the shelter.

He could not carry both.

He secured her suit to a structural handhold, knowing the station might break apart before anyone could recover her.

Then he took the children.

Inside the shelter, the little Orani girl pressed her hand against the glass.

Brenwick touched the other side.

"Easy."

At Sol, Ismera slowly rose from her chair.

"That was me."

Brenwick looked toward her.

The young representative descended to the floor.

"I remember your voice."

He nodded.

"I remember you."

"I remember the woman too."

"Leva Orn."

Ismera swallowed.

"She died giving us her air?"

"Yes."

"Because of us?"

Brenwick shook his head.

"She made a choice. You were children. It wasn't yours to make."

Ismera stood motionless.

Then returned to her seat, wiping her eyes.

Brenwick waited.

When she was ready, he continued.

The eighteenth crossing nearly ended the entire rescue operation.

A cargo fuel reservoir ruptured above Haldane's transfer terminal.

Burning propellant spilled through the station's damaged handling structure.

An automatic isolation system attempted to close the emergency gates.

One jammed.

The fire was spreading toward the rescue collar.

If the remaining fuel tanks ignited, the explosion would destroy the truss, the attached rescue sled, and possibly the overloaded evacuation barge.

Varek Oss saw the warning from cargo control.

He had been working for nearly three hours without removing his damaged respirator.

His hands were burned.

His emergency oxygen was almost exhausted.

But the fuel isolation controls were still responding.

He opened the maintenance channel.

"Last Lantern, this is Haldane Cargo."

Captain Yara answered.

"Go ahead."

"Fuel reservoir's ruptured. I've got a fire moving toward your docking structure."

"Can you isolate it?"

"Trying."

The automatic release refused to function.

Varek examined the equipment diagram.

The remaining magnetic couplings could be disengaged from an exposed control station.

That would allow the burning reservoir to drift away from the transfer truss.

But the station was outside the remaining protected compartments.

And its emergency hatch had been damaged.

Varek checked the nearest survival shelter.

Too far.

He opened the rescue channel.

"Sergeant Brenwick."

"Go ahead."

"How many aboard your sled?"

"Ten."

"Get them across."

"What's happening?"

"Cargo problem."

"Varek—"

"Get them across."

Brenwick heard the old dockmaster breathing through his damaged respirator.

Then the connection went silent.

Varek crossed the exposed terminal in an emergency suit.

Radiation warnings flashed across his visor.

He reached the external control station and disengaged the first magnetic coupling.

Then the second.

The burning fuel reservoir began separating from the station.

The final coupling remained locked.

He activated the release.

Nothing.

Again.

Still nothing.

He opened the mechanical override housing.

Inside was a damaged emergency lever.

He seized it with both hands.

Pulled.

The mechanism shifted.

A few centimeters.

He pulled again.

The coupling released.

The burning reservoir moved away from Haldane.

Varek opened his communicator.

"Fuel clear."

Brenwick answered.

"Come back!"

The dockmaster looked toward the damaged hatch.

An explosion had torn through the corridor behind it.

There was no return passage.

He looked out through his visor.

The rescue sled was crossing the truss.

Ten more people moving toward safety.

"Keep counting them, Sergeant."

The fuel reservoir detonated thirty seconds later.

Its explosion missed the rescue corridor.

Varek Oss was still outside.

Brenwick made the eighteenth crossing with ten survivors.

The man who had organized the station's evacuation was never found.

By the twenty-third crossing, Haldane's reactor distribution system was failing.

The station had lost its main cooling network.

An emergency manifold beneath the docking structure was feeding superheated coolant directly into the surviving transfer truss.

House engineer Joren Pell reached the damaged manifold with two repair drones.

The automatic isolation valve was jammed.

He attempted a remote shutdown.

Failed.

He tried to cut the supply from the adjacent compartment.

The emergency controls had melted.

Only the manual release remained.

On the wrong side of a failing radiation barrier.

Joren opened the ship's channel.

"Captain, I can close it locally."

Yara examined his telemetry.

"Your suit won't survive that exposure."

"I know."

"Find another method."

"Already tried two."

Brenwick heard the exchange while loading another sled.

"Joren, wait for me."

"No."

"That's an order."

"You're a sergeant. I'm engineering."

Despite everything, Brenwick almost laughed.

Joren continued.

"You've got civilians aboard. Keep moving."

He entered the damaged chamber.

The radiation alarm began screaming.

Joren reached the manual valve.

Its handle was hot enough to burn through his outer gloves.

He attached a mechanical wrench.

Turned.

The valve moved.

Slowly.

The coolant pressure began falling.

His suit's radiation shielding failed.

He continued turning.

Then the mechanism locked.

COOLANT FLOW ISOLATED.

The transfer truss remained intact.

Joren opened his communicator.

"Captain."

"Joren?"

"Valve's closed."

"Get out."

He looked toward the door.

His vision had begun narrowing.

"Don't think I'll manage."

Yara's voice tightened.

"Stay where you are. We're sending someone."

Joren lowered himself against the wall.

"Don't."

A pause.

"There's still people waiting."

His final transmission was a weak, exhausted whisper.

"Bring them home."

The rescue crews could not reach him.

The radiation barrier collapsed before a recovery team could enter.

Brenwick crossed the truss again.

And again.

By the thirtieth crossing, the barge contained 303 survivors.

The station had less than twenty minutes before projected structural breakup.

Brenwick's armor was failing.

His left leg actuator had been damaged by falling machinery.

The shoulder plating was fractured.

The helmet visor leaked atmosphere whenever he moved too quickly.

His oxygen reserves were low.

Then another distress signal arrived.

Nine civilians remained trapped beside the cargo gantry.

Two children.

One injured researcher.

Six maintenance workers.

Captain Yara studied the station's trajectory.

"Sergeant. One more crossing."

Brenwick checked his damaged leg.

"Understood."

"Mara will hold the collar. You bring them across. Then we cut loose."

Rigging specialist Mara Kel was already preparing the emergency stabilizer.

She looked at Brenwick.

"You look terrible."

"So do you."

"That's because I'm working with you."

He gave a tired smile.

Then entered the truss for the thirty-first time.

The station began breaking apart before he reached the civilians.

An overhead support collapsed.

It struck his left leg.

The armor buckled.

Something shattered inside his knee.

He screamed.

The suit's emergency support mechanism activated, forcing his damaged leg into position.

Pain tore through him with every step.

But the civilians were waiting.

He reached them.

Loaded the children first.

Then the wounded researcher.

Then the remaining workers.

Nine people.

The final nine.

The rescue sled began moving.

Halfway across, Haldane's outer structure collapsed.

The truss twisted.

The sled struck a support.

Its aft hatch jammed.

Brenwick forced it free.

Behind him, Mara was fighting the docking stabilizer.

"Sergeant! Move!"

The damaged collar began separating from the cutter.

Mara engaged the manual locking mechanism.

The stabilizer held.

Barely.

The sled crossed the final meters.

The last civilian reached the patrol cutter.

Brenwick turned back.

Mara remained on the station side.

Her hands were still gripping the emergency controls.

"Come across!"

"Can't."

"Release the lock!"

"The collar will separate before you're clear!"

"We've got everyone!"

"Not you!"

Brenwick tried to move toward her.

His injured leg collapsed.

He fell against the hatch.

Mara looked toward the cutter's emergency thrusters.

Then back at him.

"Thirty-one crossings."

"Mara!"

"Make them count."

She activated the emergency separation sequence.

The cutter's docking clamps released.

Brenwick reached for the open hatch.

Mara's image remained on his helmet display.

She was still holding the stabilizer.

Still preventing the collapsing truss from striking the departing cutter.

Then the station broke apart.

The impact tore away Brenwick's left shoulder plate.

He was thrown into the cutter's inner bulkhead.

The emergency hatch closed.

Mara's signal vanished.

HVS Last Lantern pulled away with its overloaded rescue barge.

Minutes later, Haldane Research Ring disintegrated.

Its remaining compartments fell toward the star.

Hundreds of distress signals disappeared.

Of the nine House crew who had entered the disaster zone, six returned.

Leva Orn did not.

Joren Pell did not.

Mara Kel did not.

And among the civilians who had held back fire, opened routes, maintained life support, and carried strangers to safety were others who would never return.

Lessa Vey.

Varek Oss.

Medical workers whose names survived only in damaged hospital records.

Maintenance personnel who had remained inside collapsing compartments to keep emergency systems functioning.

Ordinary people who had never expected to become part of a military operation.

Brenwick remembered them all as best he could.

But he remembered the calls he had not answered too.

Those were the worst.

The people beyond blocked doors.

The families whose compartments vanished before he could reach them.

The voices that had begged him to come back.

He had survived fifteen years.

Some nights, he still heard them.

The Crown of Sol was silent when Brenwick finished.

He stood beneath the chamber's lights, his damaged leg supporting him with a faint mechanical hiss.

Then he reached toward the old breastplate.

"May I remove this?"

Dravik lowered his staff.

"Yes, Sergeant."

Brenwick unfastened the armor.

His fingers trembled.

The clasps resisted.

Finally, he lifted the plate free.

He turned it around.

The inside was covered in writing.

Hundreds of names.

Different languages.

Different alphabets.

Some etched into the old protective ceramic.

Others written across preserved replacement panels.

Small handprints rested between them.

Blue.

Green.

Brown.

Red.

Some with claws.

Others with soft pads.

Several belonged to children.

"After Haldane," Brenwick said, "the people we brought out wanted to thank us."

He looked down.

"I told them there were nine of us."

His voice tightened.

"Three were already dead."

He raised the plate.

"Three hundred and twelve survivors signed this armor. Those who couldn't write left their hands."

He turned it toward the delegates.

"And beneath their names, I carry the people who helped make those signatures possible."

A narrow memorial strip held the names of Leva, Joren, and Mara.

Beside it were civilian names recovered from the evacuation logs.

Lessa Vey.

Varek Oss.

Others.

Some entries remained incomplete.

No one spoke.

Then Annette von Hjertstedt stood.

"Presiding delegates, I request permission to enter the original Haldane evacuation telemetry into the public record."

The Terran chair authorized the submission.

Two documents appeared above the chamber.

The official interjurisdictional report:

HALDANE RESEARCH RING — NO VIABLE CIVILIAN EVACUATION.

The original emergency assessment:

LIMITED EVACUATION POSSIBLE.

HIGH OPERATIONAL RISK.

ESTIMATED RECOVERABLE POPULATION: 200–400.

Annette allowed the delegates to read.

"The final report omitted the limited evacuation assessment."

An Imperial representative rose.

"The assessment did not guarantee success."

"No," Annette answered.

"The rescue exposed military personnel to unacceptable danger."

"That is a judgment your authorities were entitled to make."

She looked across the chamber.

"But declaring a rescue too dangerous is different from declaring it impossible."

A Zenith delegate stood.

"Independent intervention cannot be permitted to override military command whenever an officer disagrees with the assessment."

Brenwick looked toward him.

"Then investigate our captain."

The delegate stopped.

Brenwick continued.

"Examine her orders. Examine our records. Examine everything we did."

He rested one hand against the old armor.

"But examine the withdrawal decision too."

The Zenith delegate remained standing.

Brenwick looked toward the upper galleries.

"Nobody had to pretend Haldane was safe. It wasn't."

He paused.

"And nobody had to promise we could save everyone."

His voice weakened.

"We couldn't."

He looked at the memorial names.

"But we saved some."

Dravik slowly stood.

"My nephew was one of them."

Ismera approached Brenwick.

She placed her hand over the little blue print inside the breastplate.

It fit almost exactly.

Beside it, scratched in crooked Orani letters, was one word.

Easy.

She looked toward the delegates.

"I was six years old when this man carried me out."

Her voice trembled.

"I remember his voice."

She looked toward the three House names.

"Today I learned that a woman gave me the air I needed to survive."

Then toward the civilian memorial.

"And that others died keeping the way open."

She stepped back.

"Whatever we decide here, their names belong in the record."

The hearing continued into the next day.

There was no unanimous apology.

Several governments still argued that independent military intervention could threaten sovereignty.

Others demanded stronger safeguards against using humanitarian rescue as a pretext for invasion.

But the proposed blanket prohibition failed to gain sufficient support.

Terra, House von Hjertstedt, the Blue Republic, and several human and alien delegations endorsed a narrower emergency rescue agreement.

It established procedures for verified distress calls, limited emergency access, civilian protection, independent investigation, and accountability for forces entering restricted areas.

Other governments withheld their signatures.

The agreement was imperfect.

It could not save everyone.

But it gave future commanders a way to consider a rescue before condemning civilians to abandonment.

The Haldane records were transferred for an independently supervised review.

For the first time in fifteen years, the missing evacuation assessment was part of the public evidence.

Brenwick did not remain for the closing ceremony.

He waited in a quiet corridor overlooking Earth.

His armor lay beside him.

He was trying to fasten the chest plate when Ismera approached.

"Allow me."

"I can manage."

"I know."

She helped secure the damaged clasps.

Dravik joined them, carefully lifting the heavy shoulder strap.

Neither spoke until the armor was back in place.

Then Ismera looked up.

"Easy."

Brenwick closed his eyes.

For a moment, he was back inside Haldane.

A frightened little girl.

A dying medic.

Seven children inside a shelter.

The smell of burned insulation.

The sound of someone calling for help.

Then he opened his eyes.

"Easy," he repeated.

Joel von Hjertstedt approached as the two delegates departed.

"Sergeant."

"High Marshal."

"You did well."

Brenwick looked toward Earth.

"Three of ours died."

"I know."

"And people on that station who weren't even soldiers."

"Yes."

"They've spent fifteen years calling it a successful rescue."

Joel sat beside him.

"It was."

Brenwick looked down at the old breastplate.

"Then why doesn't it feel like one?"

Joel was quiet for a while.

"Because you remember the people who didn't come back."

Brenwick nodded.

He touched Leva's name.

Then Joren's.

Then Mara's.

His hand moved across the smaller civilian memorial.

Lessa.

Varek.

Names of people who had stood between disaster and strangers they would never know.

"I still hear them," he admitted.

"The ones I couldn't reach."

Joel looked toward the distant planet.

"I know."

Brenwick swallowed.

"Do you think this hearing changed anything?"

Joel considered the question.

"It changed what they can claim not to know."

The old sergeant nodded slowly.

Then stood.

His left leg hissed.

The damaged armor settled around him.

He saluted.

Joel returned it.

As Brenwick walked toward the departing transport, he passed a delegation of rescue personnel from several different worlds.

Some wore military uniforms.

Others wore civilian medical clothing.

One carried a battered helmet beneath his arm.

They recognized the armor.

They stepped aside.

No applause.

No cheering.

Just quiet acknowledgment.

Brenwick continued walking.

Thirty-one crossings.

Three hundred and twelve survivors.

And the names of those who had opened doors, held broken machinery together, shared their last breath, and died so others could leave.

He had once believed that the worst part of Haldane was failing to save everyone.

Fifteen years later, he understood something else.

The dead had not asked him to carry their guilt.

They had asked him to carry the living.

He touched the names beneath his chest plate.

Then boarded the transport home.

Far behind him, the representatives of the Known Reach continued debating the obligations of power.

And somewhere beyond Sol, there would be another damaged ship.

Another dying station.

Another distress signal that someone would call too dangerous to answer.

Perhaps the next commander would remember Haldane.

Perhaps they would send a rescue craft.

And perhaps someone would live because another person decided to stay.

The armor carried three hundred and twelve names of the living.

The names of the dead were there to remind them why.

The End.
