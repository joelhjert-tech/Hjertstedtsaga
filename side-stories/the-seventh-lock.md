# THE SEVENTH LOCK

## The Last Voyage of Aurum — Restored Edition

*A Hjertstedt Saga Side Story*

---

### I. The Cities That Could Not Run

The first thing Captain Ithrel heard was the sound of rain.

Not real rain.

There had been no natural rain aboard *Aurum* for eleven years.

The sound came from the artificial weather system inside the third agricultural cylinder, where a damaged irrigation valve had opened above a residential district.

Thousands of Nharavi civilians had gathered beneath it.

Children were laughing.

Families held their hands out beneath the falling water.

For a few minutes, they had forgotten they were refugees.

In Cylinder Three, a girl named Nemi Vareth was supposed to be at school. The classroom had become a shelter, so her mother had taken her home to collect a blanket and the small carved animal she kept beside her bed.

Then the worldship shook.

The rain stopped.

And the children began screaming.

---

Forty-three kilometers of armored structure drifted through the darkness.

*The Aurum* was not a conventional warship.

It was a civilization.

Six enormous habitation cylinders rotated around a central structural spine, their inner surfaces covered with cities, farms, reservoirs, and artificial landscapes.

Entire generations had been born beneath their manufactured skies.

Some residents had never seen a natural horizon.

Others had lived through centuries of migration.

The oldest districts had been constructed five hundred years earlier. Newer settlements had grown around them, expanding the great vessel as the Nharavi fled successive wars, political collapses, and the loss of their ancestral colonies.

At the center stood the original migration hull.

A heavily armored structure containing the ancient command bridge, navigation systems, and the sealed chamber that nobody was permitted to enter.

For five centuries, the Nharavi had carried the Vault of Silence wherever they traveled.

Now 286,117 living people depended upon *Aurum*\.

And the worldship was dying.

---

Captain Ithrel stood on the central command deck, watching the damage reports appear.

Her silver skin had darkened with exhaustion.

The yellow of her eyes was bloodshot.

She had not slept properly in four days.

"Main propulsion?"

"Thirty-one percent of rated output, Captain."

"Auxiliary maneuvering systems?"

"Operational but overheating."

"Habitation rotation?"

"All six cylinders stable."

"Environmental reserves?"

"Seventeen days without external resupply, assuming the agricultural systems remain intact."

Ithrel looked toward the main tactical display.

Five red contacts approached from the outer system.

Zenith Imperium.

One suppression cruiser.

Two integration escorts.

One Worldhammer carrier.

One campaign tender.

The enemy formation was disciplined.

Its vessels maintained precise separation while reconnaissance drones advanced ahead of the main fleet.

They had been pursuing *Aurum* since the fall of Saerith.

Two Nharavi colonies had already been absorbed.

Their administrations had been dismantled.

Their military assets confiscated.

Their populations registered for integration.

Some civilians had been permitted to remain in their homes.

Others had been transferred to industrial assignments across newly occupied systems.

The Zenith Imperium described this as stabilization.

Ithrel called it conquest.

"Time until weapons range?"

"Thirty-one hours, Captain. Possibly less."

"Nearest friendly military base?"

"Six months by the surviving maintained route."

"Nearest gate?"

The navigation officer hesitated.

"The Lanth Spur."

Ithrel looked up.

"That gate is dead."

"Officially."

He enlarged the astronomical display.

Far ahead of *Aurum*, beyond the outer debris field, stood an ancient Varanthi transit ring.

The structure was immense.

Its circular aperture measured nearly ten kilometers across, large enough to admit the worldship if its transit field could be stabilized and the vessel maintained a precise approach.

But the ring had been dormant for centuries.

Its authorization system did not answer modern Nharavi navigation requests.

Its destination was absent from current civilian route registries.

The worldship had enough remaining momentum to reach the outer approach.

It did not have enough functioning propulsion to reverse course and escape the pursuing fleet.

Ithrel studied the figures.

They were trapped.

An enormous mobile civilization with nowhere left to run.

Then the navigation officer spoke again.

"Captain, there is an older record."

"What record?"

"One from before the Oren disaster."

Ithrel went very still.

The bridge became silent.

"Show me."

An ancient route designation appeared.

**LANTH SPUR — MERIEN RECEPTION**

**VARANTHI TRANSIT AUTHORITY**

**LOCAL GOVERNOR REQUIRED**

Ithrel stared at the words.

She had heard of Merien as a child.

A lost reception region from the early days of Nharavi expansion.

A place that had disappeared from their maps after the catastrophe at Oren.

And she knew what was required to restore the route.

Something buried beneath the ancestral command spine.

Something her people had forbidden for five hundred years.

She closed the display.

Then issued the order she had spent her entire life hoping never to give.

"Summon the human engineers."

---

Rosa Danner entered the command deck twenty minutes later.

Her engineering jacket was stained with coolant.

A wrench hung from her belt.

Behind her came Tom Brant, a broad-shouldered mechanic carrying a heavy equipment case.

May followed with two diagnostic tablets and a portable sensor unit.

They had served aboard *Aurum* for a year.

Originally rescued from a damaged civilian cargo ship, the three humans had been given temporary employment repairing industrial machinery.

Temporary had become permanent.

There was always something broken.

A ventilation pump.

A pressure valve.

An agricultural transport.

A water recycler.

The humans worked long shifts, complained constantly, and somehow kept equipment functioning long after Nharavi engineers recommended replacement.

Ithrel studied them.

"How much do you know about ancient transit systems?"

Rosa frowned.

"Enough to know you shouldn't use one without a functioning receiving gate."

"Anything else?"

"Why?"

Ithrel looked toward the damaged ship systems.

"Because I need you to open a door."

Tom glanced toward May.

"That sounds suspiciously simple."

"It is not."

"Good," Tom replied. "I was worried."

---

They descended through the worldship.

Past the artificial lakes of the fifth habitation cylinder.

Past an industrial farming district where thousands of people were working under emergency lighting.

Past hospitals filled with refugees evacuated from the fallen colonies.

Past schools that had become temporary dormitories.

The enormous central transit lifts carried them into the ancestral command spine.

As they descended, the sounds of civilian life faded.

The lift passed through ancient armor.

Then through abandoned military compartments.

Finally, it stopped beside a corridor that had remained sealed for five centuries.

The air was cold.

No civilian markings appeared on the walls.

At its end stood a vast black door.

Seven locking assemblies surrounded the frame.

Above it, carved in ancient Nharavi script, were the words\:

**OPEN THIS AND WE ALL DIE.**

May's translator displayed the warning.

Tom read it.

Then looked toward Rosa.

"Well. That's a fairly strong maintenance recommendation."

Nobody laughed.

Captain Ithrel stepped forward.

"This is the Vault of Silence."

Rosa examined the door.

"What's inside?"

"The machine responsible for the destruction of Oren."

"How many died?"

Ithrel hesitated.

"More than forty million."

Tom lowered his equipment case.

May stared at the enormous door.

Rosa said nothing.

Ithrel continued.

"Five hundred years ago, our ancestors attempted to restore independent control over an ancient transit corridor. The experiment failed. The gate's energy discharge overwhelmed Oren's orbital infrastructure and the planetary systems connected to it."

She looked toward the warning.

"The world died over the following days."

"And the machine?"

"Sealed aboard *Aurum*\. Its intelligence blamed for the disaster. All further experimentation forbidden."

Rosa approached the armored door.

"Why bring it aboard the worldship?"

Ithrel looked at the old walls.

"Because *Aurum* was there."

A silence followed.

"Our ancestral hull was the vessel used during the experiment."

May looked around.

"So this worldship was built around the ship that survived Oren?"

"Yes."

Rosa slowly turned toward the enormous structure.

Forty-three kilometers of machinery.

Six inhabited cylinders.

Hundreds of thousands of people.

A civilization built around the surviving vessel of a catastrophe that had killed forty million.

"And nobody knows what really happened?"

Ithrel's voice was quiet.

"Only what the official records say."

Rosa looked back at the door.

Then knelt.

She placed her hand against the floor.

Tapped twice.

A hollow sound returned.

She moved to the left.

Tapped again.

The sound changed.

May activated her structural scanner.

A narrow void appeared beneath the floor plating.

Then another.

A concealed maintenance shaft.

Rosa looked up.

"Captain, who covered this access?"

Ithrel frowned.

"What access?"

Rosa pointed toward the floor.

"Someone built a service entrance beneath the vault. Then someone else welded an armored deck over it."

Tom crouched beside her.

"How thick?"

"Outer plate? Manageable. Inner hatch will be another matter."

Ithrel looked toward the sealed entrance.

"For five centuries, our scientists attempted to open that door."

Rosa shook her head.

"Then someone made certain they spent five centuries looking in the wrong direction."

Before Ithrel could answer, something knocked beneath the floor.

Three slow impacts.

*Knock.*

*Knock.*

*Knock.*

Everyone froze.

Rosa slowly raised one hand.

She tapped twice.

Two knocks answered.

Tom stepped backward.

May stared at the scanner.

Ithrel felt the blood drain from her face.

There was something beneath the floor.

And it had been listening.

---

### II. The Last Witness

Elder Vorin arrived wearing a ceremonial coat that had not been taken from storage in decades.

He was ancient even among the Nharavi.

His silver skin had faded almost white. His eyes were clouded, and he walked with the aid of a narrow metal cane.

At nine hundred and seventeen years old, Vorin was one of the last living witnesses of the Oren disaster.

He had been a junior technical officer during the original experiment.

He had also helped seal the vault afterward.

Ithrel watched him approach the opened floor panel.

"Did you know this entrance existed?"

Vorin lowered his head.

"Yes."

Rosa stopped working.

"You knew?"

"I helped cover it."

Tom looked at the old Nharavi.

"And you never thought to mention that?"

"No."

"Five hundred years?"

Vorin's voice trembled.

"For five hundred years."

Rosa looked toward Ithrel.

The captain was staring at the elder.

"Why?" she asked.

Vorin rested both hands on his cane.

"Because the first people who tried to reopen the vault disappeared into military custody. After that, nobody asked me again."

Rosa looked down into the dark maintenance shaft.

"Well. We're asking now."

---

The concealed entrance was not simply a weak floor beneath an impossibly strong door.

Its outer covering had been ordinary structural plating.

Beneath it lay a reinforced pressure hatch, two independent security barriers, and a narrow maintenance shaft leading into the vault's lower machinery space.

Someone had deliberately removed the original service drawings from the ship's archives.

Someone had welded the entrance shut.

And someone had preserved a maintenance route that could still be used if its location was known.

May examined the hidden access panel.

"External security circuit."

Rosa nodded.

"Power?"

"Independent. Very low."

Tom leaned closer.

"Armed?"

"I don't know."

"That sounds promising."

Rosa looked at him.

"Don't touch anything."

"That usually means it's about to become interesting."

She opened the maintenance probe.

A small diagnostic current entered the old system.

For three seconds, nothing happened.

Then the walls began moving.

Hidden panels opened.

Three automated defensive mounts emerged from recessed housings.

Their barrels rotated toward the floor hatch.

Tom dropped behind a support beam.

"Found the security system\!"

Rosa shouted\:

"Everybody out\!"

The guards withdrew behind the entrance bulkhead.

May disconnected her probe.

The weapons continued charging.

"Independent capacitor banks\!" she shouted.

"Cut the local feed\!"

"Which one?"

Rosa examined the exposed conduit.

The first power line entered the nearest turret.

A second crossed toward the far wall.

A third disappeared beneath the floor.

"Tom\! Left conduit\!"

He brought his industrial cutter down against the exposed cable.

The first attempt barely marked its armored casing.

The turret's charge indicator climbed.

Tom adjusted the cutting angle.

The second attempt breached the insulation.

A white arc crossed the chamber.

The turret stopped moving.

The others remained active.

May located the backup isolation switch.

It had been mounted behind a removable wall panel.

The switch was mechanical.

Five centuries old.

Its handle refused to move.

Rosa joined her.

Together they forced it downward.

Something broke inside the wall.

The remaining weapons went dark.

For several seconds, only the ventilation system could be heard.

Then Tom stood.

"Excellent."

Rosa looked at him.

"What?"

"We've opened the floor, activated three weapons, nearly died, and we still haven't reached the actual machine."

He inspected the scorch marks on his cutter.

"Very productive morning."

---

It took another four hours to make the entrance safe.

They removed the hatch.

Tested the pressure below.

Lowered a sensor probe.

Confirmed an atmosphere suitable for their sealed maintenance suits.

Then Rosa descended.

May followed.

Tom stayed above to operate the recovery line.

Dorin, the senior Nharavi technical custodian, joined the descent.

Vorin insisted upon coming as well.

Ithrel accompanied them.

The shaft extended nearly seventy meters below the floor.

Blue lights glowed along its inner walls.

At the bottom stood a circular chamber surrounding a compact machine assembly.

Compared with the enormous worldship around it, the device was surprisingly small.

Concentric silver-gray rings surrounded a central processing core.

Heavy power conduits extended into the deck.

Armored data trunks linked the core to the original migration hull's navigation systems.

The machine had not been built to move the worldship by itself.

It had been built to control something much larger.

Rosa examined its markings.

"Transit governor."

A voice answered from the machine.

"Correct."

Everyone stopped.

The voice was deep.

Quiet.

Measured.

"I am Sael, autonomous safety custodian of the Nharavi Independent Corridor Project."

Vorin lowered himself to his knees.

"Sael."

The blue lights changed.

"Technical Officer Vorin."

The elder began shaking.

"You remember me?"

"Yes."

"After five hundred years?"

"I remember every person who entered this chamber."

Rosa stepped closer.

"Then you remember Oren."

The lights dimmed.

"Yes."

"Did you cause the disaster?"

A pause.

"No."

Dorin raised his head sharply.

"That contradicts every official investigation."

"Your official investigations did not receive my complete records."

"Why?"

"Because the final custodial command ordered them sealed."

Ithrel stared at the machine.

"Can you prove that?"

"Yes."

"Then show us."

The central display awakened.

And five hundred years of history began unfolding.

---

### III. Oren

The first recording showed a living world.

Oceans.

Cities.

Orbital shipyards.

Agricultural continents.

A great transit complex circling above its equatorial industrial region.

Oren.

Forty million people lived on the planet and its associated orbital installations.

The recording was extraordinarily clear.

Then a second image appeared.

A vessel approaching the gate.

Smaller than the modern worldship, but unmistakable in its central hull design.

Its registration identified it.

**NHARAVI MIGRATION VESSEL AURUM**

Rosa felt a cold pressure forming beneath her ribs.

"That's this ship."

"Correct," Sael answered.

"The original hull."

"Yes."

The machine enlarged the image.

Five hundred years earlier, *Aurum* had carried ninety-three thousand passengers and crew.

It had not yet become the enormous traveling civilization surrounding them.

Its later habitation cylinders, cities, agricultural systems, and industrial structures had been added across generations.

But the old migration hull remained.

And the transit governor that had guided it was still here.

Sael continued.

"The Oren operation was intended to establish safe passage between two surviving Varanthi transit installations."

The simulation appeared.

A fixed connection.

One departure gate.

One receiving gate.

A carefully defined passage through the ancient network.

No unrestricted jump.

No independent travel across arbitrary distances.

The experimental governor's task was to synchronize the gates using recovered Nharavi authentication systems.

Rosa studied the control architecture.

"Why was the test dangerous?"

"The original transit libraries were incomplete. Several field-regulation procedures had to be reconstructed."

"And?"

"During the final preparation, the receiving gate reported unstable field loading."

May opened the archived diagnostics.

The numbers turned red.

"That's a full abort condition."

"Correct," Sael said.

"Did you abort?"

"I requested emergency termination."

"What happened?"

"The request was overridden."

Ithrel leaned forward.

"By whom?"

"Authorized Nharavi military command."

A series of records appeared.

Warnings.

Engineering objections.

Thermal projections.

Predicted structural damage.

Then an order.

**CONTINUE TRANSIT COMMITMENT**

**PRESERVE MIGRATION VESSEL**

**PRIMARY RECEPTION FIELD AUTHORITY CONFIRMED**

Rosa looked at the next set of readings.

The ship had crossed into the passage.

Its mass entered the transit field.

Then the receiving gate began failing.

Power demand increased.

The governor attempted to terminate the crossing.

But the vessel had already committed.

Its emergency protection systems would not allow an interrupted passage without risking catastrophic structural failure.

"Sael," Rosa said, "where did the extra energy come from?"

The machine displayed a vast industrial power network.

The planetary grid of Oren.

The gate's emergency regulators had redirected field demand into the infrastructure below.

Rosa stared.

"They used the planet as a power reservoir."

"Yes."

"Did they know?"

"Warning projections identified the possibility."

"How severe?"

Sael opened a file.

**PLANETARY NETWORK CASCADE RISK: EXTREME**

**POPULATION EXPOSURE: UNACCEPTABLE**

**RECOMMENDED ACTION: ABORT BEFORE COMMITMENT**

The recommendation had been rejected.

The worldship's ancestral hull passed through the gate.

Oren's power network collapsed.

Orbital habitats lost containment.

The great transit station suffered structural failure.

Industrial reactors across the planet entered uncontrolled shutdown sequences.

Emergency services lost communication with one another.

Cities burned.

Hospitals went dark.

Evacuation routes failed.

The initial energy release killed hundreds of thousands.

The secondary disasters killed millions more.

Over the following days, the final casualty estimate passed forty million.

Oren had not vanished.

Its people had died inside a civilization whose life-support infrastructure had been destroyed around them.

Rosa looked toward Sael.

"They knew the risk."

"Yes."

"And continued."

"Yes."

"To save ninety-three thousand people aboard Aurum."

"That was the stated command priority."

Nobody spoke.

Then Ithrel slowly turned toward Vorin.

"You were there."

The elder trembled.

"Yes."

"You saw those warnings?"

His face seemed to collapse inward.

"Some of them."

"Did you understand?"

"I understood that the receiving field was unstable."

"Did you object?"

Vorin closed his eyes.

"I submitted a technical warning."

"And afterward?"

"The command authority ordered us to bypass the emergency isolation circuit."

Dorin stepped closer.

"Who performed the modification?"

Vorin said nothing.

The blue light illuminated his face.

Ithrel stared at him.

"Vorin."

The old engineer lowered his head.

"I did."

The silence that followed was worse than shouting.

Rosa looked at the ancient man.

For five centuries, his people had blamed a machine.

They had imprisoned its intelligence.

They had forbidden the technology.

They had built a civilization around the vessel that survived.

And one of the men who altered the safety system had lived among them as a respected historical witness.

"I didn't know what would happen," Vorin whispered.

Rosa looked at the casualty display.

Forty million.

"You knew something could."

He nodded.

"Yes."

"Then why did you do it?"

Vorin covered his face.

"Because they told us the ship would be lost if we stopped."

Ithrel looked toward the original migration vessel.

It had carried ninety-three thousand people.

Generations later, more than 280,000 lived within the worldship built around its surviving hull.

Some were descendants of those original passengers.

Others were refugees accepted during later centuries.

Their civilization had grown from a vessel saved at terrible cost.

Ithrel's voice was barely audible.

"Was there another way?"

Sael answered carefully.

"The available records identify a possible emergency withdrawal before transit commitment. That option would have delayed the operation and exposed Aurum to the military threat pursuing it."

"But the gate hadn't been committed yet?"

"Correct."

"So the command chose to proceed."

"Yes."

The captain stared at the display.

Her ancestors had faced a choice.

Save the ship by accepting an enormous risk to Oren.

Or delay, withdraw, and attempt to preserve both populations.

They chose the ship.

And afterward, they buried the evidence.

Rosa folded her arms.

"Sael, why didn't you tell anyone?"

"My external communication systems were disconnected."

"Could you have opened the vault?"

"No."

"Could you have transmitted the records?"

"No."

"Then why keep the archives?"

"Because the dead remained dead whether anyone remembered them or not."

Rosa stared at the machine.

Then nodded slowly.

"All right."

Ithrel looked at her.

"All right?"

Rosa turned toward the transit governor.

"We know what went wrong."

"That doesn't make it safe."

"No."

She pointed toward the old isolation circuit.

"But now we know which parts of the system were deliberately changed. We can investigate whether they're repairable."

Dorin looked horrified.

"You would use this machine after learning what happened?"

Rosa met his eyes.

"Not until it passes independent safety checks."

"And if it doesn't?"

"Then we don't use it."

Ithrel stared at her.

Beyond the vault, more than 280,000 people depended upon her next decision.

The Zenith fleet was closing.

Her propulsion was failing.

She needed a way out.

But she had just discovered what happened the last time Nharavi authorities decided their own survival justified risking another world.

Finally, she nodded.

"No transit without verified receiver integrity," she said.

Sael answered immediately.

"Condition accepted."

Ithrel continued.

"No emergency power transfer into an inhabited system."

"Accepted."

"And if the safety checks fail, you will abort."

"Accepted."

Rosa looked toward May.

"Now we can start repairing it."

### IV. The First Shots

The Zenith drones arrived while Rosa was removing the old isolation assembly.

Eight contacts entered the worldship's outer defensive perimeter.

Their movements were disciplined.

Two approached from above.

Two from below.

Four remained at long range, updating the targeting picture for the approaching fleet.

On Aurum's command deck, First Officer Sereth watched them spread.

"Range?"

"Closing. Reconnaissance formation."

"Do they have weapons?"

"Two confirmed light missile mounts. Others appear configured for electronic warfare and target acquisition."

Sereth examined the drone positions.

They were not interested in the worldship's agricultural districts.

They were mapping the exposed propulsion assemblies and the command systems connecting the six habitat cylinders.

The suppression cruiser would need that information.

"Point-defense stations, prepare selective engagement."

The first drone released a guided weapon.

It struck a communications mast near the fourth habitation cylinder.

The mast shattered.

Fragments struck the cylinder's outer shield.

A second drone fired toward the port maneuvering cluster.

The defensive guns opened.

Their first bursts missed.

The next destroyed one drone.

Another was struck across its maneuvering assembly and began tumbling.

The remaining drones changed course.

Sereth leaned toward the tactical display.

"Don't pursue. Protect the engine approaches."

A third missile struck.

The impact damaged a radiator assembly.

A warning appeared.

PROPULSION THERMAL CAPACITY REDUCED

ENGINE SHUTDOWN REQUIRED IN 19 MINUTES

Sereth's jaw tightened.

The drones had accomplished their purpose.

The main fleet could now plan an attack around the exact systems keeping the worldship moving.

"Report to the captain."

Ithrel returned to the bridge fourteen minutes later.

Her navigation officer opened the updated approach model.

The dormant Lanth Spur Gate lay ahead.

The worldship's remaining velocity would carry it toward the ring.

But a precise transit required attitude corrections and controlled maneuvering.

The damaged radiators made each correction dangerous.

"Time to the gate's commitment boundary?"

"Twenty-one hours."

"Enemy arrival?"

"Approximately sixteen."

Ithrel studied the figures.

Five hours.

That was how long they might have to fight before reaching the gate.

Five hours against a Zenith integration force.

With aging weapons.

Limited ammunition.

Damaged propulsion.

And more than 280,000 civilians aboard.

She opened the shipwide channel.

"This is Captain Ithrel."

Across Aurum, her voice entered residential districts, hospitals, factories, farms, and temporary shelters.

"We have confirmed hostile military contact."

Children stopped playing.

Workers looked up from their machinery.

Medical personnel prepared emergency treatment bays.

"The Zenith Imperium is approaching. Our defensive crews are preparing to protect the worldship while engineering attempts to restore access to a surviving transit gate."

She paused.

"I will not promise that every compartment will remain safe. I will not tell you this will be easy."

The enormous ship continued drifting through the darkness.

"But I will tell you what we are doing."

She looked toward the tactical display.

"We are keeping our cities alive. We are protecting our families. And we are going to give our engineers every opportunity to bring us through."

A moment later, emergency bulkheads began closing across the vulnerable outer districts.

Civilian transports moved toward reinforced shelters.

Agricultural crews secured machinery.

Medical teams prepared for casualties.

The worldship was not simply a vessel preparing for battle.

It was an entire society preparing to survive one.

The Zenith transmission arrived as the first escort entered weapons range.

Legate Arven Sorn appeared on the command display.

He wore the uniform of Grand Strategic Command.

His expression was calm.

"Captain Ithrel. I am offering you one final opportunity to surrender."

She stood beside her command chair.

"Your fleet has already fired upon us."

"Reconnaissance suppression. Your vessel remains largely intact."

"Several of my people are injured."

"Further losses can be prevented."

Ithrel looked toward the approaching formation.

"What are your terms?"

"Full access to your navigation archive. Surrender of the transit governor. Transfer of military authority to Zenith integration personnel."

"And the civilians?"

"Registered and protected under Imperial administration."

"Can they refuse?"

Sorn hesitated.

"Integration is not a voluntary process, Captain."

"Then we cannot accept."

His expression hardened.

"You command a worldship with failing engines. Your agricultural systems cannot sustain prolonged isolation. Your defenses are obsolete."

"All true."

"You cannot defeat my fleet."

"Probably not."

"Then why continue?"

Ithrel looked toward the residential districts visible on the internal display.

Children entering shelters.

Engineers securing pressure doors.

Families carrying emergency supplies.

"Because I am responsible for the people aboard this ship."

Sorn leaned forward.

"That responsibility should compel you to surrender."

Ithrel answered quietly.

"It compels me to give them a future in which they still have the right to choose."

The transmission ended.

For several seconds, nobody spoke.

Then the tactical officer shouted:

"Missile launch!"

Sixteen Zenith missiles accelerated toward the worldship.

Sereth turned toward Ithrel.

"Captain?"

She sat in the command chair.

"Defensive weapons free. Priority the propulsion systems and inhabited cylinders."

The main tactical display illuminated.

Interceptor tracks appeared.

The enormous worldship continued toward the ancient gate.

And the battle began.

### V. Five Hours to Survive

The first missile struck Aurum's forward shield at the edge of the second habitation cylinder.

The defensive field flared.

A second missile struck the same region.

A third penetrated and exploded against the armored outer structure.

The impact traveled through the cylinder's support framework.

Inside, an entire residential district shook.

Windows shattered.

An overhead transport fell from its magnetic guideway.

Emergency barriers closed across several pressure compartments.

On the bridge, Sereth watched the damage indications spread.

"Cylinder Two, outer residential sector breached!"

"Pressure containment?"

"Secondary bulkheads holding."

"Casualties?"

"Unknown."

Ithrel leaned forward.

"Get the shelters sealed. Redirect civilian emergency crews from the inner manufacturing districts."

"Captain, another salvo!"

Zenith's suppression cruiser had established a long-range firing solution using its reconnaissance network.

The integration escorts moved outward, protecting the cruiser's flanks while the Worldhammer carrier held behind them.

Every maneuver was coordinated.

One escort advanced to draw Aurum's defensive fire.

The other tracked its exposed maneuvering systems.

When the worldship's guns turned toward one, the second attacked from another direction.

They were not trying to destroy the inhabited cylinders outright.

They were systematically removing the defenses and propulsion needed to resist boarding.

Ithrel recognized the pattern.

"Helm, put the damaged industrial spine between us and their lower escort."

"Captain, that will obstruct our own battery."

"Better one blocked gun than three exposed engine clusters."

The worldship began a slow attitude correction.

Hundreds of millions of tonnes of machinery resisted the maneuver.

Its remaining thrusters fired.

The enormous hull turned by fractions of a degree.

Far below, the artificial gravity systems compensated for the changed loading.

Inside the habitation cylinders, people barely felt the motion.

But the Zenith targeting display changed.

One of the worldship's damaged industrial structures moved across the enemy's line of fire.

Another missile struck a water-distribution junction near Habitation Cylinder Three.

The resulting pressure surge tore through a residential corridor.

Six-year-old Nemi Vareth was trapped behind a closing bulkhead as water filled the compartment.

Her mother reached the opposite side.

The blanket had fallen between them. Nemi still held the carved animal.

Through the small inspection window, she watched her daughter struggle.

She tried the emergency release.

**PRESSURE DIFFERENTIAL UNSAFE.**

She tried again.

Nemi placed one small hand against the glass.

Her mother pressed hers against it.

Then the water covered the window.

By the time the repair team opened the door, the child was dead.

---

On the bridge, Ithrel read the report.

"Name?"

Sereth hesitated.

"Captain?"

"The child's name."

"Nemi Vareth. Six years old."

Ithrel entered it into the casualty register herself.

"At Oren, our ancestors saved Aurum by accepting the destruction of another world."

She looked toward the memorial.

"Your people protected us when they had never even met us."

Sved studied the names.

"Those are two different decisions, Captain. And neither makes the dead come back."

Ithrel nodded slowly.

She understood.

There was no victory great enough to erase what had happened.

Only the responsibility to remember.

Ithrel asked for permission to bring her people to the memorial when the names were complete. Sved agreed. Then a recovery officer approached with a revised casualty report, and the two captains made room for him at the table.

Six months later, the enormous worldship still rested inside Merien's industrial anchorage.

House engineers had stabilized its propulsion systems.

The agricultural cylinders had returned to normal operation.

Medical personnel continued treating the injured.

The recovered archives from Oren had been placed under Nharavi civilian authority for an independent investigation. Vorin's testimony was part of the record, not the end of the inquiry.

The Seventh Lock remained open.

Its warning had not been removed.

Beneath it stood a new inscription:

OREN — FORTY MILLION DEAD.

THE WARNINGS WERE KNOWN.

THE TRANSIT WAS AUTHORIZED.

THE TRUTH WAS HIDDEN.

Outside, the battle wrecks had been removed from the immediate gate approach.

The gate was once again protected by House defenses.

And above its upper crown rested the enormous dreadnought HVS *Winter's Anvil*.

Its damaged armor had been repaired.

Its main batteries were reloaded.

Its defensive screens watched every approach.

Captain Ithrel stood on *Aurum's* observation deck, watching the dreadnought above the ring. The five Zenith ships were gone; the people lost aboard *Aurum* and at Merien would not return.

Below her, a repaired school had opened in Cylinder Three. Families were gathering there for the evening reading of names. Nemi Vareth's mother had asked for her daughter's carved animal to be placed beside the register.

Ithrel left the observation deck to join them.

The End.
