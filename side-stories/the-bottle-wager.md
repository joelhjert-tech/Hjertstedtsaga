### The Bottle Wager

An intercepted transmission from the Battle of Tarnis

Senior Intelligence Officer Severin Kael had spent twenty-six years studying enemy military communications.

He had interrogated captured admirals, broken naval encryption systems, infiltrated merchant syndicates and listened to commanders give their final orders before their ships were destroyed.

He had never heard anything quite like this.

"Play it again," he ordered.

The signals technician hesitated.

"Sir, the battle is still ongoing."

"I am aware of that, Lieutenant. Play it again."

The recording began.

A male voice spoke over a secure House command circuit.

"Four dreadnoughts. Nine Banners. Hundreds of warships. And Joel puts me on an assault carrier."

A second voice answered.

"Would you prefer a frigate?"

"I would prefer the largest enemy ship."

"You have an assault carrier full of Marines."

"Exactly. They deserve the largest enemy ship."

A third voice interrupted.

"That is the first time I've heard you describe wanting to hit something as concern for the troops."

Laughter.

Actual laughter.

Kael stared at the waveform.

"Identify the speakers."

The technician swallowed.

"High Marshal Joel von Hjertstedt. Anton von Hjertstedt. Christer von Hjertstedt."

"All members of the ruling family?"

"Confirmed."

"And they are arguing over ship assignments?"

"Apparently, sir."

Kael looked toward the enormous tactical display.

Outside their observation position, the Battle of Tarnis was becoming one of the largest fleet engagements the sector had seen in years.

The Garooth invasion fleet had committed hundreds of vessels against the industrial planet.

House von Hjertstedt had responded with an expeditionary armada.

Four dreadnoughts.

Nine Banners.

Hundreds of warships.

Thousands of Marines.

And the ruling family was arguing like children.

"Continue monitoring."

Kael's listening station was concealed inside a converted ore-survey vessel, drifting cold among the outer debris fields of Tarnis.

Officially, it belonged to a minor commercial survey company.

In reality, it was a Directorate of Ash intelligence platform.

Its mission was simple.

Observe House von Hjertstedt.

Determine its true military capabilities.

Identify command weaknesses.

Recover whatever intelligence could be extracted without revealing the vessel's presence.

Kael had expected the operation to be dangerous.

He had not expected it to become ridiculous.

"Sir," the technician said. "Another transmission."

"Play."

A deeper voice entered the intercepted circuit.

"Anton."

"Father."

"Your brother is commanding a fleet."

"Yes."

"Christer is identifying the enemy."

"Yes."

"Daniel is securing the infrastructure."

"Yes."

"And you are complaining."

A pause.

"Someone has to maintain morale."

Kael closed his eyes.

"Who was that?"

The technician checked the voiceprint.

"Lars von Hjertstedt."

Kael opened his eyes again.

"The Primarch?"

"Yes, sir."

"The same Lars von Hjertstedt who commands their strategic reserves?"

"Yes."

"And he is participating in this conversation?"

"Yes."

Kael looked at his deputy.

His deputy looked back.

Neither spoke.

Then the recording continued.

"Try maintaining silence," Lars said.

The channel erupted in laughter.

A moment later, another voice cut through.

"Enemy missile launch! Multiple Cinderback formations!"

The laughter stopped.

"All Banners," Joel ordered. "Defensive pattern Winter Three. Interceptors free. Keep the civilian evacuation corridor open."

Kael watched the tactical display.

The House fleet moved.

Not gradually.

Not after a long sequence of command confirmations.

It moved immediately.

Frigates expanded their defensive screen.

Destroyers shifted into interception positions.

Cruisers adjusted their firing arcs.

Missile-defense networks activated across hundreds of vessels.

Thousands of incoming Garooth missiles disappeared beneath coordinated defensive fire.

Kael leaned forward.

"Reaction time?"

"Less than four seconds from command transmission to formation response."

"Impossible."

"Confirmed by passive observation."

Kael looked back toward the intercepted conversation.

The same officers who had been laughing seconds earlier had coordinated hundreds of ships without confusion.

He began a new intelligence report.

SUBJECT: HOUSE VON HJERTSTEDT COMMAND CULTURE

PRELIMINARY ASSESSMENT: EXTREMELY UNCONVENTIONAL.

Twenty minutes later, the situation became worse.

For Kael.

Not for the House.

"Sir, we have intercepted what appears to be an internal competition."

Kael frowned.

"Competition?"

"Between the senior commanders."

"During combat?"

"Yes."

"Play it."

Joel's voice emerged from the speaker.

"Cleanest operational result. Enemy command seized, industrial infrastructure preserved, civilians recovered. Verified by the Justicars."

Another voice answered.

"That's three different objectives."

"Yes."

"You always make the rules impossible."

"Only when you're participating."

Kael listened.

Then came the important part.

"What is the stake?" Daniel asked.

"A bottle from my own northern Winter Reserve," Joel answered. "The sealed one from the old Vinterholm cellar."

Silence.

Then Anton.

"Joel."

"Yes?"

"You've refused to wager that bottle for eleven years."

"Then I suggest you make a good showing."

Kael slowly turned toward his technician.

"Did he say bottle?"

"Yes, sir."

"Of what?"

"Alcohol, apparently."

Kael stared at him.

"The High Marshal of House von Hjertstedt has committed hundreds of warships to a major engagement, and his senior officers are gambling over alcohol?"

The technician hesitated.

"Technically, sir, the fleet was committed before the wager."

"That is not helping."

Another transmission arrived.

"Anton, your assignment remains command seizure."

"Does first breach count?"

"Only if the objective is secured."

"What about killing the High Ravager?"

"Not if you destroy the command systems."

"You're taking all the fun out of this."

Kael rubbed his temples.

"Record everything."

The Directorate's intelligence doctrine was built around hierarchy.

Authority.

Fear.

Reward.

Punishment.

Officers competed for promotion, political influence, industrial allocations and the approval of superiors.

Competition was carefully controlled.

Personal rivalry during combat was considered dangerous.

It encouraged independent action.

Undermined centralized command.

Created opportunities for manipulation.

Kael had spent years exploiting exactly those weaknesses in foreign militaries.

The House appeared to have institutionalized them.

"Sir," his deputy said. "The wager has spread."

Kael turned.

"What do you mean, spread?"

"We have intercepted three Banner-level exchanges referencing additional contests."

"Between whom?"

"Captains. Senior officers. Possibly Marine commanders."

"Over what?"

The deputy consulted the transcript.

"One bottle of reserve spirits. Two cases of something called Winter Ale. A ceremonial dinner. And an argument concerning an old debt from Kestrel."

Kael stared.

"An old debt?"

"Yes."

"During a fleet battle?"

"Yes."

Kael stood.

"These people are insane."

Outside, the House dreadnought line opened fire.

A Garooth capital hulk broke apart beneath concentrated heavy-battery strikes.

Two more were disabled.

A fourth attempted to retreat.

House destroyers cut off its escape.

The entire maneuver took less than three minutes.

The tactical display registered the destruction of another Garooth capital formation.

Kael sat down.

"Highly effective insanity," he muttered.

Then the intelligence station intercepted something that made Kael genuinely uncomfortable.

A House Marine channel.

"DOES THE COMMANDER'S BET COUNT FOR THE WHOLE COMPANY?"

"No," Anton answered.

A chorus of disappointed voices followed.

"However," Anton continued, "if our company achieves the cleanest command seizure, with all assigned prisoner compartments secured, I'll provide a separate sealed bottle for the company's table."

The channel erupted.

"WINTER RESERVE!"

"FIRST BREACH!"

"GET THE ENGINEERS!"

"WHY?"

"BECAUSE WE NEED THE SHIP INTACT, YOU IDIOT!"

Kael stared at the transcript.

His deputy leaned closer.

"Sir, perhaps this is some form of psychological conditioning."

"Explain."

"The Marines are competing for recognition. Their commander is reinforcing aggressive behavior through social rewards."

Kael considered.

Then another voice entered the intercepted circuit.

Dennis Rask.

"Any man who leaves his assigned sector chasing a prize loses his place in the company and answers to me."

The Marines immediately acknowledged.

"UNDERSTOOD!"

Anton spoke.

"See? Discipline."

Dennis answered.

"You're a disease."

"A successful disease."

Kael slowly lowered his head into his hands.

His deputy waited.

Finally Kael spoke.

"They are not merely tolerating rivalry."

"No, sir."

"They are using it."

"Yes, sir."

"And the reward is alcohol."

"Apparently."

Kael looked toward the battle display.

"How many Garooth ships have they destroyed?"

"One hundred and eighty-seven confirmed destroyed or disabled since our last assessment."

Kael stared at the number.

"Of course they have."

The boarding operation began thirty-six minutes later.

Kael watched hundreds of House assault craft accelerate toward the Garooth command fortress.

The Red Kiln.

An enormous construction of captured warships, industrial machinery and armored boarding structures.

The fortress carried thousands of Garooth warriors.

Its internal defenses were extensive.

Its command decks were heavily fortified.

And according to intercepted House intelligence, human prisoners were being held in its lower manufacturing compartments.

The House could not simply destroy it.

Kael recognized the tactical problem immediately.

"That vessel will require a major boarding operation."

His deputy nodded.

"Likely severe casualties."

"Anton's assault group will take the manufacturing section. Dennis Rask will move toward command. Engineering teams will isolate the propulsion systems."

"How do you know?"

"They're discussing it on their command circuit."

The technician raised his hand.

"Another transmission."

Kael sighed.

"Play."

Anton spoke.

"Mine."

Dennis answered.

"No."

"I saw it first."

"Everyone saw it."

"I claimed it."

"You're not six."

Kael looked at the ceiling.

The technician continued.

Daniel entered the conversation.

"Neither of you owns the ship. And if you destroy its reactor, I'm charging you both for the replacement."

Anton answered.

"How much?"

"More than your bottle."

"That's extortion."

"That's engineering."

Kael turned toward his deputy.

"Are we certain these are genuine transmissions?"

"Voiceprints confirmed. Tactical behavior matches the conversation."

"Could they know we're listening?"

"Possibly, but their exchanges contain operational details consistent with their movements. We have no evidence of deliberate deception."

Kael watched the assault craft approach the fortress.

"Then what in the dead stars are we dealing with?"

The first boarding cutter struck the Red Kiln.

Its breaching charges detonated.

House Marines poured into the manufacturing decks.

The intelligence station received fragments of their communications through damaged enemy relays and short-range emissions.

Gunfire.

Orders.

Casualty reports.

And, somehow, more arguing.

"THAT ONE'S MINE!"

A burst of heavy weapons fire.

"WHO FIRED?"

A Marine answered.

"Me, Commander!"

"GOOD SHOT!"

A pause.

"TERRIBLE TIMING!"

Kael stared at the audio display.

His deputy began laughing.

Kael turned sharply.

The deputy stopped.

"Apologies, sir."

"Do you find this amusing?"

"No, sir."

Another transmission.

"COMMANDER! EASTERN PLATFORM SECURED!"

"ALREADY?"

"YES!"

"WHO AUTHORIZED THAT?"

"YOU DID, COMMANDER!"

Kael heard Anton pause.

Then answer.

"EXCELLENT INITIATIVE!"

The deputy made a strangled noise.

Kael pointed toward the door.

"Outside."

"Sir?"

"Go outside before I have you transferred to agricultural intelligence."

The deputy left.

Kael returned to the tactical display.

The House boarding formation had secured three major compartments.

Prisoner evacuation was underway.

The Garooth were losing control of their own fortress.

And Anton von Hjertstedt was apparently furious that his Marines were performing too well.

The decisive transmission arrived when the High Ravager was killed.

Kael listened to the distorted combat feed.

Heavy impacts.

Weapons fire.

A Garooth roar.

Then Dennis Rask.

"High Ravager neutralized. Joint kill."

Anton shouted.

"NO!"

Kael flinched.

Christer's voice entered.

"Recorded."

"What?"

"Joint kill. Two participants. One target."

"CHRISTER!"

"Independent witness."

Daniel interrupted.

"Neither of you has secured the command controls yet."

A pause.

Then running footsteps.

Metallic impacts.

And another voice.

"Command controls secured, Commanders."

Silence.

Daniel spoke.

"Engineering confirms."

Another pause.

Christer answered.

"Well."

Joel said something that Kael would remember for the rest of his career.

"Daniel wins."

Kael sat perfectly still.

Outside, the Garooth command fortress had been captured.

Its propulsion systems were isolated.

Its command structure destroyed.

Its human prisoners were being evacuated.

An entire enemy invasion was collapsing.

And the senior House commanders were arguing over which brother had won a bottle.

The technician looked toward him.

"Sir?"

Kael did not answer.

"Sir, should I classify this as a morale operation?"

Kael stared at the tactical display.

"No."

"Psychological warfare?"

"No."

"Then what?"

Kael considered for a long moment.

"Family."

The technician frowned.

"Sir?"

"They're family."

"Yes, sir. We established that."

"No. You don't understand."

Kael pointed toward the House fleet.

"They trust one another enough to insult each other during combat. They compete because they know the others will do their jobs. They can argue over a bottle because the tactical responsibilities have already been assigned."

He looked toward the captured fortress.

"And when the shooting starts, the argument stops."

The technician remained silent.

Kael continued.

"That is not a weakness."

The Garooth invasion fleet collapsed over the following hours.

Its command fortress was seized.

Its forge tenders were destroyed.

Its surviving warships were hunted down or forced to surrender.

House rescue vessels recovered survivors from damaged Compact ships.

Marine formations cleared the remaining occupied installations.

The industrial planet survived.

Kael watched the final battle statistics appear.

The House had suffered significant losses.

Destroyed escorts.

Damaged capital ships.

Hundreds of wounded.

Marines who would never return.

But the expeditionary fleet remained operational.

Its logistics formations were already recovering damaged vessels and preparing for repairs.

The Garooth force no longer existed as an organized military formation.

Kael began dictating his final assessment.

"House von Hjertstedt demonstrates an unusual command culture based upon hereditary familiarity, personal rivalry, informal competition and extremely high professional trust."

The technician recorded every word.

"Senior family members regularly engage in humor, insults and private wagers during major military operations."

He paused.

"These exchanges do not appear to compromise operational discipline. Instead, they reinforce relationships between command elements and establish informal recognition systems alongside the formal chain of command."

Kael looked toward the tactical display.

"Officers remain fully responsive to combat conditions. Informal communications cease immediately when casualties, emergencies or critical decisions require attention."

Another pause.

"Attempts to exploit perceived rivalry between family members should therefore be treated with extreme caution."

He studied the list of House commanders.

Joel.

Anton.

Christer.

Daniel.

Lars.

Dennis Rask.

Then he added the final recommendation.

"Do not mistake their informality for incompetence. Do not assume internal competition indicates disunity. And under no circumstances should an operation rely upon provoking rivalry between the brothers."

The technician looked up.

"Why, sir?"

Kael stared at the wreckage of the Garooth fleet.

"Because the last enemy to give them something to compete over has just lost six hundred warships."

The observation vessel began preparing to withdraw.

Its engines remained cold while the navigation system calculated a route through the outer debris fields.

Kael had almost completed his report when the technician spoke again.

"Sir. One final intercepted transmission."

Kael sighed.

"Play it."

The voice belonged to Lars von Hjertstedt.

"Next time, I'm entering."

Anton answered.

"Now that is a challenge."

Laughter followed.

Kael closed his eyes.

Then another transmission arrived.

This one was not intercepted.

It was addressed directly to the observation vessel.

HOUSE VON HJERTSTEDT — IDENTIFICATION REQUEST

The technician went pale.

"Sir."

Kael stared at the message.

"How long have they known?"

A second message appeared.

UNKNOWN SURVEY VESSEL: YOUR PASSIVE COLLECTION ARRAY HAS BEEN TRACKED SINCE YOUR THIRD COURSE CORRECTION. MAINTAIN YOUR PRESENT VECTOR. YOU ARE OUTSIDE THE ACTIVE COMBAT EXCLUSION ZONE.

The technician whispered.

"They knew."

Kael felt the blood drain from his face.

Then a third message arrived.

HIGH MARSHAL'S OFFICE: YOUR REPORT WILL BE MORE ACCURATE IF YOU NOTE THAT DANIEL WON THE BOTTLE.

For several seconds nobody moved.

Then Kael began laughing.

Not because it was funny.

Because he had just watched a fleet destroy an invasion force while its commanders competed over alcohol.

And those same commanders had apparently found the time to monitor his intelligence operation.

He added one final sentence to his report.

Additional assessment: The House is considerably more dangerous than previously estimated.

Outside, the four dreadnoughts remained above Tarnis.

The observation vessel withdrew without interference.

And somewhere aboard the House fleet, Anton von Hjertstedt was still demanding a recount.

THE END
