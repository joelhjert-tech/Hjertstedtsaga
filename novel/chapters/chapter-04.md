# Chapter 4: The Gate Must Hold

## 1. The Gate Must Hold

Gatehold was dying by procedure.

That was what made it dangerous.

There was no wild panic on Daniel’s tactical display.

No uncontrolled evacuation.

No random reactor failures.

Ardent Gatehold was being dismantled in sequence.

Authentication archives were being erased.

Relay keys were being pulled from live servers.

Navigation libraries were being burned.

One gate-control chamber after another was going dark.

Daniel stood on the bridge of his command cruiser and watched red sectors spread across the station schematic.

“How long until they reach the primary authentication core?”

His operations officer answered:

“Twenty-three minutes.”

Daniel’s eyes narrowed.

“Too fast.”

Another officer looked up.

“High Marshal’s reinforcement group is inbound.”

“Time?”

“Thirty-one minutes.”

Daniel stared at the numbers.

Eight minutes too late.

That was enough to lose the entire route.

Gatehold controlled more than traffic.

It held approach credentials, relay handshakes, emergency lane closures, and the route records needed to move safely through the sector. In this universe, losing a gate library could isolate worlds almost as effectively as destroying the gate itself.

Daniel opened the family channel.

“Joel.”

His brother answered from Central.

“Listening.”

“They are ahead of schedule.”

“How far?”

“Primary core in twenty-three.”

“Reinforcements thirty-one.”

Joel went silent.

Annette joined immediately.

Kira was already calculating.

“Eight-minute deficit confirmed.”

Lucy added:

“Probability Gatehold successfully destroys core before reinforcement arrival: seventy-eight percent.”

Daniel smiled without humour.

“Unacceptable.”

Annette looked toward him.

“You have what locally?”

“One cruiser group, four Vigils, three destroyers, and boarding capability.”

Joel answered before she could.

“Enough.”

Daniel looked at him.

“For what?”

“For eight minutes.”

Anton came over the channel from Bastion.

“That sounds like Daniel’s kind of problem.”

Christer added:

“Usually because Daniel creates eight-minute problems.”

Daniel ignored both.

Joel continued:

“Go.”

Annette raised one finger.

“Controlled entry.”

Daniel’s smile returned.

“Of course.”

Anton laughed.

Nobody believed him.

Gatehold filled the main screen.

A fortress wrapped around a Varanthi gate structure.

Defence rings.

Customs docks.

Military platforms.

Relay towers.

And beneath everything, the ancient gate itself.

Daniel pointed toward the inner command spine.

“That is the target.”

His captain frowned.

“Direct approach crosses two defence belts.”

“Yes.”

“Missile platforms are still active.”

“Yes.”

“Station fleet?”

Daniel looked toward the tactical display.

Ardent escorts were forming close to the gate.

“They want us outside.”

“Exactly.”

Daniel opened fleet command.

“All Vigils forward.”

His tactical officer turned sharply.

“Sir?”

“Make noise.”

Then:

“Destroyers behind them.”

“Cruiser stays dark until their guns commit.”

The formation moved.

Vigil frigates accelerated toward Gatehold.

Ardent platforms reacted immediately.

Targeting beams appeared.

Missile doors opened.

Daniel watched.

“Wait.”

The first Ardent missiles launched.

“Now.”

House Vigils broke formation in every direction.

Decoys flooded space.

Point defence flashed.

The Ardent platforms began tracking the fast targets.

Daniel’s cruiser changed vector.

Straight toward the gap.

His captain looked at him.

“You are taking the command ship through?”

“Yes.”

“That is normally considered poor flagship doctrine.”

Daniel smiled.

“I am not using it as a flagship.”

Another officer understood first.

“You are using it as a boarding transport.”

Daniel nodded.

“Much better.”

Hammer Range collapsed quickly.

Ardent destroyers fired on the Vigils.

House destroyers returned with drive-kill shots.

One Ardent escort lost engines.

Another spun away after losing fire control.

Gatehold’s platforms kept firing.

Then Daniel’s cruiser entered the gap.

“Missile warning!”

“Point defence full!”

The hull shook.

One missile struck the outer armour.

Another detonated against the forward screen.

Daniel remained standing.

“Dock target?”

“Military Collar Twelve.”

“Occupied?”

“Unknown.”

“Good.”

The cruiser burned harder.

Gatehold grew across the screen.

The station’s own guns were now firing almost point-blank.

Knife Range.

Exactly where Daniel wanted to be.

A defence platform tried to rotate toward him.

One House destroyer fired across its weapon mount.

The turret vanished.

Daniel opened the assault channel.

“All boarding teams ready.”

A familiar voice answered.

One of the former Grey Nomad maniacs.

“READY!”

Another shouted:

“CORE SAVE COUNTS?”

Daniel closed his eyes.

“Yes.”

The channel erupted.

“INTELLIGENCE CATEGORY!”

“GATE CORE IS WORTH DOUBLE!”

“WHO DECIDED?”

“ME!”

Daniel cut across them.

“Listen.”

Silence.

Mostly.

“Primary objective is authentication preservation.”

“No explosives near the core.”

“No shooting control technicians unless they resist.”

“Capture engineers alive.”

A marine asked:

“Prize?”

Daniel looked toward the countdown.

Nineteen minutes.

“If you save the core intact, I will personally argue for something from the Vintergard reserve.”

The channel exploded.

Daniel sighed.

He had become part of the problem.

The cruiser struck Collar Twelve.

Magnetic clamps locked.

The docking tube did not extend.

Ardent had disabled it.

Daniel smiled.

“Breaching teams.”

The first charges blew.

Marines flooded across.

Daniel went with them.

His officers protested.

He ignored them.

The corridor beyond was already filled with smoke.

Ardent security fired from barricades.

House marines hit them hard.

Fast.

One team pinned the defenders.

Another went through maintenance.

Daniel moved down the centre with Black Guard specialists.

“Core level?”

“Six decks below.”

“Lift?”

“Disabled.”

“Stairs.”

The marine beside him laughed.

“Traditional.”

They descended.

Above them, Gatehold continued deleting itself.

SECONDARY AUTHENTICATION VAULT — DESTROYED.

MILITARY ROUTE ARCHIVE — PURGE ACTIVE.

Daniel heard every report through his helmet.

“Time?”

“Twelve minutes.”

Too close.

Then Kira entered his feed.

“High Marshal reinforcement group has increased burn.”

Lucy followed:

“Updated arrival: seven minutes.”

Daniel smiled.

Joel had pushed them hard.

Good.

Then another warning appeared.

PRIMARY AUTHENTICATION CORE — MANUAL DESTRUCTION AUTHORIZED.

Daniel stopped.

“By whom?”

His Black Guard officer checked.

“Gatehold Commandant.”

“Location?”

The map flashed.

Same level.

Two corridors away.

Daniel’s eyes turned crimson.

“Change objective.”

The marines around him looked over.

“Commandant first.”

A Grey Nomad veteran shouted:

“CAPTURE CATEGORY!”

Daniel pointed at him.

“Alive.”

“Even better!”

They ran.

At Central, Joel watched the reinforcement group close on Gatehold.

Five minutes.

Then four.

His operations officer looked toward him.

“They are overheating engines.”

Joel nodded.

“Let them.”

“Repair bill will be severe.”

Joel looked toward the gate marker.

“A repair bill is cheaper than losing the road.”

Annette appeared beside him on tactical feed.

“That sounded almost romantic.”

“It is logistics.”

She smiled.

“Exactly.”

Then Daniel’s signal returned.

Gunfire.

Heavy breathing.

Shouting.

“We have reached core level.”

Joel leaned toward the display.

“Status?”

Daniel answered:

“Commandant is trying to destroy it manually.”

“How long?”

A pause.

Then Anton’s voice from Bastion:

“Daniel?”

“Yes?”

“Do not lose the gate.”

Daniel laughed.

“Thank you, Anton.”

Then gunfire erupted again.

The feed cut.

Joel stared at the countdown.

Two minutes until reinforcement arrival.

Gatehold’s core status flashed.

DESTRUCTION SEQUENCE ACTIVE.

Then:

MANUAL ACCESS CONTESTED.

Joel’s reinforcement group emerged into Gatehold’s outer battlespace at maximum burn.

Vigils first.

Destroyers behind.

One Oath cruiser at the centre.

The fast force opened fire immediately—not on the station.

On every ship trying to stop Daniel.

The road was still alive.

Barely.

And somewhere six decks beneath Gatehold’s armour, Daniel was racing the final seconds.

## 2. The Gatehold Breakthrough

Joel’s reinforcement group entered Gatehold’s battlespace at full burn.

The lead Vigil frigates did not slow.

They cut straight through the outer Ardent screen while destroyers followed behind them, firing into exposed drive sections and weapons clusters. The Oath cruiser at the center of the formation came in last, heavy guns already tracking.

Gatehold’s defenders had been focused inward.

On Daniel.

Now they had a second problem.

A very large one.

“Enemy formation turning!” Joel’s tactical officer reported from Central.

Joel watched the live feed.

“Good.”

One Ardent cruiser broke away from Gatehold and tried to intercept the incoming Oath cruiser.

Joel pointed at it.

“Disable.”

The Oath cruiser fired.

Its spinal shot struck the Ardent ship beneath the engines.

The cruiser’s drive flare vanished.

It began tumbling.

Joel gave the next order.

“Open Daniel’s corridor.”

House destroyers surged forward.

Missiles crossed at Knife Range.

A Vigil frigate took a direct hit and lost half its dorsal armour, but stayed between the enemy and the Oath cruiser.

Then the reinforcement force punched through.

Deep inside Gatehold, Daniel heard the distant impacts through the station structure.

House guns.

Reinforcements.

He smiled.

“Joel made it.”

The marine beside him shouted:

“THEN MOVE!”

They were less than three hundred metres from the primary authentication chamber.

Then the walls opened.

Not doors.

Walls.

Hidden armour panels slid apart along both sides of the corridor.

Daniel stopped.

For one second, nothing moved.

Then the first Husk Guard stepped out.

It was huge.

A human body had been rebuilt into something almost twice its original mass. Reinforced legs bent backward beneath an armoured torso. One arm ended in a cutting claw. The other carried a weapon fused into the forearm.

Behind it came another.

Then six more.

And behind them—

experimental soldiers.

Thin human shapes held upright by spinal frames.

Heads partially enclosed in surgical armour.

Tubes disappeared beneath skin.

One still wore an Ardent security uniform beneath the metal grafts.

A Grey Nomad veteran stared.

“Oh.”

Another answered:

“That is disgusting.”

Then the Husk Guard charged.

Daniel raised his weapon.

“CONTACT!”

The corridor exploded.

House marines fired into the first warform.

Rounds struck armour.

It kept coming.

The Husk Guard hit the front line like a vehicle.

One marine went into the wall.

Another lost his shield.

Daniel fired twice into the creature’s knee joint.

“LOW!”

The marines adjusted immediately.

Concentrated fire shattered the reinforced leg.

The creature fell.

It kept crawling.

A second Husk Guard came over it.

Then a third hidden compartment opened behind the House formation.

Daniel heard the warning too late.

“REAR CONTACT!”

Experimental soldiers poured into the corridor behind them.

Daniel swore.

They were trapped between two Seraph formations.

The Grey Nomad teams reacted exactly as expected.

Loudly.

“FRONT IS MINE!”

“THEN I HAVE REAR!”

“THAT IS NOT HOW COMMAND WORKS!”

Daniel shouted:

“IT IS NOW!”

The marines split.

Half turned forward.

Half turned back.

The corridor became a brutal two-direction fight.

Daniel checked the countdown.

PRIMARY CORE DESTRUCTION: FOUR MINUTES.

“Perfect.”

Joel watched Daniel’s telemetry collapse into overlapping hostile markers.

“What happened?”

Kira answered through Annette’s tactical link.

“Previously shielded compartments have opened throughout Gatehold core levels.”

Lucy identified dozens of new contacts.

“Husk Guard signatures confirmed.”

Annette’s face hardened.

“They were stored inside the station.”

Joel looked toward the Oath cruiser approaching Gatehold.

“Special force status?”

His operations officer checked.

“Onboard.”

Joel did not hesitate.

“Send them.”

Annette looked at him.

“The Hunters?”

“Yes.”

The Oath cruiser carried a small anti-Seraph detachment originally intended as Gatehold reserve.

They were no longer reserve.

Joel opened the cruiser’s command channel.

“Oath command.”

“High Marshal.”

“Prepare direct insertion.”

The cruiser captain hesitated.

“Into the station?”

“Into Daniel’s position.”

“We do not have a secure docking collar.”

Joel enlarged Gatehold’s schematic.

“Then do not use one.”

Silence.

Joel marked an emergency maintenance aperture less than a hundred metres from Daniel’s trapped formation.

“Breach there.”

The captain understood.

“That will put the insertion craft inside station defensive range.”

“Yes.”

“Very inside.”

Joel’s crimson eyes brightened.

“Daniel does not have four minutes.”

The response came immediately.

“Understood.”

The Oath cruiser rolled toward Gatehold.

Its heavy batteries fired continuously, suppressing nearby defensive platforms.

Then a small armored insertion craft launched from beneath its hull.

It carried eight Seraph Hunters and sixteen Black Guard specialists.

The craft accelerated directly toward the station.

Gatehold opened fire.

House Vigils crossed the firing lines.

One absorbed a missile meant for the insertion craft.

Another dumped decoys between it and the station.

The craft never slowed.

Inside, Hunter shields locked.

Weapons armed.

The pilot shouted:

“IMPACT IN TEN!”

Nobody answered.

“FIVE!”

The craft hit Gatehold.

Its reinforced nose punched through the maintenance aperture and buried itself halfway into the station.

The front ramp blew outward.

Smoke filled the passage.

The first Seraph Hunter stepped through.

Then the others.

Daniel heard the impact.

Every Husk Guard in the corridor seemed to pause.

A new transmission entered his helmet.

“Special force to Daniel.”

He laughed.

“About time.”

The Hunter commander answered:

“High Marshal sent us.”

Daniel fired into an experimental soldier advancing through smoke.

“I owe him a drink.”

Then the Hunters hit the Husk Guard from the flank.

Great shields slammed into Seraph warforms.

Heavy blades came down.

One Hunter drove an incinerator burst through an opened compartment and forced three experimental soldiers back into the chamber they had emerged from.

The pressure on Daniel’s formation broke instantly.

Not disappeared.

Broke.

That was enough.

Daniel pointed toward the core chamber.

“Grey Nomad teams with me!”

A marine shouted:

“HUNTERS GET THE MONSTERS?”

“Yes!”

“BEST PLAN ALL DAY!”

They ran.

Behind them, the special force held the corridor against the emerging Husk Guard.

Ahead, the authentication chamber door was closing.

Daniel saw the countdown.

ONE MINUTE FORTY-TWO.

He raised his weapon.

“Breach!”

The marines accelerated.

Above them, Joel’s reinforcement fleet had broken Gatehold’s outer defence line.

Outside, House ships now controlled the approach.

Inside, the Hunters were fighting things hidden inside the station walls.

And Daniel was less than two minutes from either saving the road—

or watching an entire sector lose it.

## 3. Dennis Has Arrived

Daniel hit the authentication chamber door at a run.

“Breach!”

The Grey Nomad veterans placed charges.

One marine looked at the countdown above the door.

00:54

“That is unpleasant.”

Daniel raised his weapon.

“Do it.”

The charges fired.

The door folded inward.

Daniel went through first.

Ardent security opened fire immediately.

Rounds struck his shield and armour. House marines poured through behind him, splitting left and right.

At the far end of the chamber stood Gatehold Commandant Oren Vale, one hand pressed against a manual control pedestal.

Behind him, the primary authentication core glowed inside layers of armored crystal and ancient machinery.

And above everything—

the gate status changed.

UNAUTHORIZED ACTIVATION DETECTED.

Daniel stopped.

“No.”

The commandant smiled.

The vast gate outside Gatehold began to wake.

Ancient structures around the aperture lit one after another.

Power surged through the station.

Kira’s voice entered Daniel’s helmet.

“Gate activation is not using standard Ardent authentication.”

Lucy followed.

“Handshake structure contains confirmed Seraph command architecture.”

Daniel stared at the commandant.

“What did you open?”

Vale answered quietly.

“A future.”

Daniel shot him in the leg.

The commandant collapsed screaming.

“Wrong answer.”

Outside Gatehold, space distorted.

Joel watched from Central command.

The gate aperture filled with pale light.

His operations officer shouted:

“Transit field forming!”

“Mass estimate?”

“Unknown. Rising.”

Annette appeared beside the tactical feed.

“Kira?”

“Transit object exceeds cruiser mass.”

Lucy:

“Estimate increasing.”

Joel’s expression hardened.

“Capital?”

“Possible.”

Then another transmission entered the command network.

Not Daniel.

Not Annette.

A deep voice.

Very calm.

“High Marshal.”

Joel froze for half a second.

Annette noticed.

“Who is that?”

Joel looked toward the incoming Hunter telemetry.

A name appeared.

DENNIS RASK.

Joel closed his eyes.

“Oh, no.”

Inside Gatehold, Daniel heard the same voice.

“Daniel.”

He stopped firing.

Slowly.

Very slowly.

He opened the special-force identification.

Then stared at it.

“No.”

A marine beside him glanced over.

“What?”

Daniel looked back toward the corridor where the Seraph Hunters were fighting.

“No, no, no.”

The voice came again.

“I am approaching your position.”

Daniel immediately opened the family channel.

“JOEL!”

Joel answered.

“Yes.”

“You sent Dennis?”

There was a pause.

“That was the available anti-Seraph commander.”

“You sent Dennis Rask onto a station containing an ancient gate?”

“Yes.”

Daniel stared at nothing.

“You absolute idiot.”

Anton immediately joined from Bastion.

“Dennis is there?”

Christer came next.

“You sent Dennis?”

Annette looked between all three feeds.

“I assume there is context.”

Daniel shouted while firing into an Ardent guard position.

“CONTEXT?”

A Grey Nomad veteran ducked beside him.

Daniel continued.

“Dennis does not clear Seraph infestations!”

Joel sighed.

Daniel pointed furiously at his helmet display as though Joel could see the gesture.

“He removes the geographical area containing the infestation!”

Dennis’s voice entered the same channel.

“That is an exaggeration.”

Daniel snapped:

“You destroyed half a refinery moon!”

“It was contaminated.”

“You blew up the refinery!”

“It was also contaminated.”

Anton began laughing.

Christer followed.

Daniel shouted:

“THIS IS NOT FUNNY!”

Dennis answered:

“The moon survived.”

Daniel nearly lost control.

“THAT IS YOUR DEFENCE?”

A massive figure appeared through the smoke behind Daniel’s formation.

Dennis Rask did not look like the other Hunters.

He was broader.

Older armor.

Blackened plates covered in repairs from campaigns Daniel had only heard about. His helmet carried no decoration except a row of old kill-marks and a crimson House seal personally granted decades earlier.

A huge anti-Seraph blade rested across one shoulder.

An incinerator cannon was attached to his other arm.

He walked through the remains of a Husk Guard without looking down.

Dennis had been one of Joel’s closest friends long before Joel became High Marshal.

He had survived campaigns that had killed entire commands.

He had personally dragged Joel out of at least two battles everyone else considered lost.

He was loyal.

Terrifyingly competent.

And completely unreasonable once Seraph contamination was confirmed.

Daniel pointed at him.

“You.”

Dennis stopped.

“Yes?”

“You are not allowed to destroy the station.”

Dennis looked around.

“I had not planned to.”

Daniel stared.

Dennis added:

“Yet.”

Daniel opened the family channel again.

“JOEL!”

Joel was openly laughing now.

“I heard him.”

“I hate you.”

“Save the core.”

“I am trying!”

The countdown hit:

00:19

Daniel turned.

Commandant Vale was crawling toward the destruction pedestal.

One Grey Nomad marine kicked him away.

“Not today.”

Daniel reached the console.

“Stop destruction!”

A technician shouted:

“Manual authority is locked!”

Dennis stepped beside him.

“Move.”

Daniel looked at the enormous anti-Seraph weapon.

“No.”

Dennis tilted his helmet.

“No?”

“You are not solving an authentication problem with explosives.”

“I was going to cut the power feed.”

“That is almost worse.”

Kira suddenly interrupted.

“Alternative available. Separate destruction command from gate activation.”

Daniel worked the controls.

“Do it.”

00:08

Lucy began injecting override sequences.

00:05

The gate aperture outside reached full illumination.

Something enormous became visible inside the transit distortion.

Long.

Black-gold.

Seraph.

00:03

Daniel slammed the final authorization.

PRIMARY CORE DESTRUCTION ABORTED.

He exhaled.

Then the entire station shook.

Not from an explosion.

From transit.

Outside Gatehold, the nose of a massive Seraph warship began emerging through the ancient gate.

Daniel slowly looked at Dennis.

Dennis looked at the tactical projection.

For the first time, Daniel heard amusement in his voice.

“Well.”

Daniel pointed a finger directly at him.

“No.”

“I have not said anything.”

“You are thinking about destroying the gate.”

Dennis considered.

“Yes.”

Daniel screamed into the family channel:

“JOEL, GET YOUR FRIEND AWAY FROM MY GATE!”

Joel answered immediately.

“Dennis.”

“Yes?”

“Do not destroy Daniel’s station.”

Dennis looked toward the Seraph capital ship forcing its way through the gate.

“Can I destroy what is coming through it?”

Joel smiled.

“That is why I sent you.”

Dennis raised his blade.

“Good.”

Daniel muttered:

“I still hate all of you.”

Outside, the Seraph capital continued emerging.

And Gatehold’s defenders began turning every surviving weapon toward it.

## 4. The Gate Is Not Alone

The first Seraph capital was still only halfway through the gate when Kira spoke again.

Her voice cut across Daniel’s chamber feed, Joel’s command bridge, Annette’s war room, and the surviving Gatehold tactical network.

“Additional transit handshakes detected.”

Daniel looked up from the authentication console.

“No.”

Lucy followed immediately.

“Correction: multiple additional transit handshakes.”

The chamber went still.

Even the Grey Nomad maniacs stopped shouting for half a second.

Daniel pulled the gate queue open.

One hostile command architecture sat behind the emerging capital ship.

Then another.

Then three more.

Different mass signatures.

Cruiser scale.

Boarding carrier scale.

One was larger than all the others.

He stared at the list.

“They stacked a convoy.”

Outside Gatehold, the pale transit field kept burning. The nose of the first Seraph capital pushed farther into normal space while ancient structures around the gate screamed under strain.

Joel saw the same queue from Central and swore quietly.

Annette’s expression became colder.

“How many?”

Kira answered:

“Five confirmed queued handshakes behind the leading capital. Probability of additional masked signals remains high.”

Anton’s voice entered from Bastion.

“That seems rude.”

Christer answered dryly:

“They are invading.”

Dennis Rask looked at the tactical image through his dark visor.

“Then we should be impolite back.”

Daniel pointed at him.

“No destruction.”

“I heard you the first time.”

“You were thinking about it again.”

“Yes.”

Then Dennis turned his helmet slightly toward the right-side corridor wall.

A Grey Nomad veteran blinked.

“What?”

Dennis raised his incineration cannon.

Daniel’s eyes widened.

“Dennis, do not—”

The cannon fired.

White-orange flame punched through the wall in a blinding sheet.

The corridor beyond vanished in fire.

A second later the hidden compartment behind that wall detonated outward. Husk Guards stumbled through the breach already burning, black fluid boiling out of their armour seams. Behind them, something taller reeled into view.

A Seraph commander.

Not a human warform.

A real one.

Tall, thin, draped in black-gold command armour with a human-derived face stretched too elegantly across an inhuman skull. Neural filaments hung behind its neck like a ceremonial veil.

The incineration blast had caught it full in the chest.

It tried to speak.

Then Dennis stepped forward and put a second burst through it.

The creature collapsed in flame.

Silence hit the chamber.

One Grey Nomad marine finally whispered:

“He shot the wall.”

Dennis lowered the cannon.

“There was a commander behind it.”

Daniel stared at the dead Seraph and the burning hidden compartment.

“How did you know?”

Dennis tilted his helmet.

“They hide like officers.”

That was not an answer.

It was somehow worse.

Kira confirmed the kill.

“Hidden compartment breached. Four Husk Guards eliminated. One Seraph command organism terminated.”

Anton immediately laughed over the family channel.

“I like him.”

Daniel answered without looking away from the corpse.

“I do not.”

Outside Gatehold, the battle expanded.

Joel’s fast reinforcement group had already broken the local Ardent screen, but now every surviving sensor cluster in the system was turning toward the gate.

Because the gate was not the only problem anymore.

New contacts entered the outer battlespace from the spinward approach.

Transponders bright.

Weapons hot.

Two human fleets.

One identified itself in blunt, iron-coded declaration.

IMPERIUM OF MAN RELIEF GROUP SIGMA.

The other came under colder, cleaner Zenith signatures.

ZENITH IMPERIUM EXPEDITIONARY SPEAR.

Mara, still assisting at Central command, looked toward Joel.

“They came fast.”

Joel nodded.

“Too fast.”

Annette already understood.

“They were waiting nearby.”

Not for altruism.

Not exactly.

The major powers had been circling Ardent, hoping to gain position once House von Hjertstedt broke the state open. Now two of them had committed military forces at once.

The Iron-bound Imperium group entered first: brutal-looking battleships, escort destroyers, and dense macro-battery cruisers built for direct punishment. Behind them the Zenith formation arrived with cleaner lines, disciplined arrowhead geometry, and bright sensor lattices built for command warfare and long-range strike control.

For three seconds, it looked as though Gatehold might suddenly have help.

Then hidden Seraph ships attacked them.

Black-gold signatures burst from cold shadow above the gate plane and below the ecliptic at the same time. They did not announce themselves. They simply opened fire.

The leading Imperium cruiser took a lance strike across the bow before it could finish its challenge code. Armour blew outward in molten sheets.

A Zenith escort vanished under a missile bloom less than two breaths later.

Joel watched the whole thing unfold.

“They went for the newcomers immediately.”

Annette nodded.

“Because those fleets still arrived in formation.”

The Seraphs did not want organized human reinforcement reaching Gatehold.

On the external channel, the Imperium commander bellowed in fury.

“UNKNOWN HOSTILES IDENTIFIED. RETURN FIRE. FORWARD LINE ADVANCE.”

A Zenith voice came a moment later, cold and furious.

“Combat authority accepted. All batteries engage Seraph contacts.”

Just like that, Gatehold became larger than House versus Ardent.

Human fleets were now entering the kill zone and being blooded on arrival.

Back inside the core chamber, Daniel dragged the commandant away from the console while Dennis and the Hunters sealed the burning hidden compartment.

“Report,” Joel demanded over command.

Daniel checked the gate queue again.

“Five additional hostile transit handshakes confirmed. First capital still emerging. Outer system now contains Imperium and Zenith reinforcements under Seraph attack.”

Anton whistled.

“So everyone gets a war.”

Annette looked toward the tactical spread.

“Joel, Gatehold has become the main axis.”

“Yes.”

“Bastion remains critical, but if Gatehold falls, Ardent becomes an open door.”

Daniel stared at the queue.

“And if that large mass is what I think it is, this door gets much worse.”

Dennis stepped beside him.

“Can the gate be interrupted without destroying it?”

Daniel looked at him suspiciously.

“Why?”

“So I know what I am forbidden from doing.”

Daniel opened the family channel.

“Joel, I am insulting you again.”

Joel almost smiled.

“Noted.”

The station shook as the Seraph capital forced another kilometer of its hull into real space.

Five more enemy handshakes waited behind it.

Outside, the Imperium of Man and Zenith warfleets were already exchanging fire with Seraph ambushers.

Inside, Dennis Rask had just burned a hidden Seraph commander out of a wall.

Gatehold was no longer a battle to save a route.

It was becoming the first true front of a much larger war.

## 5. Remove the Limiters

Joel watched Gatehold become the center of the war.

Seraph ships were attacking the newly arrived Imperium and Zenith fleets.

A Seraph capital ship was still forcing itself through the gate.

Five more transit handshakes waited behind it.

Daniel was fighting inside the station with Dennis.

That was enough.

Joel opened the family command channel.

“Anton. Christer.”

Both answered almost immediately.

Anton was still aboard the first captured Ardent capital.

Christer was overseeing the surrendered second capital while House ships exchanged fire with the growing Seraph force around Bastion.

Joel did not waste time.

“Finish Bastion.”

Anton smiled.

“How immediately?”

Joel’s crimson eyes brightened.

“Immediately.”

Christer understood first.

“You want both fleets at Gatehold.”

“Yes.”

“Bastion still has active Seraph ships.”

“Then break them.”
