# Chapter 3: The Excision of Ardent

## 1. Beneath Special Custody

Once the receiving corridor was secure, Joel transferred from the Resolute Dawn to Special Custody with Mara and Holt. Soren followed under guard. Their shuttle crossed a dock still crowded with security teams surrendering their weapons; by the time they reached Daniel in the archive chamber, more of the sealed files were opening.

The first Shepherd files were bad enough.

Prisoner manifests.

Transfer orders.

Genetic screening tags.

Seraph custody marks.

Medical notes written in cold administrative language beside human names.

Captain Mara Vale stood beside Daniel as lines of archive data rolled across the display.

“Prime host stock.”

“Functional labor.”

“Cultural utility.”

“Resistance disposal.”

Holt read over her shoulder and looked sick.

Marek Soren said nothing at all.

Joel stood at the center of the chamber, silent and still, crimson eyes faint in the reflected light of the archive screens.

Then Daniel stopped.

“There is more.”

Everyone looked at him.

“What?” Joel asked.

Daniel expanded a buried maintenance layer hidden under Special Custody.

A new structure appeared beneath Ardent’s official station grid.

Three levels.

No public listing.

No judicial registry.

No civilian utility map.

The files labeled them only as:

SUBLEVEL BLACK

SUBLEVEL CHAPEL

SUBLEVEL SHEPHERD

Anton stared at the display.

“Well.”

Christer folded his arms.

“That is rarely good.”

Daniel opened the access logs.

“Private lift only. Restricted to Kael’s office, archive command, and an isolated cargo spine.”

Soren leaned closer.

“I never knew about those levels.”

Joel looked at him.

“I believe you.”

Mara turned toward Director Lysandra Kael, who stood restrained under House guard.

“What are they?”

Kael said nothing.

Anton smiled without warmth.

“That was your chance.”

Joel stepped closer to her.

“Answer.”

Kael looked at him, then at the archive display, then finally down.

“Research.”

No one spoke.

Holt was first.

“Research on what?”

Kael’s voice was dry.

“Selection.”

Daniel opened another folder.

His jaw tightened.

“Human compatibility studies.”

Another.

“Host survivability.”

Another.

“Memory retention.”

Mara stared.

“Memory retention?”

Daniel read from the file.

“They were trying to measure how much of the original person could remain functional after Seraph implantation.”

Silence settled over the room like a weight.

That was worse than simple murder.

Joel’s expression became harder.

“Open the lifts.”

Kael looked up sharply.

“No.”

Anton laughed once.

“That sounded like a request to her.”

Daniel was already inside the control grid.

“It was not.”

The private lift locks broke.

Security doors opened.

A maintenance map appeared.

Three red levels beneath the station.

Ardent had been running a hidden laboratory under its own courts.

Joel turned to Mara, Holt, Christer and Anton.

“With me.”

He looked toward Soren.

“You stay under guard.”

Soren nodded once.

For the first time, he looked relieved not to be included.

The lift descended too far for normal station architecture.

No music.

No announcements.

No public lighting.

Only emergency strips and the hum of isolated power.

Anton checked his weapon.

“Who designs a legal station with secret torture floors?”

Holt answered quietly.

“People who stop believing law applies to them.”

The lift doors opened onto Sublevel Black.

The corridor beyond was white.

Sterile.

Cold.

Medical, not judicial.

Rows of sealed rooms lined both sides.

Most were empty.

Some were not.

The first occupied chamber held surgical frames and restraints.

The second held a row of suspended tanks.

Three were shattered.

Two still contained bodies.

Human.

Thin.

Scarred.

Skin marked by repeated invasive procedures.

One still had part of a medical label attached to the glass.

SUBJECT 4412 — CONVERSION FAILURE

Mara stopped moving.

Anton’s smile had vanished completely.

Daniel moved to a side console.

“These are not just holding cells.”

“No,” Joel said.

“They are laboratories.”

They advanced deeper.

The horrors became more specific.

Rooms full of bloodwork and tissue records.

Compatibility charts.

Neural scans.

A chamber where failed host tissue had been preserved for reference.

A restraint room with a speaker system designed to replay familiar voices into sealed cells.

Another with rows of implants and surgical instruments arranged like sacred tools.

Then they reached Sublevel Chapel.

The name fit badly.

It was not holy.

It was ceremonial.

Seraph symbols had been cut into the metal walls beneath removable House-neutral panels.

Here the experiments changed.

Not simply survival.

Control.

Voice imitation.

Behavior pattern retention.

Command phrase testing.

There was a chamber marked:

RETURNED SUBJECT STABILITY REVIEW

Holt read it and looked toward Joel.

“They sent some of them back.”

“Yes,” Joel said.

“That was always the worst possibility.”

Christer checked the next passage.

“Movement ahead.”

Everyone raised weapons.

A locked bulkhead stood at the end of the corridor.

Behind it, faint impacts sounded from the far side.

Not machine noise.

Someone striking metal.

Joel nodded.

Anton placed the breaching charge.

The door blew inward.

Beyond it lay Sublevel Shepherd.

This level was different from the others.

Not active laboratories.

Storage.

Rows of sealed cryo-vaults stretched into the dimness.

Some were empty.

Some were broken.

Some still glowed with low-power status lights.

Daniel moved to the nearest terminal.

“Power reserve is failing. Many pods are dead.”

He pulled up the occupant registry.

His face changed.

“What?”

Daniel turned the screen toward Joel.

The names were old.

Too old.

Missing House personnel.

Agents.

Captured officers.

Names marked dead in House records decades earlier.

Mara saw Joel go still.

Anton stepped closer.

Then swore quietly.

Christer stared at the list.

“No.”

Daniel scrolled again.

The file flagged a special category.

ANOMALOUS STOCK — HJERTSTEDT LINE

That was bad enough.

Then another cluster of names appeared under restricted preservation orders.

Not soldiers.

Not officers.

Elders.

Ancient House names.

People from old campaigns.

Figures recorded as lost, executed, or dead in transit long ago.

Joel reached for the nearest pod as if he did not trust his eyes.

Inside lay an old man with a pale face, long white hair, and the hard features of House blood.

Scars crossed his throat and scalp.

His chest still rose.

Slowly.

A faded data plate on the pod read:

Hakon von Hjertstedt

Status: Host Rejection / Retained for Study

Anton stepped back.

“Hakon?”

Mara looked between them.

“You know him?”

Christer answered, voice flat.

“He died forty-three years ago.”

Joel stared through the frost on the pod glass.

“No.”

He looked at the rest of the row.

“House was told he died forty-three years ago.”

Daniel opened another pod record.

Then another.

Three more names.

All House elders.

All believed dead.

All preserved.

All marked as failed Seraph conversion subjects.

Mara finally understood.

“The blood.”

Joel looked at her.

She saw the warning in his eyes.

Enough.

No more.

The secret remained the House’s.

But the evidence was clear enough.

The Seraphs had tried to take House blood.

Failed.

And kept the survivors alive for study.

Kael had not just sold prisoners.

She had fed ancient enemies one of the most protected bloodlines in human space.

Behind them, one of the older pods alarmed.

Power failure.

Daniel moved fast.

“This one is losing support.”

Anton was already at the manual release.

Joel checked the registry.

Name:

Elder Rurik von Hjertstedt

Status:

Viable

Christer looked at Joel.

“What now?”

Joel’s face was stone.

“We wake them.”

Behind him, in the darkness of Sublevel Shepherd, more dormant pods waited.

Some bore House names.

Some belonged to other factions.

Some had no names at all.

And on the final sealed vault door at the far end of the chamber, a red warning burned through centuries of dust:

SHEPHERD PRIORITY VAULT — AUTHORIZED FLESH MINISTRY ACCESS ONLY

Anton looked at it and smiled like a wolf.

“Now it gets interesting.”

## 2. The Scale of Ardent

The House-only signal left Ardent under maximum encryption.

Joel stood beside the cryo-vault containing Hakon von Hjertstedt.

His crimson eyes stayed fixed on the old man's face.

Forty-three years declared dead.

Forty-three years inside a Shepherd laboratory.

Around them, House medics connected emergency support to the surviving elders.

Anton stood near the sealed Priority Vault.

Christer and Daniel watched the archive feeds.

Nobody joked now.

Joel opened the command channel.

“Crimson emergency.”

The reply came immediately.

“Authentication confirmed.”

“Seraph involvement verified,” Joel said. “Living House blood recovered from experimental custody. Multiple elders previously declared dead.”

Silence followed.

Then the House officer answered differently.

“Repeat.”

Joel did.

This time his voice was harder.

“Seraph research. House blood. Living elders.”

A pause.

“Understood.”

Joel continued.

“Notify Justicar High Command. Medical command. Senior family authority.”

Anton looked toward him.

Joel added:

“And begin Excision readiness.”

That changed everything.

Daniel suddenly turned from the archive terminal.

“Joel.”

“What?”

“Ardent is larger than we thought.”

He expanded a recovered strategic file.

The station around them appeared first.

ARDENT JUDICIAL RELAY — CENTRAL.

Then two more major installations appeared.

One around the neighbouring gas giant.

Another guarding the system’s primary gate complex.

Mara stared at the display.

“Three stations?”

Daniel nodded.

“Three major stations.”

Soren looked over his shoulder.

“Ardent Central. Ardent Bastion. Ardent Gatehold.”

Holt frowned.

“I thought Gatehold was only customs.”

Soren gave him a tired look.

“That is what Ardent tells outsiders.”

Daniel opened another layer.

Planets appeared.

Several inhabited.

One agricultural world.

One mining world.

A small industrial planet with large orbital yards.

Another colony listed as a restricted administrative territory.

Mara slowly turned toward Joel.

“This is not a station government.”

“No,” Joel said.

“It is a small state.”

Daniel opened the military ledger.

Then stopped.

Christer noticed.

“What?”

Daniel enlarged the fleet count.

Everyone went quiet.

Ardent's declared warfleet should have been modest.

Patrol frigates.

Customs ships.

A few destroyers.

Perhaps several cruisers protecting the relay network.

Instead the hidden ledger showed almost three times the expected combat strength.

Reserve ships.

Unregistered escorts.

Assault transports.

Missile barges.

Several heavy cruisers.

At least two capital hulls operating under false commercial registries.

Anton stared at the numbers.

“Well.”

Christer folded his arms.

“That explains the money.”

Mara looked toward Soren.

“You knew?”

“Not the full count.”

“You were intelligence director.”

“Ardent was very careful.”

Joel studied the fleet structure.

This was not random corruption.

Someone had been building military power.

Quietly.

For years.

Holt looked toward the Shepherd archive.

“With prisoner money?”

Daniel shook his head.

“Not enough.”

Joel pointed toward several transfers.

“External funding.”

Soren looked pale.

“Seraph.”

Nobody answered.

They did not need to.

The Seraph Throne was known for building influence before exposing fleets. It preferred command seizure, host capture, and control of infrastructure over simple destruction.

Ardent had become useful.

Judicial authority.

Three major stations.

Planets.

Shipyards.

A hidden warfleet.

And Shepherd access to thousands of people.

Anton looked toward Joel.

“This is no longer an investigation.”

“No.”

“A raid?”

“No.”

Christer already understood.

“Campaign.”

Joel nodded.

House von Hjertstedt normally avoided conquest. Its Charter favored protection, limited intervention, surrender, capture, and bounded war.

Seraph involvement was one of the exceptions.

Joel opened another encrypted channel.

“Nearest Banner commands.”

Several acknowledgements appeared.

“High Marshal.”

“Listening.”

“Ready.”

Joel looked toward the map of Ardent territory.

“New operational assumption.”

He highlighted all three stations.

Then the planets.

Then the hidden fleet.

“Ardent is considered strategically compromised until proven otherwise.”

Mara looked at him.

“You are claiming the whole state?”

Joel shook his head.

“Not yet.”

Anton smiled without humour.

“That means probably.”

Joel continued.

“Seal outbound military traffic.”

“Do not interfere with civilian evacuation.”

“Sanctuary groups prepare quarantine capacity.”

“Vigil groups move toward all known gates.”

“Justicars begin legal review of Ardent authority.”

Christer stepped closer.

“And their fleet?”

Joel looked at the hidden force numbers.

“Observe.”

Anton frowned.

“Only observe?”

“Until they choose.”

Daniel nodded.

“If they surrender their records and submit to inspection, we do not need a war.”

“And if they refuse?” Mara asked.

Joel looked toward the cryo-pods.

Toward Hakon.

Toward the elders the House had mourned decades earlier.

His eyes turned fully crimson.

“Then Ardent stops being independent.”

Nobody spoke.

Joel opened the House-wide command circuit.

“This is High Marshal Joel von Hjertstedt.”

Every active House command listening went silent.

“Confirmed Seraph involvement inside Ardent territory.”

“Project Shepherd is active.”

“House blood has been used in conversion research.”

The words were enough.

Across distant ships, officers would already be changing course.

Joel continued.

“Ardent controls three major stations, several inhabited worlds, and a concealed warfleet roughly three times its declared strength.”

Anton smiled.

The kind of smile he wore before battles.

Joel finished:

“Prepare for Excision Campaign authority.”

Outside the station, the first Ardent warships began changing formation.

Their drives lit.

Weapons systems woke.

On Joel’s display, the changing formation gave him no answer yet. He kept the inspection offer open and watched their weapons.

## 3. The Primarch Approves

The message reached Vintergard under Crimson priority.

It did not go through ordinary fleet command.

It passed the Winter Road relay network, crossed the Shield around Hjarta, and reached the restricted command chambers beneath Vinterkrona.

Primarch Lars von Hjertstedt read it once.

Then again.

Around him, the old strategic chamber remained silent.

PROJECT SHEPHERD CONFIRMED.

SERAPH INVOLVEMENT VERIFIED.

MULTIPLE HOUSE ELDERS RECOVERED ALIVE.

HJERTSTEDT BLOOD USED IN HOST RESEARCH.

Lars stopped at that line.

His eyes turned crimson.

The officers around the table noticed.

Nobody spoke.

Lars was Joel and Anton's father.

He had commanded House fleets before either son had been trusted with a pistol, survived campaigns now taught as doctrine, and watched too many names enter the family memorial halls.

Several of those names had just been found breathing inside Seraph cryogenic vaults.

“Hakon?” Lars asked.

A medical officer answered carefully.

“Alive.”

Lars looked up.

“Confirmed?”

“Three independent scans. House genetic verification. He remains under suspended revival until Joel authorizes full extraction.”

Lars's jaw tightened.

“Hakon died before Anton had command authority.”

“Yes, Primarch.”

“He did not die.”

“No.”

Lars looked back toward the Ardent strategic map.

Three stations.

Several inhabited worlds.

Hidden yards.

A warfleet almost three times the strength Ardent publicly admitted.

And beneath all of it—

Project Shepherd.

Lars placed both hands on the table.

“Joel requested Excision readiness.”

“Yes.”

“Approved.”

Nobody moved.

The senior Justicar beside him leaned forward.

“Full authority?”

Lars looked at him.

“Seraph custody facilities. House blood experimentation. Living elders stolen from our dead rolls.”

His voice stayed calm.

That made it worse.

“If Ardent cannot prove command integrity, then yes.”

The Justicar nodded.

“Excision authority will be prepared.”

“Not announced.”

“Understood.”

Lars pointed toward the Ardent systems.

“Joel still gives them the chance to surrender.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

A faint smile appeared.

“He remembers what I taught him.”

Then Lars looked toward another display.

Sol.

The neutral heart of human diplomacy.

The system where rival human powers could meet without bringing fleets into the same room.

“Prepare my ship.”

One officer hesitated.

“Destination?”

“Sol.”

That earned several looks.

Lars noticed.

“If this becomes an Excision Campaign, every major human power will hear about it within hours.”

He pointed toward Ardent.

“A judicial state with three stations and a hidden fleet does not disappear quietly.”

The Justicar understood.

“You want recognition before the war expands.”

“I want witnesses.”

Lars travelled aboard a House command cruiser rather than a full battle formation.

Even so, Sol Control noticed the ship immediately.

The vessel emerged beyond Neptune under full legal authentication and transmitted diplomatic status before advancing.

No fleet followed.

That was deliberate.

Sol was neutral ground.

House von Hjertstedt respected that neutrality.

Lars stood on the bridge in full Primarch uniform.

Black.

Deep crimson.

Old campaign markings at the collar.

No unnecessary decoration.

His officers stood behind him.

“Sol Control confirms diplomatic corridor.”

“Escort?”

“Terran neutral-security frigates.”

Lars nodded.

“Accept.”

The cruiser moved inward.

Neptune fell behind.

Then Saturn.

Jupiter's defensive bastions appeared across the tactical screens.

Earth remained distant.

Blue.

Small.

Important.

Lars watched it without speaking.

The emergency briefing was called on Luna.

Not Earth.

Too many civilians.

Too much symbolism.

Luna's Concordat chambers were built for exactly this kind of crisis.

Terran representatives arrived first.

Then observers from the Throne Imperium.

The Hiigaran Restoration sent a gate-security delegation.

Several smaller human states joined remotely.

Nobody had yet been told everything.

Lars entered last.

Conversation stopped.

The Terran chairwoman stood.

“Primarch von Hjertstedt.”

“Chairwoman.”

“You requested emergency human-security status.”

“Yes.”

“For Seraph involvement?”

Lars looked around the chamber.

Imperial uniforms.

Hiigaran lineage officers.

Terran security.

Neutral judges.

Everyone knew what the word Seraph meant.

Nobody needed a history lesson.

“Yes.”

The room became much quieter.

Lars activated the central display.

Ardent appeared.

“Ardent Judicial Authority operates three major stations, several settled worlds, and a warfleet approximately three times its declared legal strength.”

The Imperial representative frowned.

“That alone is not grounds for intervention.”

“No.”

Lars changed the display.

Project Shepherd appeared.

Prisoner transfers.

False warrants.

Missing detainees.

Genetic classifications.

Then Seraph custody markings.

Nobody interrupted now.

Lars continued.

“Ardent officials have transferred human prisoners into Seraph hands.”

The Terran chairwoman looked sick.

“Knowingly?”

“At senior levels, yes.”

Another file appeared.

Conversion experiments.

Host-retention studies.

Failed implantation records.

Lars did not show everything.

Some horrors did not belong on a diplomatic screen.

Then he displayed the cryogenic vault.

House names appeared.

Names recorded dead decades earlier.

The Hiigaran delegate leaned forward.

“They kept them alive?”

“For research.”

Lars's voice hardened.

“My son found living members of my family beneath Ardent Special Custody.”

The Throne Imperial representative finally understood why Lars himself had come.

“And High Marshal Joel von Hjertstedt?”

“Currently inside Ardent territory.”

“Anton?”

“With him.”

Lars almost smiled.

“Which means Ardent's situation is already deteriorating.”

A few people around the chamber understood that remark differently than others.

Lars changed the display again.

Three Ardent stations.

The hidden fleet.

House banners beginning to reposition outside the sector.

“This is the decision before us.”

The room waited.

“If Ardent submits to inspection, releases prisoners, opens its records and purges Seraph influence, the House will support restoration of lawful authority.”

Then the red marks appeared.

Gatehold.

Bastion.

Central.

“If they resist…”

Lars looked around the chamber.

“…House von Hjertstedt will declare an Excision Campaign.”

The Terran chairwoman asked quietly:

“And seize Ardent territory?”

“Yes.”

The answer came without hesitation.

“Until the Seraph presence is dead.”

The Imperial delegate stiffened.

“You are openly claiming the right of conquest.”

Lars looked at him.

“Against the Seraph?”

Silence.

That question had an old answer.

Every major human power had burned fallen systems before.

Every one of them carried records they preferred not to discuss.

Lars continued.

“The House normally leaves territory when the contract ends.”

He looked toward Ardent.

“This is not a normal contract.”

The tactical display showed Joel's latest signal.

ARDENT WARFLEET MOBILIZING.

Lars read it.

Then smiled.

Coldly.

“Apparently they are making the decision for us.”

## 4. The Rose of Judgment

The secure channel opened from Luna.

Joel stood inside Ardent Special Custody Command.

Anton leaned against the tactical table beside him.

Christer and Daniel were still below with the recovered elders and the Shepherd archives.

The image stabilized.

Primarch Lars von Hjertstedt appeared first.

Joel straightened.

“Father.”

Anton smiled.

“You still look angry.”

“I am.”

“That is reassuring.”

Lars ignored him.

Then someone stepped into view beside the Primarch.

Joel’s expression changed immediately.

Annette von Hjertstedt wore the black and crimson uniform of the Head Justicar.

Joel’s wife.

Christer and Daniel’s sister.

Fleet strategist.

And one of the few people inside House von Hjertstedt who could make senior officers go quiet simply by entering a room.

Her long peach-coloured hair fell around her shoulders and down her back.

Most of the time, it hid the two small horns at the sides of her head.

Those horns were not ornamental.

Inside them lived two advanced AI systems.

Kira.

Lucy.

But what drew everyone’s attention was not the horns.

It was the flower in Annette’s hair.

A small pink rose pin sat above her left temple.

Elegant.

Delicate.

Harmless-looking.

Inside the House, almost nobody saw it that way.

The rose was the Justicar Shield.

When Annette wore it openly, it meant she was not merely attending as family.

She was acting under full judicial authority.

Evidence would be preserved.

Commands would be examined.

Corruption would be dragged into the light.

And rank would not protect anyone.

Anton saw it.

His smile weakened.

“Oh.”

Joel looked at him.

“Yes.”

“You noticed.”

“Yes.”

“That is unfortunate.”

Annette heard him.

“Good evening, Anton.”

Anton straightened slightly.

“Head Justicar.”

Joel almost laughed.

Annette looked toward him.

“And you.”

Joel’s voice softened.

“Annette.”

“You found Seraph laboratories without me.”

“I did not arrange them.”

“That is not an excuse.”

Lars looked between them.

“Can the family discussion wait until after the war?”

Annette smiled.

“Of course, Primarch.”

She stepped toward the Luna tactical table.

Ardent appeared between them.

Three major stations.

Several settled worlds.

Hidden yards.

A warfleet almost three times its declared size.

Joel’s present force remained concentrated near Central.

Ardent ships were moving.

Annette reached up and moved part of her peach hair behind one ear.

The first horn became visible.

A thin line of light ran through it.

A cool female voice entered the channel.

“Kira active.”

Annette moved the hair on the opposite side.

The second horn appeared.

Another voice followed.

Warmer.

“Lucy active. Strategic model synchronized.”

Anton smiled.

“Hello, girls.”

Lucy replied:

“Hello, Anton. Your probability of making this operation more difficult than necessary remains high.”

Daniel’s voice came over the lower-level channel.

“Accurate.”

Anton frowned.

“This family has no loyalty.”

Annette ignored them.

Kira reorganized the Ardent map.

Fleet contacts separated.

Central Defence Fleet.

Gatehold Fleet.

Bastion Reserve.

Then more contacts appeared.

Commercial hulls.

Customs ships.

Cargo vessels.

Several changed classification.

Hidden warships.

Mara stepped closer to Joel’s display.

“How did she identify those?”

Annette touched the first horn.

“Kira tracks military logistics.”

Kira answered:

“Fuel consumption exceeds Ardent’s declared military requirement by 214 percent.”

Lucy added:

“They hid hulls. They failed to hide what the hulls consume.”

Joel smiled faintly.

“That sounds like you.”

Annette looked at him.

“It sounds like logistics.”

Lars pointed toward the three major stations.

“Joel.”

“Yes?”

“You are not attacking all three with the force currently at Central.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

Lars continued.

“You hold Central.”

“Secure the elders.”

“Preserve Project Shepherd evidence.”

“Prevent military traffic from leaving with Seraph material, prisoners, or archive cores.”

Joel nodded.

“And if they attack?”

Lars smiled.

“Then stop asking questions you already know the answer to.”

Anton laughed.

There was the Primarch Joel remembered.

Annette leaned closer to the display.

Her expression changed.

Less wife.

More Head Justicar.

“Joel.”

He looked toward her.

“Seraph involvement means we assume the entire Ardent command chain may contain compromised elements.”

“Yes.”

“That does not mean we declare the entire population infected.”

“Agreed.”

“We separate negligence, corruption, collaboration, and conversion.”

Joel nodded.

“Agreed.”

“People who took money face trial.”

“Yes.”

“People forced into cooperation are investigated.”

“Yes.”

“Confirmed Seraph hosts go into quarantine.”

Joel’s expression hardened.

“And active Seraph facilities?”

Annette touched the pink rose pin.

The tiny movement was enough to make Anton stop smiling again.

“Destroyed.”

The word was quiet.

Final.

Mara looked from Annette to Joel.

She understood now why the flower mattered.

Annette continued.

“The Rose is open.”

Joel stared at her.

“Full Justicar authority?”

“Yes.”

Even Lars looked toward her.

That meant every House unit involved would preserve records.

Every prisoner transfer.

Every shot.

Every warrant.

Every Ardent official who tried to destroy evidence would become part of the case.

The pink rose in Annette’s peach hair had just become more dangerous than some battleships.

Anton muttered:

“Someone in Ardent is going to have a very bad day.”

Lucy replied:

“Statistically, several thousand people.”

“Thank you, Lucy.”

“You are welcome.”

Annette changed the tactical display.

“Joel, I want Project Shepherd intact.”

Anton frowned.

“I thought we were destroying it.”

“The organization, yes.”

She pointed toward the files.

“The evidence, no.”

Her voice became colder.

“I want every false warrant.”

“Every payment.”

“Every prisoner.”

“Every transfer route.”

“Every official who signed.”

“Every intermediary who profited.”

“Every Seraph contact.”

“We follow the chain until it stops.”

Joel watched her.

“That could cross several states.”

“I know.”

“Possibly major factions.”

“I know.”

Lars spoke then.

“If it reaches a major faction, evidence comes to Sol before escalation.”

Annette nodded.

“The House does not start a human war on suspicion.”

Joel looked toward the Ardent fleet.

“No.”

Anton smiled faintly.

“But if the evidence is real?”

The room became still.

Lars looked directly at him.

“Then we deal with reality.”

An alarm sounded in Ardent Command.

Mara turned.

“High Marshal. Central Defence Fleet is accelerating.”

Kira answered at once:

“Weapons-range transition projected.”

Lucy followed:

“Probability of hostile opening fire now exceeds seventy-two percent.”

Annette moved another lock of peach hair aside.

Both horns were visible now.

The pink rose remained between them.

Beautiful.

Terrible.

“Joel.”

“Yes?”

“Try not to destroy everything before I arrive.”

Anton laughed.

Joel looked toward the tactical display.

Ardent warships were moving into Hammer Range.

His crimson eyes brightened.

“No promises.”

## 5. Three Blades

Annette reached Ardent before the fleets opened fire.

Her command cruiser emerged under full House authentication, followed by a strategic escort and two Sanctuary tenders.

Joel met her in Ardent Central Command.

Annette entered wearing full Head Justicar black.

Her long peach hair flowed behind her.

The pink rose of the Justicar Shield sat above her left temple.

House officers who had been talking stopped.

Anton noticed.

He leaned toward Christer.

“Everyone suddenly remembers their paperwork.”

Christer nodded.

“Healthy instinct.”

Annette looked toward them.

Both became silent.

Joel smiled.

“Welcome.”

Annette kissed him once.

Then she turned toward the tactical table.

“War first.”

Her peach hair shifted.

Two small horns became visible.

## 9. First Capital Boarding

Bastion’s orbit became a furnace.

A House destroyer vanished inside a strike bloom and emerged with one flank burning. An Ardent heavy cruiser lost its bow battery to a precise House salvo and drifted backward, alive but crippled.

Christer’s bridge shook under another near hit.

“Enemy capital two adjusting angle!”

“Trying to cross our line!”

He was already focused on the first capital.

Perfect.

## 11. Excision War

“Understood.”

“Twelve is enough.”

The reference image of their armour lived in old House archives for a reason: giant warded shields, great swords edged for anti-symbiote work, incinerator cannon systems, reinforced thrusters, null filters, redundant optics, sanctified munition feeds. Bastion was no longer a political crisis.

## 13. The Hunters of Bastion

The Seraph Hunter transport hit Bastion’s battlespace like a falling blade.

It was not elegant.

It was compact, armoured, and ugly in the way only purpose-built warcraft could be. Heavy warding seals covered the hull. Null exhaust bled pale light behind it. Two escort cutters stayed close while Bastion’s guns and Ardent missiles tried to kill it before it reached the fight.

Husk Guard.

Experimental soldiers.

Walking surgery.

Then grinned.

“Good. Now kill the rest.”

His marines surged past the Hunters’ flanks and hit the human troops behind the warforms. One shouted, “PRISONERS STILL COUNT!” Another yelled back, “THEN AIM BETTER!”

The corridor became a slaughterhouse.

The Husk Guard fought with terrifying strength, but the Hunters had been built for exactly this. Their shields caught claws and heavy rounds. Their blades bit through reinforced host armour. Their incinerators burned symbiote tissue without setting the whole section alight. When one experimental soldier tried to grapple a Hunter from behind, the suit’s null projector flared and the thing dropped, screaming as black filaments writhed out of its neck seal.

Anton fired twice into another soldier’s legs and shoulder.

“Alive if possible,” he ordered.

A marine looked at the twitching thing on the deck.

“Possible is a very flexible word, sir.”

“Use a less flexible interpretation.”

Outside, Bastion still burned.

Christer’s capital duel had become savage.

His ship and the second Ardent capital were nearly broadside to one another now, each trying to cripple the other before boarding became necessary. Heavy shells crossed the void in visible streaks. A House cruiser died under concentrated Bastion fire. In return, one of Ardent’s escort destroyers split open from stem to stern under a mass-driver hit.

Then the second Seraph craft entered his battlespace.

Not a boarding shuttle.

A warship.

Small by fleet standards, but unmistakably Seraph in form and heat signature.

Christer saw it at the same moment Annette did.

“More coming,” he said.

Annette’s voice answered over command. “Hold your line. The Hunters are buying us time.”

Back aboard Anton’s target capital, Lance-Captain Voss reported as her Hunters cleared the lower decks.

“Two Husk Guard teams neutralized.”

“Seven experimental combatants dead.”

“Three secured alive.”

“Seraph biomatter present in engineering access.”

Anton stopped.

“Engineering?”

“Yes.”

That was wrong.

Or worse than wrong.

Why would Seraph combat stock already be staged inside an Ardent capital unless the ship had become more than a warship?

He looked toward the deck map.

There were sealed compartments below engineering that did not match standard military layout.

Medical.

Containment.

Storage.

He smiled without humour.

“Of course.”

The marines around him noticed.

That smile meant he had found another problem.

Lance-Captain Voss looked at him through her dark visor.

“You have identified a new objective.”

“Yes.”

“State it.”

Anton keyed the bridge-route team to continue upward without him and turned toward the lower-seal passage.

“There is a laboratory inside this ship.”

The Hunters did not react.

They simply began moving that way.

## 14. The Duel Ends

Christer’s capital ship shuddered under another direct hit.

The bridge lights failed.

For three seconds, only emergency red remained.

Then tactical screens returned one by one.

“Forward armour down to thirty-eight percent!”

“Starboard batteries still firing!”

“Two destroyers lost from screen!”

“Enemy capital maintaining pressure!”

Christer stood behind the command rail, one hand locked around it as the deck rolled beneath him.

Ahead, the second Ardent capital ship filled the main display.

It was wounded.

So were they.

Both ships had crossed beyond the point where a careful commander would normally disengage.

Neither captain intended to be careful.

“Enemy spinal gun charging,” tactical warned.

Christer looked at the range.

Close Hammer.

Too close.

“Turn twenty degrees.”

The helmsman looked back.

“That exposes our damaged port side.”

“I know.”

The ship began turning.

One officer stared at Christer.

“Sir, if that spinal shot hits—”

“It will not.”

The Ardent gun fired.

White light crossed the void.

Christer’s ship continued turning.

The shot missed the command spine by less than a kilometre and tore through the wreck of a drifting frigate behind them.

The bridge crew went silent.

Christer pointed toward the enemy.

“Now.”

His own capital ship fired everything that still worked.

Spinal gun.

Heavy batteries.

Missile racks.

Cruisers around him joined the volley.

The Ardent capital disappeared inside impacts.

Its armour split across the forward quarter.

One engine died.

Then another.

But the ship remained alive.

Still turning.

Still fighting.

Christer smiled.

“Hard bastard.”

Then Kira’s voice entered the command net.

“New Seraph contacts.”

Christer’s expression changed.

The tactical display expanded.

Three black-gold signatures were moving from behind Bastion.

Then five.

Small cruisers.

Boarding carriers.

One larger hull still hidden behind sensor interference.

Lucy followed.

“Enemy Seraph force is committing toward the Ardent line.”

Christer frowned.

“Toward us?”

Annette answered.

“Toward everyone.”

The first Seraph cruiser opened fire.

Not at House ships.

At the crippled Ardent capital.

Christer stared.

A lance struck the Ardent vessel near its command section.

Another hit followed.

“They are destroying their own allies,” his tactical officer said.

Annette understood first.

“No.”

Her pink rose caught the light on the strategic feed.

“They are destroying evidence.”

Christer looked toward the damaged capital.

The Ardent ship had fought beside Seraph forces.

Its crew knew things.

Its computers knew more.

And now the Seraphs wanted it dead before the House could take it.

“Boarding status?” Christer demanded.

“None launched yet.”

“Then launch now.”

The assault officer looked toward him.

“Sir, point defence is still active.”

“Not for long.”

Christer opened fleet command.

“All available guns. Suppress enemy point defence.”

House destroyers accelerated.

Cruisers shifted fire.

The damaged Ardent capital found itself caught between two enemies.

House weapons stripped turrets and tracking arrays.

Seraph lances tried to kill the entire ship.

Then the Ardent captain opened a channel.

His face appeared through smoke.

“House vessel.”

Christer looked at him.

“You finally want to talk.”

The man coughed.

“Seraph ships are firing on us.”

“Yes.”

“They were supposed to reinforce Bastion.”

Christer leaned closer.

“And now?”

The captain looked toward his own bridge.

Several crew were wounded.

One console burned behind him.

“Now I think they intend to leave no witnesses.”

Christer’s expression hardened.

“Cut engines.”

The captain hesitated.

“Lower weapons.”

Another hesitation.

Christer raised his voice.

“You have two choices. Surrender to me or die proving the Seraphs never cared about you.”

A Seraph lance struck the capital’s aft section.

The bridge feed shook violently.

Then the Ardent captain looked back.

“Weapons cold.”

Across the tactical display, the great ship’s batteries stopped firing.

Its remaining drive bloom faded.

Christer opened the House-wide channel.

“White Warrant. Enemy capital surrendering.”

Annette responded immediately.

“Accepted.”

“Protect the surrendered vessel.”

That order changed the battle.

House destroyers moved around the crippled Ardent capital.

Not to finish it.

To shield it.

Vigil frigates filled the space between it and the incoming Seraph cruisers.

Point defence turned outward.

Christer watched Seraph missiles begin crossing the dark.

“Now they are shooting at us for saving their allies.”

Annette’s voice went cold.

“That tells us exactly what those allies know.”

A House assault cutter launched toward the surrendered capital.

Then another.

Then ten more.

Christer checked his armour.

His bridge officer looked toward him.

“You are going personally.”

“Yes.”

“You just fought a capital duel.”

“Yes.”

“The ship needs you here.”

Christer looked toward the surrendered Ardent vessel.

“That captain just chose surrender over Seraph execution.”

He pointed toward the Seraph cruisers closing behind it.

“If they reach him first, we lose the crew and the evidence.”

The officer sighed.

“I knew that was going to be your answer.”

Christer smiled.

“Good.”

He turned toward tactical.

“You have command until I transfer.”

Then Annette cut in.

“Christer.”

He stopped.

“Yes?”

“The larger Seraph contact just crossed confirmation range.”

The tactical screen changed.

The hidden silhouette appeared.

A heavy Seraph carrier.

Its lower hull opened.

Hundreds of small launch signatures began appearing.

Boarding craft.

Coffin barges.

Husk Guard transports.

Christer stared.

Annette continued.

“Bastion is becoming a full Seraph engagement.”

Christer’s crimson eyes brightened.

“Then we finish this quickly.”

He left the bridge at a run.

Behind him, House capital ships turned their guns away from the surrendered Ardent vessel and toward the approaching Seraph force.

The duel was over.

The real battle had just arrived.

## 15. Central Becomes House Ground

Ardent Central was quiet.

Not peaceful.

Quiet.

There was a difference.

The last organized Ardent resistance had surrendered six hours earlier. Special Custody remained sealed under Justicar authority. Shepherd’s archive cores were already duplicated and moved into armored storage aboard two Sanctuary tenders. The surviving elders were under medical guard. Civilian districts had been searched, cleared, and reopened one by one.

No more boarding alarms.

No more hostile launches.

No more hidden security teams trying to burn records.

For the first time since Project Shepherd had been uncovered, Joel could look at the tactical map of Central and see green.

All of it.

Captain Mara Vale stood beside him on the command deck of his capital ship.

“Station command confirms full control.”

Joel nodded.

“Civilian sectors?”

“Stable.”

“Life support?”

“Stable.”

“Medical?”

“Overloaded, but functioning.”

“Seraph contamination?”

Mara looked toward the Justicar report.

“No active biological signatures outside sealed evidence zones.”

Joel exhaled slowly.

Central was secure.

That meant he could finally move.

The tactical display widened.

Bastion was burning.

Gatehold was under pressure.

The Seraph carrier at Bastion had turned that front into something far larger than originally planned.

Daniel’s Gatehold force was still fighting to prevent route denial.

Joel looked at his fleet.

Central Banner had taken losses, but not enough to pin it in place.

Two cruisers damaged.

Three destroyers out of line.

Several Vigil frigates carrying armor scars and heat damage.

But the force remained combat capable.

Behind them, Ardent Central itself could now act as an anchor.

That changed the campaign.

“Split the fleet,” Joel ordered.

His operations officer looked up.

“High Marshal?”

“Central no longer needs the full Banner.”

Joel began marking groups.

“Leave one Oath cruiser.”

“Four Vigils.”

“Two destroyers.”

“Sanctuary support.”

“Enough marines to hold internal security.”

Mara studied the remaining force.

“And the rest?”

Joel pointed toward Bastion.

“Bastion gets the heavy group.”

Then Gatehold.

“Gatehold gets fast reinforcement.”

His tactical officer nodded.

“Two detachments.”

“Yes.”

Annette entered the command channel.

Her face appeared beside the battle map.

Peach hair.

Pink rose clearly visible.

Kira and Lucy active.

“You are moving already.”

“Yes.”

“Central status?”

“Secure.”

Annette checked the same reports through Kira.

“Confirmed. Station-wide hostile resistance below operational threshold.”

Lucy added:

“Civilian confidence indicators improving.”

Joel smiled faintly.

“Then we stop treating Central like disputed ground.”

Annette looked at him.

That sentence had weight.

Mara noticed too.

“What does that mean?”

Joel stared at Ardent Central.

The station had hidden Shepherd beneath its courts.

It had trafficked prisoners.

It had preserved House elders as laboratory stock.

It had invited Seraph access into human space.

And now its old government was gone.

There was no intact authority he trusted enough to hand it back to.

Not yet.

Joel opened the station-wide broadcast.

Every deck received it.

Courts.

Docks.

Habitation.

Engineering.

Medical.

Security.

House troops came to attention as his voice passed through the station.

“This is High Marshal Joel von Hjertstedt.”

The remaining Ardent civilians stopped to listen.

“Ardent Central is secure.”

A pause.

“Project Shepherd has been seized.”

“Special Custody is under Justicar control.”

“Seraph facilities are being dismantled.”

“Civilian life-support, food, medicine, housing, and local law will continue.”

Then Joel’s tone changed.

Stronger.

Final.

“The government that permitted Shepherd no longer holds authority here.”

Mara looked toward him.

Annette did not interrupt.

Joel continued.

“Under Excision Campaign authority, Ardent Central is hereby placed under direct protection and military governance of House von Hjertstedt.”

The command deck became very still.

“Until contamination is eliminated.”

“Until the surviving population is secure.”

“And until a lawful future government can be built without Seraph influence.”

He paused again.

Then removed the uncertainty completely.

“From this moment, Ardent Central belongs to House von Hjertstedt.”

Not conquered for profit.

Not claimed because it was rich.

Claimed because the House could not safely leave it behind.

Across the station, House heraldry replaced the dead Special Custody authority seals.

Black.

Crimson.

The crown and blade.

Joel continued:

“Anyone who lives here and obeys civilian law remains protected.”

“Anyone who surrenders receives due process.”

“Anyone who attempts to restore Shepherd…”

His crimson eyes brightened.

“…will discover why this station changed hands.”

The transmission ended.

Mara looked at him.

“You just annexed a capital station.”

Joel nodded.

“Yes.”

“You people really do make exceptions for the Seraph.”

“Yes.”

Annette finally spoke.

“The claim is lawful under Excision authority.”

Her fingers touched the pink rose.

“And witnessed.”

That mattered.

The House was not pretending the seizure had not happened.

It was recording it.

Owning it.

Accepting responsibility for everything that came after.

Joel looked toward his officers.

“Raise House colors.”

Outside, lights changed along the command spine of Ardent Central.

The old Ardent emblem dimmed.

A massive House insignia came alive across the primary station tower.

Black field.

Crimson crest.

The mark could be seen from orbit.

There would be no confusion now.

Joel returned his attention to the war.

“Bastion reinforcement.”

His operations officer highlighted the heavy detachment.

“One capital ship, two Oath cruisers, three destroyers, six Vigil frigates.”

“Add an assault carrier.”

Mara raised an eyebrow.

“That is not a reinforcement.”

“It is from Central.”

“For most people, that is a fleet.”

Joel ignored her.

“Send it to Christer.”

Annette nodded.

“Agreed.”

“And Gatehold?”

Joel marked the second group.

“Fast force only.”

“Vigils, destroyers, one cruiser.”

Annette looked toward Daniel’s front.

“Kira agrees. Gatehold needs speed more than mass.”

Kira confirmed:

“Probability of gate-core destruction increases with delay.”

Joel’s jaw tightened.

“Send them.”

The orders went out.

House ships began breaking from Central formation.

Heavy hulls turned toward Bastion.

Fast escorts accelerated toward Gatehold.

Behind them, Ardent Central remained under the crown and blade.

A conquered station.

A protected population.

A new House responsibility.

And the first permanent territorial claim of the Excision War.

Annette watched the movement.

“Joel.”

“Yes?”

“You understand what this does politically.”

“Yes.”

“The Imperium will object.”

“Yes.”

“The Hiigarans will object.”

“Yes.”

“Terra will negotiate.”

Joel smiled.

“Of course.”

Mara looked between them.

“And you are still doing it.”

Joel looked toward the newly raised House colors over Ardent Central.

“Yes.”

Then the Bastion feed flashed.

Seraph launch signatures multiplied again.

Gatehold transmitted a new warning.

Both fronts needed help.

Joel pointed toward the moving detachments.

“Central is ours.”

His voice hardened.

“Now reinforce the others.”
