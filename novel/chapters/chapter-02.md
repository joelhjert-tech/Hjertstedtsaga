# Chapter 2: Return to Kestrel

## 1. Return to Kestrel

By the time Joel returned with the engineering convoy, Kestrel had moved from emergency survival into reconstruction.

The station appeared on the main screen as the House convoy cleared the gate.

For several seconds, Joel von Hjertstedt said nothing.

The station looked nothing like the wreck he remembered.

Lights covered the broken asteroid from pole to pole. Docking arms moved steadily around the outer structure. Patrol ships held disciplined positions beyond the traffic lanes. New armour surrounded the command tower. Defensive platforms tracked incoming vessels without pointing weapons directly at them.

Civilian freighters waited in organized holding patterns.

Repair craft moved between them.

Kestrel was alive.

Anton stood beside Joel.

This time neither man looked like a passenger.

Joel wore the full black military uniform of House von Hjertstedt.

Armoured fabric covered his chest beneath a long command coat lined in deep crimson. The House crest—crown and blade—rested over his heart.

Across his collar sat the insignia that removed any possibility of misunderstanding.

**HIGH MARSHAL.**

The highest military authority of House von Hjertstedt.

A ceremonial crimson command sash crossed beneath his coat, but nothing about the uniform looked decorative. The material was reinforced. His sidearm rested openly at his hip. His dark-steel augmented arm was uncovered from wrist to elbow.

Anton stood beside him in heavier black combat uniform.

His coat remained open over reinforced command armour. A heavy pistol rested on one thigh and his compact breaching hammer was locked across his back.

His collar carried a different insignia.

**BREACH COMMANDER.**

Beneath it sat the crimson mark identifying him as House blood.

Anton looked at the station.

“Well.”

Joel nodded.

“Well.”

“The guns work now.”

“Some of them.”

“Better than last time.”

“Last time they had fourteen missiles.”

Anton smiled.

“That was funny.”

“It was not funny.”

“It became funny after we survived.”

Behind their command transport came the rest of the convoy.

Not warships.

Work ships.

Two Sanctuary tenders.

Three heavy engineering transports.

A fabrication barge.

A cargo vessel carrying reactor parts, structural materials and replacement environmental systems.

And one very large agricultural transport.

Anton looked toward its identification marker.

“You really brought the whole thing?”

Joel glanced at him.

“Yes.”

“Sera asked for agricultural equipment.”

“She is getting agricultural equipment.”

Anton looked at the huge vessel.

“That is not equipment.”

“No.”

“It is a farm.”

“Yes.”

“A very large farm.”

Joel folded his arms.

“Kestrel spent years depending on ration shipments and failing recycling systems. That will not happen again.”

Anton smiled.

“There he is.”

Joel looked at him.

“What?”

“The High Marshal.”

“I am wearing the rank.”

“You could be wearing a cooking apron and still somehow turn breakfast into a strategic logistics problem.”

Joel ignored him.

Kestrel Control opened a channel.

“House command vessel, authentication complete.”

The controller paused.

Then his voice became more formal.

“High Marshal von Hjertstedt. Breach Commander von Hjertstedt. Welcome back to Kestrel.”

Anton leaned toward the microphone.

“Much better greeting than last time.”

The controller answered immediately.

“Last time you brought a Coalition fleet with you.”

Joel looked at Anton.

Anton looked offended.

“We did not bring them.”

“They followed you.”

“Important distinction.”

The controller laughed.

“Command Collar Three is prepared. Sector Commander Venn is waiting.”

Joel looked at Kestrel.

“Take us in.”

---

The military docking collar opened twenty minutes later.

Sera Venn was waiting.

She wore the grey-black uniform of Kestrel Sector Command with House affiliation markings beneath the station crest.

Dax stood beside her.

Commander Halden Rook stood on the other side.

Behind them waited a formal honour guard made from Kestrel soldiers.

Joel stopped when he entered.

Sera stared at him.

Then Anton.

Then back at Joel.

“You wore uniforms.”

Anton looked down at himself.

“We own them.”

“I had started to think you only had traveller coats and illegal weapons.”

“They were not illegal,” Joel said.

“On Terminus Spire?”

Joel paused.

“Probably illegal.”

Dax looked at Anton’s rank insignia.

“Breach Commander.”

Anton smiled.

“You sound disappointed.”

“No. It explains several things.”

Rook looked at Joel’s collar.

Unlike Dax, he did not smile.

He came to attention.

“High Marshal.”

Joel sighed.

“Rook.”

“You are wearing the rank.”

“That does not mean you need to become ceremonial.”

Rook remained perfectly straight.

“It absolutely does.”

Anton laughed.

Sera looked past Joel toward the docking windows.

More House transports were moving into position.

Her expression changed.

“How many people did you bring?”

Joel looked at his slate.

“Two hundred and forty-eight engineers and technical specialists.”

Sera stared.

“For an inspection?”

“For the next phase.”

“What next phase?”

Joel pointed toward Kestrel.

“The part where we stop repairing battle damage and start rebuilding the station properly.”

Sera folded her arms.

“The station is operational.”

“Yes.”

“Fully repaired.”

“Most visible systems are repaired.”

Anton leaned toward Sera.

“He read your engineering report.”

Sera’s eyes narrowed.

“All of it?”

Joel answered.

“Yes.”

“That report was nine hundred pages.”

“Eight hundred eighty-seven.”

Sera stared.

Anton smiled.

“I told you. Strategic breakfast.”

Joel continued.

“Your outer systems are repaired. Your civilian districts are stable. Your main reactor is healthy. Your docks work.”

He looked toward the asteroid.

“But you still have sealed lower levels, old coolant trunks, incomplete industrial automation, and too much dependence on imported replacement parts.”

Sera was silent.

Joel smiled slightly.

“So I brought engineers.”

---

They walked into the docking concourse.

Behind them, House engineering personnel began leaving the transports.

They did not march like soldiers.

They arrived pushing equipment.

Diagnostic crawlers.

Portable fabrication units.

Pressure-test drones.

Cable spools.

Reactor tools.

Hull scanners.

Replacement pumps.

Some wore House black.

Others wore the dark grey of Chaplain-Mechanist engineering orders.

Sera watched them spreading into organized teams.

“You planned this before asking me.”

Joel nodded.

“Yes.”

Anton added:

“He expected you to say yes.”

Sera looked at Joel.

“And if I said no?”

“They return home.”

Anton looked shocked.

“All of them?”

“Yes.”

“That would be very embarrassing.”

Joel continued walking.

---

Their next stop was the main cargo dock.

That was where Sera finally saw the agricultural system.

The transport had opened along its entire side.

Inside were rows of sealed modules stacked like giant containers.

But these were not ordinary cargo pods.

Transparent sections showed growing racks.

Green.

Actual green.

Plants already growing under controlled light.

Sera stopped.

Dax stopped beside her.

Rook stared.

“What is that?” Sera asked.

Joel looked pleased.

“Kestrel Autonomous Agricultural Network.”

Anton nodded toward the modules.

“We need a shorter name.”

Joel ignored him.

Sera walked closer.

A House agricultural engineer approached.

“Commander.”

She activated a holographic model.

The system expanded across the loading dock.

Hydroponic towers.

Aeroponic racks.

Algae protein systems.

Mushroom beds.

Seed storage.

Pollination drones.

Automated harvesting machines.

Water recovery.

Waste processing.

Nutrient reclamation.

Robotic maintenance crawlers.

The engineer continued.

“The system is designed to operate with minimal permanent staff. Crop rotation, nutrient balance, irrigation, harvesting and reseeding are automated.”

Sera looked toward Joel.

“You brought us a complete farming network.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Joel looked at her as if the answer were obvious.

“You have a station.”

“Yes.”

“You have people.”

“Yes.”

“People eat.”

Anton nodded seriously.

“Constantly.”

Sera ignored him.

Joel continued.

“Kestrel survived the Coalition because people refused to abandon one another.”

He looked around the huge dock.

“It should never again be possible to defeat this station simply by cutting food shipments.”

Rook became very quiet.

He remembered.

Seven years of rationing.

Children growing up hungry.

People dying because supply ships never came.

The engineer changed the hologram.

“The system is modular. We install the first sections inside former military storage levels. Later we expand into the repaired habitation ring.”

Dax studied the production figures.

“This is enough for the current population?”

“Yes.”

“Reserve?”

“Considerable.”

Joel added:

“And scalable.”

Sera looked at him.

“You intend Kestrel to grow.”

“No.”

She raised an eyebrow.

Joel corrected himself.

“I expect Kestrel to grow.”

Anton smiled.

“Better answer.”

---

The inspection continued.

Dock Ring Two had been rebuilt completely.

The old barricades were gone.

Armoured checkpoints had replaced them.

Independent pressure controls divided the docking zones.

Automated weapons remained recessed behind armour.

Joel inspected each section carefully.

This time people knew exactly who he was.

Officers came to attention when he passed.

Engineers stopped to salute.

Joel returned every salute, but he did not interfere with local command.

Sera noticed.

“This is still my station?”

Joel looked at her.

“Why would that have changed?”

“You arrived with two hundred engineers, automated farms and enough military rank to frighten half the sector.”

“That sounds like a logistics visit.”

Anton nodded.

“A very aggressive logistics visit.”

Sera laughed.

---

The military training levels were active.

Six hundred personnel now rotated through Kestrel’s defence structure.

Permanent security.

Reservists.

Technical combat teams.

Flight crews.

Boarding personnel.

House instructors were present, but Kestrel officers commanded every unit.

Joel stood above the training floor with his hands behind his back.

“Better.”

Sera looked at him.

“That is all?”

“Much better.”

“That sounded painful for you.”

“I am practicing encouragement.”

Anton stepped beside him.

“He is terrible at it.”

Joel pointed toward one defensive drill.

“That squad is bunching at the doorway.”

Sera sighed.

“There he is.”

---

Then they saw the *Wayward Star*.

Anton stopped immediately.

The rebuilt frigate rested inside her dock under white maintenance lights.

New armour.

New point defence.

Rebuilt drives.

Replacement weapon mounts.

But still unmistakably Sera’s ship.

Anton stared.

“Oh.”

Dax smiled.

“You want to see the weapons.”

“Yes.”

Joel answered without looking at him.

“No.”

“I outrank most people here.”

“You do not outrank me.”

Anton looked toward Sera.

“Sector Commander?”

Sera considered it.

Anton smiled hopefully.

“No.”

Anton looked betrayed.

Dax laughed.

---

Their final inspection was Central Habitation.

That silenced everyone.

The old refugee camps were gone.

Markets filled the main concourse.

Schools occupied former military offices.

The inspection group stepped into Kestrel Medical.

A proper hospital was open: staffed treatment bays, diagnostics, sterile preparation and patients under active care.

Families lived in proper apartments.

Children ran beneath a restored observation window.

Near the centre stood the memorial.

Names covered the wall.

Old garrison.

Refugees.

Mercenaries.

House soldiers.

Everyone lost during Kestrel’s abandoned years and the battle that ended them.

Joel set a small bundle of station-grown flowers among the offerings with his mechanical left hand.

Beside it, Anton placed a simple glass memorial candle and steadied the flame.

Joel bowed his bare head.

Anton did the same.

For several seconds, the High Marshal and Breach Commander stood silently in full military uniform before the dead.

Then Joel lifted his gaze.

“Good.”

This time nobody joked.

---

The debrief began inside Kestrel Command.

Sera sat in the same old commander’s chair.

Joel remained standing at the tactical table.

His High Marshal insignia caught the light whenever he moved.

Anton sat beside Dax, breaching hammer resting against the chair.

Sera activated the station model.

“Structural restoration: ninety-four percent.”

Joel nodded.

“Better than your report.”

“Your engineers already started changing numbers.”

“That is why I brought them.”

“Life support: full operational capacity.”

“Defence?”

“Point-defence grid eighty-eight percent. Two heavy towers fully operational. Third tower undergoing final alignment.”

Anton nodded.

“Missiles?”

Sera looked at him.

“Enough.”

“That is not a number.”

“You do not need the number.”

“That hurts.”

Sera continued.

“Military readiness is green.”

Another display appeared.

“Civilian population continues increasing.”

Then she added the agricultural modules.

“With your farming network installed, Kestrel should become largely food-independent.”

Joel nodded once.

That mattered more to him than the weapon report.

Sera noticed.

“You like that more than the guns.”

“Yes.”

Anton leaned toward Dax.

“He always has.”

Joel looked at Sera.

“A fortress without food is a coffin with artillery.”

Rook slowly nodded.

He understood that better than anyone.

Sera changed the display again.

The Vardos Sector appeared.

Several systems were stable.

Several were neutral.

Others were marked yellow and red.

“The station is fixed,” Sera said.

“The military works.”

“The farms will make us harder to isolate.”

She looked at Joel and Anton.

“But Kestrel is no longer the problem.”

Joel studied the sector map.

Anton’s smile returned.

Sera continued.

“The Coalition is gone.”

“But the sector it left behind is beginning to reorganize.”

Joel rested both hands on the tactical table.

The inspection was over. Sera had brought him a sector problem.

“Then,” Joel said, “we debrief the sector.”

## 2. The Next Contract

The tactical map of the Vardos Sector filled Kestrel’s command table.

Sera Venn stood at one side.

Joel stood opposite her in full High Marshal uniform.

Anton remained beside him, arms folded across his Breach Commander armour.

Commander Halden Rook and Dax Corven completed the circle.

The repaired command centre was quiet.

No alarms.

No boarding warnings.

No Coalition fleet hammering the outer armour.

Joel preferred it that way.

Sera pointed toward the first system.

“Harven Reach.”

The marker changed from yellow to blue.

“Former Coalition mining territory. The local council has declared independence.”

Anton leaned toward the display.

“Stable?”

“For now.”

“Military?”

“Four patrol ships. Two armed freighters.”

Anton looked at Joel.

“Not stable.”

Sera continued.

“They have requested trade access through Kestrel.”

Joel nodded.

“Give it.”

Rook looked toward him.

“Immediately?”

“Trade creates dependence in both directions. Better they depend on Kestrel markets than some local warlord.”

Sera smiled slightly.

“That sounds almost imperial.”

Joel looked offended.

“It is practical.”

Anton leaned toward Rook.

“That means yes.”

Sera selected another system.

“Damaris.”

The marker turned red.

“Two former Coalition captains are fighting for control.”

“Civilian population?” Joel asked.

“About two hundred thousand across three habitats.”

“Any attacks against civilian infrastructure?”

“Not yet.”

Joel nodded.

“Then we watch.”

Anton frowned.

“No intervention?”

“No.”

“They are fighting.”

“They are allowed.”

Anton looked disappointed.

Joel continued.

“If they begin destroying life support, food production or civilian transport, the situation changes.”

Sera looked at him.

“You really intend to let the sector solve some of its own problems.”

“Yes.”

“You have enough ships to take half of it.”

Joel’s expression hardened.

“That is exactly why we should not.”

The room became quiet.

The House did not need another empire.

It needed allies.

Ports.

Contracts.

People who opened their gates when the House arrived instead of preparing for occupation.

Joel pointed at Kestrel.

“This station becomes stronger if neighbours choose to work with it.”

Then he pointed toward the surrounding systems.

“It becomes weaker if every neighbour believes Sera Venn is simply the first stage of Hjertstedt expansion.”

Sera nodded.

“Good.”

Anton looked at her.

“You sound surprised.”

“I have met powerful people before.”

“That does sound unpleasant.”

“It usually was.”

---

The next system appeared.

“Korlan Free Port,” Sera said.

A station icon appeared.

“Mercenary market?”

Dax nodded.

“Small.”

“Growing,” Sera corrected.

“Most captains avoided the Vardos Sector when the Coalition controlled the lanes. Now they are coming back.”

Joel became interested.

“How many independent crews?”

“Twenty-seven registered this month.”

Anton smiled.

“There we are.”

Joel ignored him for the moment.

“Quality?”

Sera shrugged.

“Mixed.”

“Pirates?”

“Three crews are under investigation.”

“Slavers?”

“None confirmed.”

Anton looked disappointed again.

“You really need better enemies.”

Dax stared at him.

“We are trying to have fewer enemies.”

Anton considered this.

“That sounds reasonable when you say it.”

Sera changed the display.

The final marker was Kestrel itself.

It was surrounded by a new defensive formation.

Six Vigil frigates.

Two destroyers.

One Oath cruiser.

A Sanctuary tender.

Several local Kestrel patrol craft moved inside the larger formation.

Sera looked at Joel.

“House Shield Group Kestrel.”

Joel nodded.

“Temporary assignment.”

“How temporary?”

“Until your local fleet can protect the station without needing them.”

Rook looked toward the display.

“That could take years.”

“Then they stay for years.”

Sera studied him.

“No annexation clauses?”

“No.”

“No House governor?”

“No.”

“No claim to station revenue?”

“No.”

Anton leaned toward her.

“He will, however, complain about your ammunition storage.”

Joel looked at him.

“The storage is inefficient.”

Sera folded her arms.

“It passed inspection.”

“It passed minimum inspection.”

“It passed.”

Joel sighed.

“This is why I brought engineers.”

Sera smiled.

“And they are staying too.”

“Some.”

“How many?”

“One hundred and twelve for the next phase.”

Rook almost laughed.

Sera stared at Joel.

“You brought two hundred and forty-eight.”

“They finished the emergency work.”

“And half are staying?”

“Yes.”

Anton nodded.

“He has become attached to your reactor problems.”

Joel ignored him.

Sera looked toward the agricultural section of the station model.

Green areas now occupied several former military storage levels.

The automated farming network was expanding.

Food reserves were rising.

Water recycling had improved.

New habitation sections were opening.

Kestrel was becoming something more than a fortress.

A town.

A port.

A mercenary base.

Perhaps eventually a city.

Sera looked around the table.

“So this is the plan.”

Joel nodded.

“You rebuild.”

“The House protects the station while I rebuild.”

“Yes.”

“And the sector?”

Joel looked toward the surrounding systems.

“We help those who ask.”

Anton added:

“And shoot those who make that necessary.”

Joel glanced at him.

“Within the Charter.”

“Obviously.”

Sera laughed.

“Somehow it sounds less obvious when you say it.”

---

The meeting lasted another two hours.

Trade agreements.

Patrol routes.

Refugee intake.

Repair capacity.

Docking tariffs.

Salvage law.

Weapons inspections.

The glamorous work of rebuilding civilization.

By the end, Anton looked ready to invade someone simply to escape the paperwork.

Sera closed the final display.

“That is everything.”

Joel stood.

“Good work.”

Sera looked at him.

“That sounded almost encouraging.”

“I have been practicing.”

Anton stood beside him.

“He practiced in the transport.”

Joel stared at him.

Sera laughed.

The meeting ended.

---

Several hours later, Joel and Anton walked through Kestrel.

No guards followed.

No officers.

No formal inspection party.

They had changed clothes.

The High Marshal coat was gone.

The Breach Commander armour was gone.

Joel wore a dark civilian jacket.

Anton wore an old brown coat that had clearly survived several adventures it should not have.

Their House insignia was hidden.

Their weapons were smaller.

Mostly.

Anton looked toward the growing market district.

“You realize everyone knows what we look like now.”

“Not everyone.”

“We were on station broadcasts.”

“People rarely look closely.”

Anton glanced toward him.

“You have crimson eyes.”

“Not constantly.”

“That is true.”

They walked past food stalls.

Repair shops.

Recruiting offices.

A merchant selling salvaged ship parts.

Children ran between the tables.

Two Iron Guard soldiers sat outside a noodle shop arguing with Kestrel mechanics.

Joel smiled faintly.

This was why they had come back.

Not the guns.

Not the armour.

This.

Life.

Anton stopped beside a viewing window.

Outside, House frigates held position beyond Kestrel.

“You stationed an Oath cruiser.”

“Yes.”

“That is not subtle.”

“The Coalition tried to take the station twice.”

“Fair.”

Anton looked toward the civilian ships entering the docks.

“So what now?”

Joel continued walking.

“We find someone else.”

Anton smiled.

“There it is.”

---

The bar was called **The Broken Gate**.

Anton loved the name immediately.

Joel hated the lighting.

That made it acceptable.

They took a table near the back.

Not hidden.

Just unimportant.

A place where two travelling men could drink without anyone asking questions.

The bar was full of mercenaries.

Pilots.

Bounty hunters.

Cargo escorts.

Former soldiers.

Independent captains.

A few people who were probably criminals and several people who were definitely pretending not to be intelligence agents.

Anton looked around.

“Better.”

Joel ordered two drinks.

“Do not start betting immediately.”

“I have not.”

“You are looking at people.”

“That is normally necessary for recruitment.”

Joel looked toward the bar.

A captain was arguing with a broker about an unpaid rescue contract.

At another table, three mercenaries quietly divided their payment with the family of a dead crew member.

Near the door, a young pilot refused a high-paying job after discovering the client intended to transport forced labour.

Anton noticed the same things Joel did.

Neither commented.

Not yet.

The drinks arrived.

Anton lifted his glass.

“To Kestrel.”

Joel touched his glass against it.

“To Sera.”

Anton drank.

Then his eyes moved toward the crowded room again.

“So.”

Joel leaned back.

“So.”

“Captain?”

“Maybe.”

“Crew?”

“Maybe.”

“Interesting disaster?”

Joel looked around the bar.

“That usually finds us.”

Anton smiled.

Across the room, someone suddenly slammed a contract slate onto a table.

A mercenary captain stood.

Angry.

Young.

Exhausted.

And very clearly refusing an offer that would have made her rich.

Joel looked toward Anton.

Anton was already smiling.

Neither brother spoke.

They simply picked up their drinks.

The next recruitment opportunity had just entered the room.

## 3. The Bounty Hunter’s Contract

The Broken Gate became quiet before the bounty hunter said a word.

That was the first thing Joel noticed.

The door opened.

A tall man stepped inside wearing a long dark coat over reinforced field armour. The coat had been cut around the shoulders so it would not catch on his weapons.

He carried three.

A heavy pistol on his right thigh.

A compact rifle across his back.

And a short-barrelled weapon under his left arm that looked designed for fighting inside ships.

The man was perhaps fifty.

Grey hair.

Scar across the jaw.

One artificial eye.

No visible faction colours.

That alone told Joel something.

Most mercenaries wanted people to know who they worked for.

Bounty hunters wanted people to know they worked for themselves.

Anton leaned back in his chair.

“Interesting.”

Joel took another drink.

“You say that too easily.”

The bounty hunter walked straight toward the centre of the bar.

Behind him came four station security officers.

Between them walked a prisoner.

The prisoner wore restraint cuffs around both wrists.

A locking collar covered his neck.

His clothes were clean.

Too clean for a captured pirate.

He looked perhaps forty.

Thin.

Well-fed.

Educated.

And completely calm.

Joel watched his eyes.

Not frightened.

Not angry.

Calculating.

Anton noticed too.

“That one is dangerous.”

“The prisoner?”

“Yes.”

“He is in restraints.”

Anton smiled.

“That rarely makes someone less dangerous.”

The bounty hunter reached an empty table.

He placed a metal contract slate on it.

Then he looked around the room.

“Mercenaries.”

Nobody answered.

Several captains watched him carefully.

The bounty hunter raised his voice.

“My name is Garran Holt.”

That produced a reaction.

A few people whispered.

One man at the bar turned away immediately.

Anton leaned toward Joel.

“You know him?”

“By reputation.”

“Good?”

“Alive.”

Anton nodded.

“Useful measure.”

Holt continued.

“I have a transport contract.”

The prisoner smiled faintly.

Holt ignored him.

“I need to move one secured prisoner from Kestrel to the Ardent Judicial Relay.”

A captain near the bar shouted:

“Then hire station security.”

Holt looked toward him.

“Station security does not operate outside Kestrel jurisdiction.”

Someone else called:

“Hire House escort.”

Anton nearly choked on his drink.

Joel kicked him lightly under the table.

Holt answered:

“I am not paying House rates.”

Anton looked offended.

Joel whispered:

“We are technically expensive.”

“We are sitting in a cheap bar.”

“That is personal choice.”

Holt continued.

“I need ships.”

That changed the room.

Mercenaries understood ships.

“How many?” someone asked.

“Minimum three combat-capable hulls.”

Another voice:

“Destination?”

“Ardent Relay.”

“How far?”

“Two gate transits.”

A captain near the back laughed.

“That route crosses Damaris space.”

“Yes.”

“Damaris is fighting a civil war.”

“Yes.”

“And you are moving a prisoner through it.”

“Yes.”

Anton looked at Joel.

“I like him.”

Joel ignored him.

One captain stood.

“What is the prisoner worth?”

Holt looked toward the restrained man.

“Alive?”

“Yes.”

“Three million credits.”

The bar became silent.

Anton slowly put down his glass.

“There it is.”

Joel watched the room change.

Three million credits was enough to buy ships.

Enough to settle debts.

Enough to make otherwise sensible people become stupid.

Holt continued.

“Dead, he is worth nothing to me.”

A mercenary shouted:

“But worth something to someone else?”

Holt looked directly toward him.

“Yes.”

That was the answer everyone had expected.

Another captain stood.

“How much for escort?”

Holt touched the slate.

The contract terms appeared on several nearby displays.

**BASE PAYMENT: 180,000 CREDITS PER ACCEPTED SHIP.**

**COMBAT BONUS: 40,000.**

**PRISONER DELIVERY BONUS: 60,000.**

**SALVAGE RIGHTS: LIMITED.**

**CASUALTY COMPENSATION: INCLUDED.**

Anton whistled quietly.

“Respectable.”

Joel nodded.

“Too respectable.”

Anton looked at him.

“You think he expects trouble.”

“He is advertising in a bar full of armed strangers.”

“Fair.”

Holt raised one hand.

“There is one condition.”

The prisoner finally spoke.

“He means several.”

His voice was calm.

Educated.

Almost amused.

Holt looked toward him.

“Silence.”

The prisoner smiled.

The bounty hunter faced the room again.

“Any ship accepting this contract accepts full escort discipline until delivery.”

One captain frowned.

“Your command?”

“Convoy command.”

“Same thing.”

“No.”

Another captain asked:

“What happens if someone tries to claim the prisoner?”

Holt answered immediately.

“We leave them behind.”

Anton smiled.

Joel looked at him.

“No.”

“I said nothing.”

“You were thinking something.”

“I was thinking that is efficient.”

The prisoner spoke again.

“You should probably tell them who will be trying.”

Holt turned toward him.

“I said silence.”

The prisoner looked around the bar.

“You are asking strangers to die for money. They deserve accurate information.”

That produced several murmurs.

Holt’s artificial eye shifted.

The prisoner smiled wider.

Then Joel saw something interesting.

Holt did not strike him.

Did not threaten him.

Did not panic.

He simply waited.

Controlled.

Professional.

Joel approved.

A young mercenary captain near the front spoke.

“Who is he?”

Holt said nothing for a moment.

Then:

“Former Vardos Coalition intelligence director.”

Sera’s war had ended recently enough that everyone in the bar understood what that meant.

The prisoner inclined his head slightly.

“Former Acting Director Marek Soren.”

The room changed again.

Some recognised the name.

Others recognised the title.

Anton leaned forward.

“Now it is interesting.”

Joel looked at him.

“More interesting.”

Soren continued before Holt could stop him.

“I managed Coalition covert operations in six systems.”

Holt’s jaw tightened.

“I signed warrants.”

## 6. The Prisoner Moves
