# Chapter 1: The Unwanted Fortress

## 1. The Unwanted Fortress

Terminus Spire turned slowly above the dead moon.

The station was one of the largest mercenary hubs in the sector. Thousands of ships surrounded it. Frigates waited beside cargo haulers. Armed transports drifted near repair docks. Old warships carried the colours of companies that had survived too many battles and trusted no government.

Every docking arm displayed contracts, warnings and bounty notices.

Joel and Anton walked through Dock Ring Seven wearing plain traveller coats.

No armour.

No House colours.

No guards.

To the station scanners, they were Joel Hart and Anton Berg, independent traders looking for transport into the frontier.

Anton looked through the main observation window.

A damaged frigate was approaching the station. Its hull was black around the engines. One weapon turret was missing. Emergency plates covered several sections along its left side.

“That one?” Anton asked.

Joel checked the small data screen hidden inside his sleeve.

“The *Wayward Star*,” he said. “Captain Sera Venn. Twenty-eight crew. Independent mercenaries. No confirmed crimes. Good record protecting civilians.”

“Poor record getting paid.”

“Very poor.”

The frigate entered the docking cradle. Magnetic arms locked around it.

Through the windows, Joel watched repair crews move toward the damaged ship.

A boarding hatch opened.

Captain Sera Venn stepped onto the station.

She was perhaps thirty-five years old. Her dark hair was tied behind her head. A fresh cut crossed one cheek. Her flight coat had been burned at the shoulder. Behind her came a tired crew carrying helmets, tool cases and two sealed body bags.

The station crowd cheered.

Sera did not.

She turned and placed one hand on each body bag as they passed.

Only then did she walk toward the contract hall.

Joel watched her crew follow.

No one left.

No one complained.

Anton gave a small smile.

“Interesting.”

They entered the contract hall behind them.

The hall was loud enough to feel like a battle bridge. Mercenary captains argued with brokers. Bounty hunters pushed through the crowd. Large screens displayed wars, escort jobs, missing ships and unpaid debts.

At the centre of the hall, Captain Sera stood before a contract judge.

“You completed the mission,” the judge said. “The miners were recovered. The raider base was destroyed. The client accepts the result.”

“Then release our payment,” Sera replied.

The judge looked uncomfortable.

“The client cannot pay.”

Sera’s crew became silent.

Her gunner, a broad man with a metal left hand, stepped forward.

“We lost two people.”

“I understand.”

“Our ship needs a drive rebuild.”

“I understand.”

“Understanding does not buy reactor parts.”

Security officers moved closer.

Sera raised one hand. Her gunner stopped.

“What did the client offer instead?” she asked.

The judge touched the display.

A hologram appeared above the table.

It showed a large military station built around a broken asteroid. Long defence towers rose from its surface. Docking arms extended into the dark. A central command structure stood at the highest point like a black crown.

Many parts of the station had no power.

Other sections showed weak heat signals.

“Sector Command Hub Kestrel,” the judge said. “Former military headquarters for the Vardos Sector.”

Someone in the crowd laughed.

Then several others joined.

Sera studied the image.

“Vardos is outside protected patrol space.”

“Yes.”

“The local systems are controlled by raiders, warlords and private mining armies.”

“Yes.”

“The last government fleet sent there disappeared.”

“That is also correct.”

The judge placed a metal command key on the table.

“The client owned the legal salvage claim. The station, its defensive zone and all attached military property now belong to you.”

Sera stared at him.

“You are paying us with a dead fortress in enemy territory.”

“A major fortress.”

“Without a working defence grid.”

“The condition of the defence grid is unknown.”

“Without a fleet.”

“You have a frigate.”

Her crew laughed this time.

It was not a happy sound.

Sera picked up the command key.

“How long before someone challenges the claim?”

“Thirty days. You must enter the station, activate its command core and broadcast ownership through the old military relay.”

“And if we do not?”

“The claim returns to the client.”

Sera closed her hand around the key.

“Of course it does.”

She turned toward her crew.

“We sell the claim,” her pilot said.

“No buyer will touch it,” said the engineer.

“We could trade the location to a warlord,” the gunner suggested.

Sera looked at the two body bags waiting near the wall.

“No,” she said. “We earned something. We are not leaving with nothing.”

Her engineer lowered her voice.

“Captain, the *Wayward Star* can barely reach the Vardos gate.”

“Then we repair what we can.”

“With which money?”

Sera looked around the hall.

“With passengers.”

Joel stepped forward before the brokers could surround her.

“How much for two cabins?” he asked.

Sera studied him. Then she looked at Anton.

Anton smiled in a way that made her gunner rest his metal hand near his pistol.

“You understand where we are going?” Sera asked.

“Vardos Sector,” Joel said. “Old military station. Unfriendly neighbours.”

“Unfriendly means they may fire before asking who we are.”

Anton shrugged.

“That often saves time.”

Sera looked between them.

“You soldiers?”

“Traders,” Joel answered.

Her eyes moved over their clean boots, balanced stance and empty hands.

“You do not stand like traders.”

“We have travelled through difficult places.”

The gunner stepped closer.

“What do you trade?”

Joel looked at the damaged frigate through the hall windows.

“Opportunities.”

Anton tried not to laugh.

Sera did not trust them. Joel could see it in her eyes.

That was good.

A captain who trusted two armed strangers within a minute was not worth recruiting.

“Half payment now,” she said. “Half when we reach Kestrel.”

Joel transferred three times the requested amount.

Sera checked the number.

“This is too much.”

“Then provide meals.”

“We have military ration blocks.”

Anton leaned toward Joel.

“This was a mistake.”

For the first time, Sera smiled.

It lasted only a second.

“Dock Seven. Departure in six hours. Bring your own pressure suits.”

---

The *Wayward Star* left Terminus Spire with patched armour and an engine that shook during every hard burn.

Joel and Anton took a small passenger cabin near the ship’s centre.

Anton sat on the lower bunk while Joel reviewed the crew through a hidden House intelligence feed.

“Pilot is excellent,” Joel said. “Engineer rebuilt the cooling system during combat. Gunner refused an order to fire through a civilian transport.”

“Captain?”

Joel closed the display.

“She divided the last mission payment between the families of the dead before taking her own share.”

Anton leaned back.

“So we recruit them.”

“Perhaps.”

“Joel, we followed them onto a broken frigate travelling toward a dead military fortress surrounded by warlords.”

“Yes.”

“That feels like a decision.”

“It is an investigation.”

The ship alarm sounded.

Both brothers became still.

A voice came through the cabin speaker.

“Passengers to the bridge,” Captain Sera ordered. “Now.”

They entered the bridge thirty seconds later.

Red light covered the command deck. The crew sat at their stations. Ahead, the Vardos gate filled the main screen.

Something was coming through from the other side.

Not one ship.

Many.

Drive blooms appeared across the sensors.

Ten.

Twenty.

Thirty.

The sensor officer looked at Sera.

“Captain, they are broadcasting Kestrel military codes.”

Sera slowly turned toward Joel and Anton.

“You two knew this would happen.”

Joel looked at the growing formation.

Old destroyers. Armed transports. Salvage cruisers.

A fleet built from several rival factions.

“No,” Joel said.

Anton stepped closer to the tactical display.

“But this is becoming much more interesting.”

The lead ship transmitted a message.

Its image appeared on the bridge screen.

A scarred man sat inside an old military command chair.

“Unknown frigate,” he said. “Sector Command Kestrel belongs to the Vardos Coalition. Cut your engines and surrender the command key.”

Sera placed one hand on her chair.

“We have thirty ships ahead of us and one damaged frigate behind us.”

Anton looked at Joel.

Joel gave the smallest nod.

They would not reveal themselves.

Not yet.

Captain Sera opened the shipwide channel.

“All crew to battle stations.”

The bridge lights changed from red to deep blue.

Weapons came online.

The *Wayward Star* continued toward the gate.

## 2. Run the Gate

The hostile fleet spread across the gate approach.

Thirty ships.

Most were old military hulls rebuilt with civilian engines and salvaged weapons. Destroyers held the centre. Frigates moved along the sides. Armed transports waited behind them, ready to carry boarding teams.

The *Wayward Star* continued forward.

Captain Sera Venn stood behind her command chair.

“Time until weapons range?” she asked.

“Four minutes,” the sensor officer answered.

“Gate condition?”

“Stable enough for transit. The arrival envelope is wider than normal.”

The engineer looked up from her console.

“Our drive may survive one transit.”

“May?”

“It will survive.”

“That did not sound confident.”

“It was the best confidence I had.”

The Vardos commander returned on the main screen.

“Captain Venn, this is your final warning. Surrender the command key.”

Sera sat down.

“Remove him.”

The transmission vanished.

Her gunner flexed his metal hand over the controls.

“We cannot fight through thirty ships.”

“We do not need to fight through them,” Sera said. “We need to make them move.”

Joel stood near the rear bridge wall. Anton leaned beside him, looking relaxed.

Too relaxed.

Sera noticed.

“You have experience with gates?” she asked.

“A little,” Joel said.

Anton smiled.

“We have passed through several.”

“How many while being shot at?”

Joel glanced at his brother.

“Enough.”

Sera turned back toward the tactical display.

The hostile formation was built to stop them from reaching the gate. The Coalition ships held a wide wall across the approach, but their lines were uneven. Rival captains stayed close to their own allies. No one wanted to be the first ship damaged for another warlord’s claim.

Joel saw it immediately.

So did Sera.

“Pilot,” she said, “turn fifteen degrees toward their left screen.”

The *Wayward Star* changed course.

The enemy frigates reacted.

Three moved forward.

Two moved back.

A gap appeared between them.

“Missile doors opening,” the sensor officer warned.

“Point defence ready,” said the gunner.

Sera watched the range count fall.

“Hold.”

Enemy missiles launched.

Twenty white marks appeared on the tactical display.

Then thirty.

Then forty.

The bridge lights dimmed as the frigate redirected power.

“Defensive fire,” Sera ordered.

The *Wayward Star’s* remaining point-defence guns opened.

Light flashed across the forward screens. The first missiles died at long range. Others broke apart closer to the ship. Fragments struck the armour like heavy rain.

The ship shook.

“Forward plating hit.”

“No breach.”

“More missiles coming.”

Anton looked at Joel.

Joel touched the control hidden inside his sleeve.

A silent signal passed into the *Wayward Star’s* defensive network.

The ship’s targeting systems corrected by less than a degree.

No one on the bridge noticed.

The next point-defence burst was far more accurate.

Seven missiles vanished within two seconds.

The gunner frowned at his display.

“System calibration just improved.”

The engineer looked confused.

“I did nothing.”

“Do not complain,” Sera said.

The Coalition ships moved to close the gap.

That was what she wanted.

“Turn toward the centre,” she ordered. “Full burn.”

The pilot stared at her.

“The destroyers are there.”

“They are also too slow to move.”

The *Wayward Star* rolled hard and accelerated.

The engine screamed through the hull.

Crew members grabbed their stations. Joel and Anton shifted their weight without touching the wall.

Sera noticed that too.

The hostile destroyers opened fire.

Heavy shells crossed the dark.

One passed above the frigate.

Another struck the rear armour.

The impact threw everyone forward.

Alarms filled the bridge.

“Engine housing hit!”

“Drive remains active.”

“Port cooling line damaged.”

“Fire in compartment twelve.”

“Seal it.”

“Two crew trapped behind the bulkhead.”

“Can they breathe?”

“Yes.”

“Then they stay there until we clear the gate.”

The *Wayward Star* passed between two enemy formations.

Coalition frigates tried to turn with it, but they blocked one another. One captain fired too early. The rounds struck an allied ship’s armour.

Angry transmissions filled the open channel.

Anton laughed quietly.

“They do not like each other.”

“They like the station,” Joel said.

“That may not be enough.”

A Coalition destroyer turned across their path.

Its spinal gun began charging.

The weapon was old, but one direct hit would break the *Wayward Star* apart.

“Time to fire?” Sera asked.

“Eight seconds.”

“Gate?”

“Twenty-two seconds.”

“We cannot dodge both.”

Joel watched the destroyer rotate.

Its main weapon followed them.

He touched his sleeve again.

A tiny signal entered the destroyer’s damaged military identification system.

For half a second, the destroyer’s sensors identified the *Wayward Star* as an allied vessel.

Its spinal weapon stopped charging.

The Coalition commander shouted over the open channel.

“What are you doing? Fire!”

The destroyer corrected its systems.

But the delay was enough.

The gate filled the forward screen.

Its ancient structure awakened. White energy moved across the ring. Space inside the opening became dark and deep.

“Gate is active,” the sensor officer said.

The engineer struck the transit controls.

“Throat lock.”

The hostile fleet launched everything it had.

Missiles filled the scope.

Sera gripped her chair.

“All hands, prepare for transit!”

The first impacts hit the rear armour.

A weapon mount disappeared.

The frigate rolled.

The gate swallowed them.

For one moment, there was no sound.

Then the universe struck back.

The *Wayward Star* emerged spinning.

Stars moved across the main screen.

Loose equipment flew through the bridge. A console exploded beside the communications officer.

“Kill rotation!” Sera ordered.

“Thrusters responding.”

“Point defence to debris mode.”

“Collision warning!”

A dead cargo ship drifted directly ahead.

The pilot fired every side thruster at once.

The *Wayward Star* missed the wreck by less than two hundred metres.

Metal fragments struck the hull.

The ship rolled again, slower this time.

“Rally position?” Sera asked.

The sensor officer searched the local space.

“I have the station.”

The tactical display changed.

Sector Command Hub Kestrel appeared ahead.

It was larger than the hologram on Terminus Spire had suggested.

The station surrounded a broken asteroid almost forty kilometres wide. Defence towers rose from the rock. Docking arms extended into the dark. Huge military hangars lined the lower sections.

Most of the structure was without power.

But not all of it.

Weak lights moved around the command tower.

Sera stood slowly.

“Are those heat signatures?”

“Yes.”

“How many?”

“Hundreds.”

The gunner turned toward her.

“The station is occupied.”

A second alarm sounded.

Three Coalition frigates emerged from the gate behind them.

One arrived too close to another. Their armour collided. Both ships spun away with damaged engines.

The third remained under control.

It began firing.

“Hostile frigate behind us,” the sensor officer reported.

“Range?”

“Hammer Range. Closing.”

The *Wayward Star* had one working turret and fewer than twelve missiles.

Sera looked at the station.

“Can we reach the nearest docking arm?”

“If it still works.”

“Do it.”

The damaged frigate accelerated toward Kestrel.

Enemy fire crossed the darkness.

One shell struck near the engine section. The bridge lights went out.

Emergency lighting came on.

“Drive output falling,” the engineer called.

“Give me another minute.”

“I can give you forty seconds.”

Anton moved closer to Joel.

“We could end this.”

Joel watched Sera.

She did not panic.

She did not ask who would save her.

She studied the station and made a decision.

“Gunner,” she said, “target the docking structure above us.”

The gunner looked at her.

“That is not the enemy.”

“It will be.”

The *Wayward Star* passed beneath one of Kestrel’s dead defence towers.

Sera waited.

The Coalition frigate followed.

“Fire.”

The remaining turret struck the docking structure.

Metal supports broke.

A section of the ancient arm collapsed behind the *Wayward Star*.

The pursuing frigate tried to turn.

Too late.

Thousands of tonnes of metal struck its forward armour. The enemy ship spun away. Its weapons went dark.

The *Wayward Star* reached the nearest docking port.

Magnetic locks activated.

The entire ship shook as it connected with the station.

“Drive offline,” the engineer reported.

“Reactor stable.”

“Three injured.”

“No dead,” said the medical officer.

The bridge became quiet.

They had crossed the gate.

They had reached Kestrel.

They had lost one weapon mount, most of their rear armour and nearly the entire cooling reserve.

But they were alive.

Minimal losses.

Captain Sera looked at Joel and Anton.

“Your cabin survived.”

Anton nodded.

“Excellent service.”

Sera ignored him.

The station command key was still in her coat.

Beyond the airlock, something moved inside Kestrel.

Heavy footsteps echoed through the docking tunnel.

Then the old military speakers came alive.

A calm voice filled the frigate.

“Unauthorized vessel, remain sealed.”

Sera drew her pistol.

The lights outside the airlock switched on one by one.

Figures appeared behind the armoured glass.

They wore old sector military uniforms.

Their faces were hidden behind black helmets.

The voice continued.

“Sector Command Kestrel remains operational.”

Joel looked at Anton.

Anton’s smile returned.

The journey had not been the test.

The station was.

## 3. The Abandoned Garrison

The figures behind the armoured glass did not move.

Captain Sera Venn stood before the inner airlock with her pistol raised. Her gunner, Dax Corven, stood beside her with his metal hand around a compact rifle.

Joel and Anton waited behind them.

The old military speaker repeated its warning.

“Unauthorized vessel, remain sealed.”

Sera pressed the communication panel.

“This is Captain Sera Venn of the mercenary frigate *Wayward Star*. I hold the legal command key for Sector Command Hub Kestrel.”

Silence answered her.

Then one of the soldiers beyond the glass stepped forward.

He wore an old sector army uniform. The armour plates were scratched and repaired with civilian metal. His black helmet had once carried an officer’s crest, but someone had removed it.

“Display the key,” he ordered.

Sera held the metal command key against the airlock scanner.

The station accepted it.

A green line moved across the panel.

**COMMAND CLAIM VERIFIED.**

The soldiers reacted at once.

Several raised their rifles.

One turned toward the corridor behind them.

The officer removed his helmet.

He was an old man.

His hair was white. One eye had been replaced by a cheap mechanical lens. Scars covered the side of his face.

He stared at the words on the display.

“Who gave you that key?”

“A contract court on Terminus Spire.”

“Then the court sold you a grave.”

Sera kept her weapon raised.

“Open the door.”

The old officer looked at the damaged frigate behind her.

“You came alone?”

“Yes.”

“Coalition ships followed you.”

“They did.”

“Then you have brought them back.”

The airlock remained closed.

Dax moved closer to the glass.

“We can cut through.”

The old soldier gave him a tired look.

“If you damage that door, three residential sections lose pressure.”

Sera lowered her pistol slightly.

“Residential sections?”

The officer looked behind him.

For the first time, Sera noticed the movement beyond the soldiers.

A child stood at the end of the corridor.

The girl wore an oversized pressure coat. She held a small metal cup in both hands.

Another child pulled her away.

Sera looked back at the officer.

“How many civilians are on this station?”

The old man did not answer.

Joel stepped closer to the glass.

“More than the station can support,” he said.

The officer’s mechanical eye focused on him.

Joel pointed toward the ceiling.

“The lights are running at low power. The heat system is cycling by section. Your soldiers are wearing armour indoors because you cannot keep every corridor warm.”

Sera glanced at Joel.

The old officer studied him for several seconds.

“Who are you?”

“A passenger.”

Anton smiled.

“He notices things.”

The station shook.

A heavy impact passed through the docking arm.

The Coalition frigates outside had begun firing.

The old officer turned.

“Defence teams to outer sections. Seal Dock Ring Four.”

The soldiers moved immediately.

The officer looked back at Sera.

“If I open this door, you follow my orders until we reach command.”

“This is my station.”

“Not yet.”

Another impact struck.

Warning lights flashed along the corridor.

The old officer opened the airlock.

---

His name was Commander Halden Rook.

He had served at Kestrel before the Vardos government collapsed.

He walked quickly through the station, carrying his helmet under one arm. Sera, Dax, Joel and Anton followed him.

The corridors were crowded.

Not with soldiers.

With refugees.

Families lived inside old offices and equipment rooms. Blankets covered the walls. Water containers stood beneath leaking pipes. Emergency cables ran across the floor. Old ammunition crates had been turned into tables and beds.

People watched Sera pass.

Some looked hopeful.

Most looked afraid.

“How many?” Sera asked again.

“Four thousand eight hundred civilians,” Rook answered. “One hundred and twelve military personnel. Fewer every month.”

Dax stopped walking.

“You have nearly five thousand people here?”

“We had more.”

“What happened to them?”

“Hunger. Disease. Life-support failures.”

Rook continued.

Sera looked through an open doorway. A medical room had been built inside an old communications centre. Two elderly doctors worked beside military medics. Every bed was full.

“Why are they here?” she asked.

“Because the Coalition brought them.”

“The Coalition?”

Rook gave her a cold look.

“The Vardos Coalition did not begin as one fleet. It began as six local powers fighting over the sector after the government failed.”

They entered a large transit lift.

Rook entered an old military code.

The lift began moving toward the command tower.

“Each faction wanted Kestrel,” he continued. “The station controlled the defence network, the military docks and the relay routes. They fought through these corridors for three years.”

“Who won?” Anton asked.

“No one.”

Rook looked at the lift doors.

“They could enter the station. They could seize sections. They could not control the command core.”

“The station rejected them?” Joel asked.

“Yes. Old authentication rules. Every faction had part of the military authority, but none had enough.”

“So they made an agreement,” Sera said.

“They called it an agreement. It was a withdrawal.”

The lift stopped.

The doors opened onto another crowded level.

These refugees looked different. Many wore mining uniforms. Others carried old company badges or colonial identification tags.

Rook continued walking.

“The Coalition evacuated its own officers and valuable technicians. It removed portable weapons, medical supplies and working ships.”

He looked toward the civilians.

“Then it abandoned everyone else.”

A woman heard him.

She stepped into the corridor.

“My husband served them,” she said. “They promised to return.”

Rook did not look at her.

“They promised everyone.”

The woman stared at Sera.

“Are you Coalition?”

“No,” Sera answered.

“Imperial?”

“No.”

“Then why are you here?”

Sera touched the command key inside her coat.

“I was given the station.”

The woman laughed.

It was a bitter sound.

“They gave you something they could not take.”

She returned to her family.

Anton leaned closer to Joel.

“I dislike the Coalition.”

Joel watched two soldiers carry a broken air processor past them.

“I believe that was the intention.”

---

They reached the command tower.

The main operations chamber was enormous.

Rows of dead consoles surrounded a central tactical pit. Old fleet symbols remained on the walls. The main screen showed the dark space around Kestrel.

The *Wayward Star* remained locked to the outer docking arm.

Beyond it, Coalition ships were taking positions.

More vessels had emerged from the gate.

Seven frigates.

Two destroyers.

Three armed transports.

Rook approached the tactical display.

“They will demand the station again.”

“They already did,” Sera said.

“And now they know you reached it with a valid key.”

One of the old soldiers looked up from a console.

“Commander, incoming transmission.”

The scarred Coalition leader appeared on the main screen.

“Halden Rook,” he said. “You should have accepted our last offer.”

Rook stood straight.

“You offered transport for two hundred officers. You wanted us to leave the civilians behind.”

“They are not our responsibility.”

“They became your responsibility when you moved them into a war zone.”

The Coalition commander looked at Sera.

“Captain Venn, Rook is a mutineer occupying Coalition property.”

Sera stepped closer to the screen.

“The contract court recognized the station as abandoned military salvage.”

“The court had no authority.”

“You used its authority to transfer the claim when you could not pay your own debt.”

The commander’s face hardened.

“Give us the key. We will allow your ship to leave.”

Sera looked at the damaged *Wayward Star* on the tactical display.

Then she looked through the command room.

Old soldiers.

Refugees.

A station without food reserves, repair support or working defences.

A reward that had become a death sentence before she ever accepted it.

“What happens to the people on the station?” she asked.

“They will be processed.”

Rook’s mechanical eye narrowed.

Sera understood the word.

Prison camps.

Forced labour.

Hostages divided between Coalition factions.

Perhaps worse.

The Coalition commander leaned toward his camera.

“You are a mercenary, Captain. Do not die for people who cannot pay you.”

Behind Sera, Joel watched her carefully.

This was the real test.

Not the gate.

Not the damaged ship.

Not the station key.

A captain could be brave when fighting for a prize.

Joel wanted to know what she would do when the prize became a burden.

Sera turned to Commander Rook.

“Can the station weapons be activated?”

“Most of the grid is offline.”

“Most?”

“Three defence towers may still work. We cannot access them without full command authority.”

Sera removed the command key.

“Where is the command core?”

“Deep inside the asteroid.”

“Can we reach it?”

Rook looked toward a sealed blast door at the rear of the chamber.

“The lower station is not secure.”

“What is down there?”

“Old automated defences. Damaged maintenance machines. Sections we sealed years ago.”

Anton’s eyes brightened.

Sera noticed.

“You find that exciting?”

“A little.”

Joel stepped toward the tactical display.

“How long before the Coalition boards?”

The sensor officer checked the enemy formation.

“Twenty minutes.”

Joel nodded.

“Enough time.”

Sera looked at him.

“For what?”

“To decide whether your reward is a station or a responsibility.”

Silence spread through the command chamber.

Outside, the Coalition transports began moving toward Kestrel.

Sera looked at the refugees displayed on the internal station network.

She closed her hand around the command key.

“Commander Rook, prepare your soldiers to defend the docking levels.”

Rook studied her.

“And you?”

“I am going to the command core.”

She looked at Dax.

“Bring a security team.”

Then she looked at Joel and Anton.

“You two wanted adventure.”

Anton smiled.

“We did.”

Sera pointed toward the sealed blast door.

“Now you have it.”

## 4. The Captain’s Contract

The Coalition transports moved closer to Kestrel.

Three carried boarding troops. Two armed frigates followed behind them, ready to fire into the docking rings if the defenders resisted.

The enemy destroyers remained near the gate.

They were not coming to negotiate.

Captain Sera Venn stood inside Kestrel’s command chamber, the station key held tightly in one hand.

Around her waited the survivors of two broken forces.

Her own mercenary crew had lost people reaching the station. Commander Halden Rook’s old soldiers had spent seven years defending refugees no government wanted.

The tactical display counted down.

“Eighteen minutes before the first transport reaches Dock Ring Two,” Rook said.

Sera looked across the dark command consoles.

“Can your people hold them?”

“For a while.”

“How long?”

“If they attack one dock, perhaps an hour.”

“And if they attack all three?”

Rook glanced at the approaching transports.

“Not long enough.”

Dax Corven placed his rifle on the command table.

“We should go below and activate the command core.”

Joel stood near the rear wall.

“Not yet.”

Every face turned toward him.

Sera’s eyes narrowed.

“You said we had enough time.”

“We do,” Joel answered. “But there is a decision you must make first.”

“I already made it. We defend the station.”

Anton pushed away from the wall.

“No, you have not.”

His voice carried across the chamber.

Dax turned toward him.

Anton walked directly toward Sera.

“You have a key,” he said. “You have a legal claim. You have a damaged ship and almost five thousand mouths waiting for food.”

Sera did not move.

“What is your point?”

“My point is that you keep saying we defend the station.”

Anton pointed toward the internal display. Thousands of civilian identification marks filled the habitation levels.

“What about them?”

Sera stared at him.

“They are inside the station.”

“That was not the question.”

Dax stepped between them.

“You should stop talking.”

Anton looked at the metal hand resting near Dax’s pistol.

“Why?”

“Because you are speaking to my captain.”

“I noticed.”

“You are a passenger.”

“I paid for the privilege.”

Dax moved closer.

“You paid for transport. Not the right to question her command.”

Anton smiled.

“That sounds expensive. How much does the right cost?”

Dax grabbed the front of Anton’s coat with his metal hand.

The sound echoed through the chamber.

Three members of Sera’s crew immediately reached for their weapons.

Rook’s old soldiers reacted as well. Rifles shifted. Boots moved across the deck.

Joel remained against the wall.

He looked almost bored.

Anton looked down at Dax’s hand.

“Careful,” he said. “That coat is borrowed.”

Dax tightened his grip.

“You think this is amusing?”

“No. I think your captain is avoiding an answer.”

“She owes you nothing.”

“She is about to become responsible for five thousand people. She owes them an answer.”

Dax pulled Anton closer.

Anton did not resist.

He did not reach for a weapon.

He only looked past Dax toward Sera.

“Say it clearly, Captain.”

Sera’s voice became cold.

“Dax. Release him.”

Dax did not move.

“Captain—”

“Now.”

His metal fingers opened.

Anton straightened his coat.

Two crew members continued watching him with open anger.

One, a young marine with a bandage around her head, stepped closer.

“We lost friends getting here,” she said. “You stood on our bridge and made jokes.”

Anton looked at her.

“Yes.”

“You think you could have done better?”

“Yes.”

Her hand closed around her rifle.

Dax raised one arm, stopping her.

Anton’s smile disappeared.

“But your captain did well,” he continued. “That is why we are still here.”

The marine did not relax.

Joel finally left the wall.

He walked to Anton’s side.

“You are being rude again.”

“I am being direct.”

“You can be direct without encouraging a firefight.”

“They were not going to shoot me.”

Dax stared at him.

“You are very confident.”

Anton looked at the weapons surrounding him.

“I have reasons.”

Joel sighed.

“This is why I refused your first wager.”

Sera looked between them.

“What wager?”

Anton answered before Joel could stop him.

“We disagreed about you.”

The room became even quieter.

Dax’s face hardened.

“You placed bets on the captain?”

“Only small ones.”

Joel folded his arms.

“He predicted you would claim the station, lock the food stores and demand service in exchange for protection.”

Anton looked offended.

“That is not exactly what I said.”

“It is almost exactly what you said.”

“I said she might do that.”

“You gave it a sixty-percent probability.”

“Seventy.”

“That does not improve your position.”

Sera stepped toward them.

“And what did you predict?”

Joel met her eyes.

“That you would protect the people.”

Anton nodded.

“He also said you would take too long to admit it.”

Joel glanced at him.

“That was not part of the bet.”

“It should have been.”

Dax struck the command table with his metal fist.

“Enough.”

The impact shook the tactical display.

“You walk onto our ship under false names. You interfere with our systems. You question the captain in front of strangers. Now you tell us this journey is some game between you?”

Anton’s gaze moved toward him.

“No.”

For once, his answer carried no humour.

“The Coalition outside thinks this is a game. They abandoned these people when the station refused their command codes. Now they have returned because someone else may gain what they could not control.”

He looked at Sera.

“You were given a fortress because your employer believed it would kill you.”

The approaching transports crossed another range marker.

Sixteen minutes.

Anton pointed toward them.

“They expect you to surrender the key, save your crew and leave the refugees behind.”

His voice hardened.

“Will you?”

Sera stepped close enough that only a metre remained between them.

“You push too far.”

“Probably.”

“My crew nearly fought you.”

“They are loyal.”

“They could have killed you.”

Anton looked toward Dax.

“No.”

Dax reached for him again.

Sera raised one hand.

“Stop.”

Nobody moved.

The tension remained inside the chamber like pressure behind a sealed door.

Sera looked at the tactical display.

The station could be worth a fortune.

Its docks could repair fleets. Its defence towers could control the gate. Its military archives might contain route keys, weapons plans and old sector intelligence.

But its corridors were filled with refugees.

Children slept inside abandoned offices.

Families drank recycled water from damaged pipes.

Old soldiers wore armour indoors because there was not enough heat.

Joel watched Sera.

Anton waited.

The Coalition commander appeared on the main screen without permission.

“Captain Venn, your time is ending.”

Sera did not turn toward him.

“Remove the transmission.”

The screen went dark.

Anton leaned toward Joel.

“I knew she would do that.”

“We did not bet on it.”

“We should begin.”

“No.”

Sera heard them.

“Both of you, be silent.”

Anton obeyed.

For almost three seconds.

Then he said, “You still have not answered.”

Dax took one step forward.

Sera turned on Anton.

“You want confirmation?”

“Yes.”

“You want me to say it clearly?”

“Yes.”

“Then listen.”

Sera faced the communications officer.

“Open every station channel.”

The officer hesitated.

“Captain?”

“Do it.”

A tone passed through Kestrel.

Speakers came alive across the station.

In the habitation sections, refugees stopped beside heating units and ration lines.

In the medical centre, doctors looked up from wounded patients.

At the docking rings, old soldiers waited behind barricades built from cargo containers and armour plates.

Sera placed both hands on the command table.

“This is Captain Sera Venn of the mercenary frigate *Wayward Star*.”

Her voice travelled through the forgotten fortress.

“I came here because I was given a salvage claim. That claim includes the station, its docks, its military systems and everything legally abandoned inside it.”

She looked directly at Anton.

“It does not include the people.”

Anton nodded once.

“The Coalition left you here,” Sera continued. “It removed the ships, weapons, supplies and officers it considered valuable. Now it has returned because I carry a command key.”

The tactical display counted down.

Fourteen minutes.

“I have been asked whether I intend to protect this station or the people living inside it.”

The refugees listened.

Rook stood straight beside the table.

Dax watched Sera, his anger slowly giving way to understanding.

“The answer is the people.”

Anton glanced at Joel.

Joel held out one hand without looking at him.

Anton quietly placed a credit token in it.

Sera continued.

“I will not give you to the Coalition. I will not trade you for safe passage. I will not use you as shields while my crew escapes.”

The young marine who had nearly challenged Anton lowered her rifle.

“My ship is damaged,” Sera said. “My crew is small. The station defences are offline. I cannot promise victory.”

Her voice remained steady.

“But I can offer a contract.”

Rook looked toward her.

Sera lifted the command key.

“Every person aboard Kestrel is offered protection under my command.”

The communications officer turned in her chair.

“Captain, they cannot pay us.”

“They already have.”

“With what?”

Sera looked toward Rook and the old soldiers.

“With seven years of holding this station while everyone else ran.”

Silence followed.

Then Sera began stating the terms.

“No civilian will be transferred, sold, forced into service or surrendered without consent.”

Joel’s expression changed slightly.

“Medical aid comes before salvage.”

Anton looked toward him.

“Food is rationed according to need, not rank.”

Joel opened his hand.

Anton placed another credit token into it.

“Any person may leave when safe transport becomes available.”

Rook slowly removed his helmet.

“If we survive,” Sera continued, “Kestrel will not be my private fortress. It will become a protected settlement and an independent mercenary base.”

She placed the command key against the central console.

“Those who choose to serve will be paid when the station has income. Those who do not serve remain protected residents.”

Anton whispered, “That was better than your prediction.”

Joel replied quietly, “It was better than yours.”

“My prediction was intentionally pessimistic.”

“You are losing badly.”

“I am gathering information.”

Sera looked toward the station cameras.

“If you accept this contract, confirm through your identification terminal.”

Nothing happened.

One second passed.

Then another.

The first green signal appeared inside the medical section.

A nurse.

A second appeared in the engineering levels.

Then ten more.

Fifty.

Hundreds.

The habitation levels turned green.

Old soldiers accepted.

Dock workers accepted.

Miners accepted.

Refugees pressed identification bands against damaged terminals.

Parents lifted children so they could reach the controls.

The marks spread across the display until most of the station glowed.

Four thousand six hundred and twenty-three people accepted.

One hundred and seventeen declined or could not respond.

Sera studied the numbers.

“Mark the non-signatories as neutral civilians.”

The communications officer looked surprised.

“They declined the contract.”

“They still receive protection.”

Anton slowly turned toward Joel.

Joel held out his hand again.

Anton placed a third credit token in it.

“This wager has become unreasonable.”

“You designed it.”

“I did not expect her to exceed the conditions.”

“That is why you lost.”

Commander Rook stepped before Sera.

He removed the faded officer insignia from his uniform and placed it on the command table.

“Halden Rook. Former commander, Vardos Sector Army.”

He stood at attention.

“I accept your contract.”

He looked toward his soldiers.

“Anyone who does not accept may leave their weapon and join the protected civilians.”

No one moved.

Rook put his helmet back on.

“My soldiers will hold the docking levels.”

Sera nodded.

“My crew will support them.”

Dax lifted his rifle and looked toward Anton.

“You almost started a fight on my captain’s command deck.”

Anton adjusted his coat.

“I wanted a clear answer.”

“You could have asked politely.”

“I did.”

Joel looked at him.

“No, you did not.”

Anton considered this.

“Then I could have asked politely.”

Dax pointed one metal finger toward him.

“When this is over, we are going to discuss your behaviour.”

Anton smiled again.

“That sounds almost like a threat.”

“It is a promise.”

“Better.”

The station alarm sounded.

“Boarding pods launching,” the sensor officer called.

Dozens of contacts separated from the Coalition transports.

They spread toward Dock Rings Two, Four and Six.

The station’s point-defence weapons remained silent.

Kestrel had no active shields.

No working defence grid.

No friendly fleet.

Only a damaged frigate, old soldiers and thousands of people who had just chosen to stand together.

Sera drew her pistol.

“Rook, take command of the docking defence.”

Rook checked his rifle.

“Understood.”

“Dax, take half our crew and reinforce him.”

Dax looked toward the tactical display.

“And the other half?”

“They come with me to the command core.”

The sealed blast door at the rear of the chamber began to open.

Cold air moved from the darkness beyond it.

Emergency lights activated one after another, leading down into the asteroid.

Joel walked toward the passage.

Anton followed.

Sera stopped him with one hand against his chest.

Anton looked down at it.

“You wanted confirmation,” she said.

“I received it.”

“If you question my command like that again, I will let Dax throw you through an airlock.”

Anton looked toward the gunner.

Dax smiled without warmth.

Anton nodded.

“Clear terms. I approve.”

Sera removed her hand.

Joel entered the passage.

Anton followed beside him.

“How much do I owe you?” Anton asked.

“Thirty-five credits.”

“That cannot be correct.”

“You bet against the neutral-civilian protection.”

“I did not bet against it. I failed to predict it.”

“That is the definition of losing.”

Behind them, the first Coalition boarding pod struck Kestrel’s hull.

The station shook.

Sera entered the dark passage, command key in one hand and pistol in the other.

Across every occupied level, the new contract remained displayed on the walls.

**PROTECTION ACCEPTED.**

**COMMAND RESPONSIBILITY ACTIVE.**

**DEFEND THE STATION.**

## 5. The Winning Bet

The blast door sealed behind them.

The sound of the battle above faded into a distant vibration through the stone.

Joel walked first.

Anton followed beside him.

Captain Sera Venn came behind with six members of her crew. Rifles remained raised. Emergency lights ran along the passage in weak red lines, illuminating old military warnings stamped into the walls.

**COMMAND AUTHORITY ONLY.**

**AUTOMATED DEFENCE ZONE.**

**LETHAL RESPONSE ACTIVE.**

They descended deeper into Kestrel.

For almost a minute nobody spoke.

Then Anton began laughing.

At first it was a low sound.

Then it grew.

He stopped walking, leaned one hand against the wall and laughed harder.

Not polite laughter.

Not nervous laughter.

The sound of someone who had just watched something dangerous happen exactly the way he hoped it would.

Joel turned toward him.

Anton pointed back toward the sealed door.

“She threatened to throw me through an airlock.”

Joel stared at him.

Then Joel started laughing too.

Sera stopped.

Her crew stopped with her.

The two men laughed like madmen in the middle of an abandoned military fortress while enemy boarding troops were cutting into the station above them.

Anton bent forward, almost unable to breathe.

“And Dax—”

He struggled to speak.

“Dax actually grabbed me.”

Joel shook his head.

“You deserved that.”

“I wanted confirmation.”

“You nearly caused a firefight.”

“It worked.”

That only made Joel laugh harder.

Sera’s engineer looked at her captain.

“Are they insane?”

Sera kept watching them.

“I am beginning to consider it.”

Anton finally straightened.

His smile remained wide.

“She did it.”

Joel wiped one eye.

“She did.”

“She protected the ones who refused the contract.”

“She did.”

“She gave away control over part of her own reward.”

“Yes.”

Anton pointed at Sera.

“I told you.”

Joel immediately stopped laughing.

“No.”

Anton’s smile weakened.

“I was close.”

“You said she would demand service first.”

“I said she might.”

“You said seventy percent.”

“I was estimating.”

“You were wrong.”

Sera stared between them.

“You were betting on me.”

Anton looked at her.

“Yes.”

Joel answered at the same time.

“Unfortunately.”

The crew behind Sera shifted.

One marine raised her rifle slightly.

Anton noticed.

His smile widened again.

“You really are loyal.”

“Anton,” Joel said.

“What?”

“Stop testing them.”

“I am complimenting them.”

“That is not what it looks like.”

The marine stared at him.

“You think this is funny?”

Anton’s expression changed.

The laughter vanished.

“No.”

The answer was calm.

Heavy.

For the first time, the man who had joked his way across the station sounded like someone used to giving orders in rooms where thousands of lives depended on them.

“You followed your captain through a hostile gate in a damaged frigate. You stayed when the armour came apart. You accepted five thousand refugees you cannot feed.”

His eyes moved across Sera’s crew.

“And when a stranger challenged her authority, you were ready to kill him.”

Anton nodded.

“That is good.”

Joel stood beside him.

His manner had changed as well.

The relaxed passenger was fading.

“We have seen crews with newer ships, richer contracts and better weapons break under less pressure.”

Sera studied him.

“You talk like military officers.”

Anton glanced toward Joel.

Joel glanced back.

Neither answered.

Then Anton held out his hand.

“My prize.”

Joel sighed.

“You lost three other bets.”

“I won this one.”

“You changed the terms twice.”

“I refined the conditions.”

“You cheated.”

“Strategic interpretation.”

Joel slowly reached inside his coat.

Sera’s crew reacted instantly.

Six weapons came up.

“Hands!” the marine shouted.

Joel stopped.

Anton looked around at the rifles pointed toward them.

Then he began laughing again.

Joel looked at him.

That was enough.

Joel started laughing too.

Sera’s crew stared at the two men as if they had completely lost their minds.

“You have six rifles pointed at you,” Sera said.

Anton looked at the nearest muzzle.

“Yes.”

“And that is funny?”

“A little.”

“Why?”

Anton’s eyes slowly turned crimson.

Not reflected emergency light.

The colour came from inside them.

Deep red spread across his irises until they looked like burning glass.

The marine took a step back.

Joel’s eyes changed next.

Crimson.

The family trait appeared stronger when they were excited.

Or angry.

Or enjoying themselves too much.

Sera raised her pistol.

“What are you?”

Joel reached into his coat very slowly.

“Getting Anton his winnings.”

His hand emerged holding a bottle.

Old black glass.

A faded metal seal wrapped around the neck.

Anton’s crimson eyes widened.

“You actually brought it.”

Joel handed it to him.

Anton took the bottle almost reverently.

Sera looked at it.

“What is that?”

“Liquor,” Joel said.

“That looks older than this station.”

“It probably is.”

Anton examined the seal.

“The western reserve?”

“The last bottle.”

“You said you were saving it.”

Joel shrugged.

“You won.”

Anton smiled.

For several seconds he looked almost normal again.

Then the station shook.

A distant explosion rolled through the rock.

Dust fell from the ceiling.

The moment ended.

Anton carefully put the bottle inside his coat.

“When this is over, we open it.”

“One glass,” Joel said.

“Each.”

“One.”

“Each.”

“We will discuss it.”

Sera lowered her pistol slightly.

“You two are celebrating while Coalition troops board my station.”

Anton looked toward the ceiling.

“Dock Ring Two first.”

Sera frowned.

“How do you know?”

“Because their commander wants pressure, not destruction.”

Joel activated the old station map on the wall.

His voice changed again.

Clean.

Cold.

Commanding.

“He will attack Ring Two hard. Ring Four will receive a secondary force.”

Joel pointed toward another section.

“Ring Six is noise.”

Sera’s engineer stepped closer.

“You cannot know that.”

Anton shook his head.

“Ring Six gives them nothing. It is too far from command.”

Joel moved his finger across the map.

“The real attack will come through Maintenance Junction Twelve.”

“That section is sealed.”

“So they will cut through it.”

Sera stared at the map.

“You are very certain.”

Joel looked at her.

“Because that is what I would do.”

Anton nodded.

“And I would hit the reactor access first.”

Joel looked at him.

“That is why you are not planning this attack.”

“It would work.”

“It would damage the station.”

“The enemy does not care.”

“The commander does. He wants the station intact.”

Anton considered it.

“Fair.”

Sera watched them.

They were no longer talking like travellers.

They were talking like fleet commanders.

Senior ones.

Experienced ones.

Joel opened his communicator.

Then stopped himself.

He looked toward Sera.

“Captain. Recommend you move ten defenders from Ring Six to Junction Twelve.”

Recommend.

But it sounded exactly like an order.

Sera noticed.

So did her crew.

“You are giving me tactical instructions on my station.”

Joel inclined his head slightly.

“Yes.”

Anton smiled.

“He almost said ‘order.’”

Joel ignored him.

Sera opened her channel.

“Rook. Move ten soldiers from Ring Six to Maintenance Junction Twelve.”

Static.

Then Rook answered.

“Junction Twelve is sealed.”

“Assume it will not stay sealed.”

A pause.

“Understood.”

Anton nodded.

“Good captain.”

Sera slowly turned toward him.

“Do not start again.”

“I was approving.”

The engineer looked toward Joel’s coat.

“You have anything else hidden in there?”

Anton grinned.

“Oh, yes.”

Joel closed his eyes.

“Anton.”

“What?”

“You were asked.”

Anton opened his coat.

Sera’s crew froze.

The weapon beneath it was nothing like the cheap pistols carried through mercenary stations.

It was large.

Black.

Heavy enough that most men would have worn it in a thigh rig.

The pistol’s barrel was thick and angular, with reinforced cooling channels running down both sides. Strange metal markings covered the frame. The grip was clearly custom made.

There was no manufacturer’s mark.

No serial number.

No civilian safety lock.

The weapon looked less like a pistol and more like someone had cut down a starship cannon until a very strong man could carry it.

The marine stared.

“What in hell is that?”

Anton drew it.

Half the crew aimed at him again.

Anton held the weapon away from them.

“This?”

He inspected it.

“Old.”

Joel looked at him.

“Put it away.”

“You carry something worse.”

Sera’s eyes moved toward Joel.

Joel sighed.

Anton smiled broadly.

“Show them.”

“No.”

“You know they are going to find out.”

Joel stared at his brother.

Anton lifted the black pistol.

“I showed mine.”

“That sentence explains most of your problems.”

Despite herself, Sera almost smiled.

Joel opened his coat.

The smile disappeared.

A long-barrelled handgun rested beneath his left arm.

It was slimmer than Anton’s weapon.

That somehow made it worse.

The barrel extended almost to his waist. The upper receiver was made from dark metal with thin silver lines cut through it. The grip carried a worn crest that Joel had covered with a strip of black cloth.

Sera could still see part of it.

A crown.

A blade.

Something heraldic.

Something old.

Joel drew the weapon.

The passage seemed smaller with it in his hand.

The engineer stared at the power indicator.

“That charge level cannot be correct.”

“It is.”

“That weapon would break a normal person’s wrist.”

Anton nodded.

“Yes.”

Sera looked from the pistol to Joel.

“Trader?”

Joel looked at her.

“Occasionally.”

Anton laughed again.

This time Joel joined him immediately.

Both men stood beneath the red emergency lights, crimson eyes burning, holding weapons that belonged on armoury inspection tables rather than hidden beneath civilian coats.

Sera’s crew did not laugh.

One marine whispered:

“Captain…”

“I see them.”

Anton finally controlled himself.

He holstered the weapon.

Joel did the same.

“There,” Joel said. “Everyone feels better.”

“No,” Sera replied.

“Reasonable.”

Her communicator erupted.

Gunfire filled the channel.

Rook’s voice came through.

“Captain! Ring Two breached!”

A second voice shouted behind him.

“Movement at Junction Twelve!”

Sera looked at Joel.

Joel’s crimson eyes sharpened.

“How many?”

Rook answered through static.

“Unknown! Cutting charges just opened the maintenance wall!”

Anton slowly looked toward Joel.

Joel gave him a small nod.

“Reserve force.”

“Early.”

“By perhaps two minutes.”

Anton smiled.

“Competent enemy.”

Sera stared at them.

“You predicted that.”

Joel looked toward the lower station.

“Yes.”

Before Sera could ask anything else, a deep metallic impact sounded ahead.

Everyone turned.

Something moved beyond the sealed command-core door.

Metal against metal.

Then again.

The emergency lights changed.

Red became darker.

The station voice came through hidden speakers.

“Command identity unverified.”

Panels opened along both walls.

Weapon mounts unfolded.

Sera’s crew rushed for cover.

Joel and Anton remained standing in the centre of the passage.

Anton drew the black pistol again.

His crimson eyes almost glowed.

He looked at Joel.

“Five credits.”

“No.”

“I take the first three.”

“No wager.”

“Four?”

Joel drew his own weapon.

A targeting mechanism unfolded along the long barrel with a quiet mechanical click.

“Anton.”

“Yes?”

“Try not to destroy the station.”

Anton’s grin returned.

“No promises.”

The automated guns turned toward them.

For one second there was silence.

Then both brothers began laughing again.

Mad laughter echoed through the buried military passage as the ancient defence system opened fire.

## 6. Two Battles at Once

## Dock Ring Two

The first Coalition heavy soldier came through the broken airlock in a cloud of smoke.

Commander Halden Rook fired immediately.

His rifle struck the attacker in the chest.

The armour held.

“Heavy boarding armour!”

Dax Corven stepped from behind the barricade.

His metal hand locked around the forward grip of his rifle.

Three controlled bursts struck the same damaged plate.

The Coalition soldier fell backward.

Another stepped over him.

Then another.

The corridor filled with gunfire.

Old Vardos soldiers fought from positions they had built years before. Sera’s mercenaries held the centre. Refugee engineers moved ammunition, sealed pressure doors and dragged wounded defenders away from the line.

“Breach charge!”

Rook ducked.

The side wall exploded.

Metal fragments tore across the corridor.

A soldier beside him fell.

Dax grabbed the man and dragged him behind cover.

Through the new breach came more Coalition troops.

“Side passage!”

Rook checked his wrist display.

Then froze.

Maintenance Junction Twelve flashed red.

Ten defenders were already there.

Exactly where Joel had told them to be.

The Coalition reserve company cut through the wall expecting an empty maintenance corridor.

Instead, rifles opened from both sides.

The first boarding squad collapsed.

Rook listened to the report.

Then looked at Dax.

“The passengers knew.”

Dax replaced his magazine.

“They are not passengers.”

## The Lower Station

The automated defence system opened fire.

Sera threw herself behind a support pillar.

Rounds tore chunks from the wall.

“Cover!”

Her crew fired back.

Their shots sparked uselessly against military armour.

Anton stepped into the centre of the corridor.

“Anton!” Sera shouted.

He fired.

The black handgun produced a crack that Sera felt through her chest.

The first defence turret disappeared.

Not damaged.

Gone.

Its mounting tore out of the wall and slammed into the opposite bulkhead.

Everyone stopped firing for half a second.

Anton looked at his weapon.

“That one was fragile.”

Joel stepped beside him.

Three turrets rotated toward them.

Joel fired once.

The projectile crossed the passage faster than Sera could follow.

It punched through the first turret.

Then the wall behind it.

Then something inside the second mounting.

Both weapons died.

Anton stared.

“That counts as two.”

“One shot.”

“Two targets.”

“One shot.”

“You are impossible.”

The third turret fired.

Joel moved.

Not fast.

Too fast.

One moment he was standing beside Anton.

The next he was three metres away behind a structural beam.

Sera stared.

Anton fired twice.

The remaining turret collapsed.

He looked toward Joel.

“Three.”

“You destroyed two.”

“The first one counts double because it was dramatic.”

Sera shouted over the weapons fire.

“Would both of you stop betting!”

They looked at her.

Together:

“No.”

Then they started laughing again.

The sound echoed down the passage.

The ancient station voice spoke.

“Escalating defence response.”

A heavy door opened ahead.

Something large stepped through.

Three metres tall.

Military armour.

Rotary cannon.

Old sector insignia still visible beneath layers of dust.

Sera’s engineer went pale.

“Guardian frame.”

Anton’s smile widened.

Joel looked almost pleased.

Sera stared at both of them.

“You cannot possibly find that funny.”

The guardian frame raised its cannon.

Joel’s crimson eyes burned.

Anton’s pistol rose.

Then both men started laughing like lunatics again.

Sera’s crew slowly backed farther into cover.

For the first time since meeting them, none of Sera’s people worried about protecting Joel and Anton.

They were beginning to worry about whatever had just walked into the corridor.

## 7. The Truth Behind the Passengers

Sera Venn threw herself behind a support pillar.

“DOWN!”

The cannon fired.

Rounds ripped across the corridor.

Ancient armour exploded from the walls. Emergency lights shattered. The passage filled with sparks and fragments.

Joel and Anton remained standing.

Anton looked at the guardian.

Then at Joel.

“Still think this is not worth five credits?”

Joel brought his long-barrelled pistol to bear.

“You are impossible.”

The guardian tracked them across the open corridor. From behind cover, Sera shouted, “Move!”

Anton raised his weapon.

“Watch this.”

The guardian fired.

Anton moved sideways.

Too fast.

The rounds struck empty deck.

His pistol fired once.

The sound hit the corridor like a hammer.

The guardian’s left knee disappeared.

The machine dropped.

Joel fired immediately afterward.

His shot entered beneath the guardian’s shoulder armour.

The entire weapon arm stopped moving.

Sera’s engineer stared at her scanner.

“You hit the control junction.”

Joel looked toward the crippled machine.

“Yes.”

“You could not see it.”

“I knew where it should be.”

Anton fired again.

The guardian’s second leg collapsed.

It crashed to the floor.

Still alive.

Still dangerous.

The machine tried to raise its remaining weapon.

Joel walked toward it.

Sera rose from cover.

“Stop!”

Joel stopped.

Sera’s pistol pointed directly at him.

Her crew aimed at Anton.

Nobody laughed now.

Sera looked from one brother to the other.

“Enough.”

Anton tilted his head.

“Captain?”

“No more trader stories. No more passenger stories. No more jokes.”

She pointed at Anton’s weapon.

“That is not civilian equipment.”

Then at Joel.

“You move like military command. You predict enemy operations before my own tactical officers. You carry weapons my engineer cannot identify.”

Her eyes settled on their crimson eyes.

“And whatever that is, it is not normal.”

Anton looked at Joel.

“She finally asked.”

Joel sighed.

“You owe me another five.”

“I thought she would ask earlier.”

“You said after the first turret.”

“I was close.”

Sera stepped forward.

“If either of you makes another bet while I am pointing a weapon at you, Dax will not need to throw anyone through an airlock.”

Anton considered this.

“Fair.”

Joel slowly holstered his pistol.

Anton did the same.

Sera did not lower hers.

“Names.”

## 12. Take the Station

Her battered crew surrounded them.

She looked through the observation window.

House ships filled the space around Kestrel.

Sanctuary tenders repaired its docks.

Coalition ships waited peacefully under surrender protocols.

For the first time, the station did not feel abandoned.

Joel lifted his glass.

Not Winter Reserve this time.

Something safer.

“Kestrel.”

Sera lifted hers.

“My station.”

Joel smiled.

“Your station.”

Anton raised his cup.

“Our newest captain.”

Sera looked at him.

“Careful.”

Anton laughed.

Then the entire table joined him.

The laughter spread.

Mercenaries.

Old soldiers.

House Guards.

Refugees.

A captain who had gone to Terminus Spire expecting payment and instead received a broken fortress, five thousand people, a war and a new family she had never asked for.

Kestrel’s damaged lights burned above them.

The feast continued long into the station night.

And for once, no alarm interrupted it.
